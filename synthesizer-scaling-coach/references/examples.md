# Calibration examples from backbone reviews (anonymized)

Use these to calibrate your feedback. They are the program's reviewer advice for other members' situations. Do not paste them at the user. Pull the matching pattern when the user's situation looks similar, and say it is a pattern from another member's review.

## 1. Large social following, many small offers, unclear funnel (language tutor)
Situation: hundreds of thousands of followers, mostly unmonetized. Small offers for several audiences (children, adults, corporate). A past low-ticket video course drew 500 webinar attendees but only 12 sales because it targeted total beginners rather than the core advanced audience. Organic reach per post about 3 percent.
Advice: focus the offer on the existing audience. Narrow to one avatar (mid-to-senior professionals who feel overlooked despite strong skills). Sell the promotion, salary increase, status and recognition that result, not "feel comfortable". Price in the $4,000 to $5,000 range, consistent with similar offers. Content strategy is not the priority for the next 30 days. Validate one core offer and make the first premium sales. No paid ads until about 30K per month organically. Treat corporate deals separately, customize the backbone per company, price on total transformation value. Simplify deliverables.

## 2. Local in-person business moving online (sports trainer)
Situation: in-person group training at a monthly fee with long client lifetimes, a small online membership nobody engaged with, a previously sold 8-week program with unclear pricing.
Advice: one clear 8-week core program, raise the price (about $2K), upsell continuity after (about $500 per month). Shift the avatar to the older athlete preparing for college recruiting (highest intent and spend). Sell the scholarship outcome, not skill development. Proof was exceptional (graduates earned large scholarships), so a high price and an outcome-based guarantee were suggested. No one-to-one in the online offer so it stays scalable. Leave in-person pricing as is, and consider doubling it later.

## 3. Course creator changing a cohort structure (dog training coach)
Situation: a 2-month cohort at $1,950 with 2 calls per week, planned 12-month access as a safety net.
Advice: change to 4 months with 2 calls per month to fit the transformation timeline and the coach's schedule. Remove the 12-month access promise (it signals low confidence, undermines continuity upsell, and turns the program into a year-long commitment). Set the cohort start about 30 days out and keep selling. Low-ticket products can stay as a downsell and reactivation path. For a request for one-to-one outside the cohort, set a minimum price and hold it.

## 4. Personal brand agency owner choosing between done-for-you and education
Situation: LinkedIn presence, a few done-for-you clients at monthly retainers, unsure of the ideal customer.
Advice: done-for-you is faster to cash but hard to scale profitably. Education or consulting is the better long-term flywheel. If financial stability is needed, lock in two or three more done-for-you clients first, then transition. Uncertainty about the ICP is itself a signal to keep serving clients until patterns emerge. Niche and outcome tend to remain consistent whether delivery is done-for-you or done-with-you. Do not force a premature niche, use the accordion. Make aggressive offers to the network for 30 days, then report whether the constraint is delivery or lead flow. Hybrid positioning (advisor plus done-for-you on high-leverage tasks, client or VA executes the rest) was the right direction. Do not personally do low-leverage tasks like appointment setting. Frame price around ROI. Let prospects compute ROI in discovery. A first client grew from about $1K to $5K per month as results proved out.

## 5. Template seller with YouTube income (digital product creator)
Situation: roughly 600 templates sold over two years, minimal done-for-you history, tentative niche ("digital product business owners").
Advice: do not let niche indecision block action. The niche should emerge from analyzing who buys. Contact past buyers to learn who they are and why they bought, and start capturing phone numbers at purchase. Outcome pillars: know your numbers, make data-driven decisions, save time on manual tracking, scale. Start around $2K one-to-one and raise after five clients. Differentiator: incorporate AI into the spreadsheet workflow. The main competitor is a general AI assistant, so the counter is that AI makes mistakes, over-complicates, and cannot tell you what to build. Problem framing needs layers (disorganized, then do not know numbers, then cannot prioritize, then waste focus, then not earning enough). Reach out to members of existing communities. Run a discovery call with a prospective client as practice.

## 6. Sports coach with a growing free group (boxing coach)
Situation: free community with a decent visit-to-signup rate, plan for a low monthly price, three possible segments.
Advice: the math on low ticket needs thousands of signups per month to reach a $30K goal, versus about 20 clients per month at $1,500 to $3,000. Rule out segments that want in-person support. Segment with the highest buying power (other coaches) can be a later offer. Launch a 6-week to 3-month accelerator cohort with 1 to 2 group calls per week and async community support. Build as you go, one week ahead. Freemium plus low ticket in one community was the lowest-converting setup seen. Go direct to paid on the about page. If using free, make it a funnel to high ticket. Lead magnets can live outside the community.

## 7. Solo consultant who can solve many problems (AI and systems consulting)
Situation: strong word-of-mouth consulting, many types of clients, a broad offer ("I will help you systematize and save time") that did not resonate, builds priced well below peers, a large untapped old email list, a new community with a small member count.
Advice: two-stage plan. Stage 1 now: keep custom case-by-case work, raise prices significantly, reduce deliverables, add consistent content to drive lead flow. Expect revenue to roughly double. Stage 2 when delivery becomes the bottleneck and a pattern emerges in who buys and what: pick one repeatable core offer with a concrete outcome (for example save a stated number of hours per week without paying for a human assistant) and sell it to the same avatar. Push hard enough to break delivery on purpose, so the scalable offer gets engineered. Clients pay for speed and outcome, not hours. Treat the community and building in public as an attraction engine, not the product. Analyze the old list with AI tools to confirm audience fit before mailing, and wait for the launch posts before sending.

## Patterns across reviews

- Underpricing is the most repeated finding.
- Offers with too many deliverables or tiers confuse buyers. One core offer, then a continuity upsell.
- The offer should match the audience that already exists. Starting from scratch on a new audience is the slower path.
- Sell the end result and the money, status or time it unlocks, not the skill.
- Done-for-you and one-to-one are valid starting points, and the bottleneck decides when to move on.
- Uncertainty about the niche is solved by selling and observing, not by more worksheet work.
- Content strategy comes after the first premium sales.
- Delivery promises that signal low confidence (open-ended access) hurt.
- Specific proof, especially visual and numerical, justifies a higher price and a stronger guarantee.
