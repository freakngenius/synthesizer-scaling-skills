# Stage 2E and 2F: Launch Convert and Launch Deliver

Coverage note: the source notes for these two phases are thin. The program's sales training is released just before the Convert phase, and the delivery training is released once customers are signed. Coach from the principles below, label anything beyond them as scaffolding, and ask the user to paste the program's sales script, discovery, pitch and objection-handling lessons (listed in the program's resources) so you can coach from the source.

## 2E: Convert (sales calls)

Goal: turn booked calls into paying customers. The program's math, used for planning: at a 33 percent close rate, a $2,000 offer needs 15 calls for $10K per month; a $5,000 offer at a 60-call month gives $100K per month at 20 customers. Track calls taken, offers made, closes, and revenue per week.

Process facts from the notes:
- No sales page or contract is required. The offer is made on the call and payment is taken on the spot. Payment processors named: Skool (built in) or Wise (useful for international clients where Skool payments can be declined). A tutorial for processing payments on Skool and for moving a member between paid and free is in the resources list.
- Record calls with a free recorder (Fathom was named) and review them.
- Use the two-call close if the user is new to selling: a short research or qualifying call first (about 15 minutes), then a longer call to present the offer. Frame the first call so the user stays in control, and book the second call before ending it ("I can build you a custom plan, does Monday or Tuesday work?"). Experienced sellers can make the offer on the first call.
- Qualify with questions that make ROI tangible. In one review the questions were current versus desired salary and position, which made a $5,000 investment clearly justified against a $20K to $40K per year gap. Let the prospect calculate their own ROI with a few questions (for example booking rate to calls to revenue) rather than telling them.
- Price on the call, not in DMs. Price is anchored to the value of the outcome, not to hours spent. Reviewers repeatedly said members underpriced.
- For one-to-one requests outside the program structure, set a minimum acceptable price and offer it at that rate. If they decline, move on.
- If someone is not getting results or is unhappy, they can leave. No contracts are needed in the program's model. Communicate decisions openly. Most people respond well to honest reasoning.
- B2B opportunities (companies with many employees) are separate from the core offer. Use the same backbone, customize it per business (headcount, problems, desired outcomes), price on total transformation value, and get pricing guidance before quoting. Longer cycles and more decision-makers.
- Mindset line: you are either learning or earning. Even one booked call is a win. Momentum calls with the customer success coach exist if energy dips mid-launch.

Coaching moves:
- Before each call, ask the user for the prospect's stated goal, problem, and mechanism preference (from the DM or research call), and write a one-paragraph call plan: opening frame, three diagnostic questions, the offer, the price line, and the close.
- After each call, ask: outcome (paid, follow-up booked, not a fit), what the prospect said in their own words, which objection appeared, what the user would change. Update the ledger and the objection list.
- Offer roleplay. Play a prospect with a realistic objection (price, timing, "need to think", "ask my partner", "can AI do this"). Debrief on whether the user diagnosed, quantified the gap, stayed calm and asked for the decision.
- (coach scaffolding) A call counts as progress only if it ends with a commitment: payment, a booked follow-up with a named goal, or an intro to the decision maker. Mark everything else as cold in the tracker so the pipeline stays honest.

Objection handling: capture every objection in the ledger with the prospect's words, then build a short response bank with the user. Do not invent a script from nowhere. Use the program's objection lesson if the user can paste it.

Exit bar for Convert (launch): calls taken against goal, closes and revenue logged, objection list started, payment flow tested, first customers onboarded.

## 2F: Deliver

Goal: get customers the best result humanly possible. Results are the moat.

Delivery models (from the offer pillar):
- Coaching: one-to-one calls. Easiest to sell and deliver. Best start.
- Cohort: group calls plus community. Use when selling to 10 or more at once. Structured weekly cadence in the program: kickoff and backbone review, then attract, convert and deliver content in order.
- Course: pre-built curriculum plus group calls and community. Build it through a cohort, not before.

Principles:
- Build as you go. Do not wait to finish the program before selling. The program launched with an empty classroom and built weekly based on member needs. Build one week ahead of participants. This generates cash while building and shapes the product around member needs.
- Keep a time-bound package (six weeks to six months), not an open monthly engagement, and offer the next package at completion.
- Do not offer extended access as a safety net or open-ended access promises. It weakens the guarantee and complicates continuity.
- One community is enough for one-to-one coaching under about 20 clients. Create a separate paid community only when it solves a problem.
- A free community is a funnel and container, needed mainly when bringing in a large external audience. During the launch, do not split calls to action between calls and the free community. Move people to the free community after the launch.
- Existing low-ticket members: offer the premium program first and credit what they already paid. Do not overhaul the low-ticket setup until after launch.
- Cohort timing: allow about 30 days from today to fill a cohort.
- Delivery unlocks upsells and ascension. Record client wins and testimonials continuously. They feed the proof database and later posts.
- Set up the next step for each client before they finish (resell at completion).

Coaching moves:
- Make a delivery plan with the user: customer list, start dates, weekly session schedule, what each session covers, what homework, what results to measure.
- Define the measurable outcome for each client in writing at the start so the guarantee is clean.
- Track delivery hours per client. When time becomes the bottleneck and the user repeats the same protocol on every call, flag the move to a cohort or course (see `10-after-launch.md`).
- Ask for a testimonial and a photo or screenshot at the milestone. Add to the proof database.

Exit bar for Deliver: every customer has a plan and start date, outcomes are written, wins are being collected.
