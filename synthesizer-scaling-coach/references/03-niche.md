# Stage 1C: Niche

## Purpose

"The better the niche, the better the business." The niche is who the user helps. The program calls it one of the most impactful decisions an educational creator makes, because the whole business follows from it. A great niche brings income, impact and purpose. A poor one brings none of them. If the user is unsure, say that the review call exists for this decision and that it is a "super important decision".

The user only has two variables to decide: the niche (who) and the outcome (what they get). The worksheet formula is:

> I help [niche] to [outcome].

The program's specificity level is shown by examples like "men over 50 learn piano without sheet music" and "have a 10-minute conversation in Korean without translating in your head."

## The five niche pillars (worksheet fields)

1. Who you serve. The specific person.
2. Their current situation. What they have in common, and what most people do not have.
3. Their problems. What stresses and frustrates them. The program: "value is created by alleviating suffering."
4. Their desires. The future state they crave. The creator's job is to solve the problems that stand between them and the desires.
5. Who you do not serve. Arguably more important than who you do. Serving everyone means serving no one.

The program's example for its own niche: educational creators who have achieved a valuable outcome; they often have a community and want more money and impact; their problems are a weak model, time on the wrong things, overwhelm; their desires are $10K to $200K per month, impact, and freedom; not served are non-creators and people with no valuable outcome yet.

## Niche selection criteria

Essential:
- They have a problem the user can solve. Otherwise "you are screwed from the very beginning".

Ideal, and score each:
- The user knows them well. The program's ideal is "your niche is old you".
- They have money. The same outcome is valued at $5 by someone with $10 and far more by someone wealthy.
- The user likes them. The host's first business crumbled when he stopped liking the people he served.
- The user already knows many of them. Selling to an existing network short-circuits months or years of audience building. If the user has a network or audience, they are the niche, so do not build a new one from scratch.
- Specific. Broad niches ("young men") fail to resonate. Specificity unlocks attraction.
- Unique or blue ocean. Far from essential. Red oceans prove demand but need strong proof or a different message.

## Niching down by chunking

If the niche is too broad, ask what sits inside it, repeatedly, until something resonates. Examples from the program: entrepreneurs, creators, educational creators, high-ticket synthesizers. Women, mums, mums after menopause. People, accountants, accountants with 10 or more employees. Chunk until you find resonance, then stop. Too narrow is also possible, so check market size: could the user find 30 of them in the network audit?

## The accordion strategy (important when the user cannot choose)

- Starting out: go broad enough to get your foot in the door, make sales, and learn who you help best.
- Scaling: go narrow, one niche, one outcome, one product. This makes delivery scalable and marketing sharper. "When you speak to everyone, you speak to no one."
- Expand first, contract over time.
- Multiple niches or products multiply complexity. A second niche needs a second outcome and a second product.
- If the user cannot name a specific ICP, the program's coaching in reviews was: keep doing the work (done-for-you or one-to-one) and sell aggressively to your network for 30 days, observe who buys fastest and who gets the best result, then narrow from data. Niche and outcome tend to stay stable whether delivery is done-for-you or done-with-you. Clarity comes from serving clients and seeing where the most value is created. Come back after 30 days and report whether the constraint is delivery or lead flow.
- Overly narrow coaching programs can backfire by alienating the founder's existing network.

## Outcome vs mechanism (do not confuse)

- The outcome is the core desire: what the client ultimately wants (for example earning a stated amount, getting a scholarship, getting promoted). It is boolean and measurable where possible.
- Steps, clarity, frameworks, deliverables and lessons are mechanisms or stepping blocks, not outcomes.
- If the user says their outcome is "clarity" or "learn the tool", ask what the client gets in their life or income because of it, and write that.
- Desire intensity sets price (see 04-offer.md), so pick the outcome the niche wants most badly.

## Interview sequence

1. Start from evidence, not preference: "Who has paid you, asked you for help, or thanked you for a result in the last 12 months? List them." Group them.
2. "Which group did you enjoy most, get the best result for, and would happily talk to every day?"
3. "What do they have in common right before they hire you or ask for help?" (current situation)
4. "What are they stressed or frustrated about, in the words they use?" (problems; use their language, not the user's)
5. "What do they badly want that solving those problems unlocks?" (desires)
6. "Who looks similar on the surface but is a bad fit? What happens when you work with them?" (who not to serve)
7. Score the top two or three candidates against the criteria in a small table (yes, partial, no) and pick a recommendation.
8. Write an "I help [niche] to [outcome]" statement for each option and mark the chosen one.
9. If broad, chunk down two or three levels and compare resonance. State which one you recommend and why.
10. Decide: commit to a niche for the launch (accordion start) or run the 30-day broad test. Make the decision explicit and dated.

Quality bar:
- The niche can be pictured as one person, with a job, a situation and a problem.
- The outcome is boolean or measurable, and it is what the niche wants, not a step.
- The user can name 30 or more people in this niche from their network or audience (or has a clear way to reach 30).
- The "who I do not serve" line is specific and has a reason.
- The user's proof (1B) shows they can deliver this outcome for this niche.

## Pressure test questions

- "If I put this sentence on a post, would the right person feel called out by name?"
- "Is this who you serve, or what you wish to serve? Where is the evidence?"
- "Does your top proof show this outcome for this niche?"
- "Could you list 30 of them tomorrow?"
- "Is this three niches joined by 'and'?"
- "If the niche is old you, what was your situation then and what changed?" (feeds the backstory)
