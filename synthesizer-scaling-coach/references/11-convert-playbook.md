# Stage 2E and 4: Convert playbook (sales calls)

Read when the user has booked calls, or is writing or rehearsing a sales script, pitch, or objection handling. Source notes with full detail and verbatim scripts are in the library skill: `notes/2-launch-convert-1-discovery.md`, `2-launch-convert-2-pitch.md`, `2-launch-convert-3-objection-handling.md`, `sales-script-template.md`. Original docs: Launch Convert Worksheet and Sales Script Template (links in the library `master-index.md`, Launch Convert entries).

Exit bar for this stage: the user has a customized script they have read at least 10 times, a pitch of 2 to 5 minutes built from their Backbone, a call tracker in use, and has made a real pitch on a call (a sale is not required to pass).

## The call, in order (about 45 minutes; 30 to 60 is the normal range)

1. Prep (about 5 min before): quiet room, headphones, printed script, review the applicant's profile and earlier DMs, record the call. The program suggests six physiological sighs to settle.
2. Intro and framing (1 to 2 min): light rapport, ask if they can take notes, state the agenda: you ask about where they are and where they want to go, and if there is a fit you explain the offer and they decide.
3. Discovery (20 to 30 min), the Gap Framework: where they are, where they want to be, the cost of staying put, then you are the bridge. Five checkpoints: primary motivation (why they booked), current situation, pain and problems (biggest challenge, why unsolved, what tried, how long), desired situation (12-month goal, why, effect on life), cost of inaction (what happens if nothing changes in 6 months). Ask a 1 to 10 commitment question and let them explain their number.
4. Transition (about 1 min): "Before we move on, what else should I know?" Ask twice. Address early concerns. Give evidence-based reasons you can help, then ask permission to walk through the offer.
5. Pitch (2 to 5 min), the Bridge Framework: (a) one sentence promise of transformation, with a measurable result and timeframe, written in boolean terms that can be judged true or false; (b) three short pillars in sequence (foundational quick win, core mechanism, what comes next) each tied to the prospect's own words and to true proof; (c) how it is delivered, in benefits not features. Ask "What stands out?" after each pillar. No slides. Pull the content from the Business Backbone: Outcome becomes the promise, Unique Mechanism becomes the pillars, Deliverables become the delivery.
6. Commitment (2 to 5 min): confidence 1 to 10. Ask why not a 1 and what would make it a 10. Aim for 8 to 10 before price.
7. Investment: state the price plainly with an anchor that is true. Then stop talking.
8. Objections and close (5 to 15 min), see below. Ask for payment in full first. Offer a split only after full payment is confirmed as the remaining barrier.

## Objection handling

- AAA: Acknowledge, Add (frame the caution as a trait of thoughtful buyers; optional), Ask a question that surfaces the real concern.
- Double tap: ask again, more precisely, after the first answer ("Is it the process, the price, or something else?").
- Ask "What else?" until the list is complete. Isolate one concern at a time, test "if that were solved, would you join?", then re-ask for the sale.
- Money: confirm money is the only barrier ("if you had it in hand today, you would be ready?"), have them state the value in their own words, then "How can we make this work for you?" Payment plan only after that.
- Spouse or partner: find the decision-maker during qualification, invite them to the call or reschedule when both can attend, ask what questions the partner would have.
- "I need to think": affirm, ask what they need clarity on, ask what makes them think it could help, re-ask. If time is really needed, BAMFAM: book a specific follow-up from this meeting and send the invite.
- "Not the right time": remove time hypothetically and ask what else blocks them. Pay-now-start-later only if the user's business actually allows it.
- Guarantees: only describe a guarantee the user really offers. The program's own examples (30-day refund, a "high ticket or bust" 10-clients-in-6-months guarantee) are examples, not defaults.

## Rules for the coach

- Never write a guarantee, price anchor, discount, scarcity claim or result the user has not confirmed. Mark placeholders until the user supplies them.
- The lesson's pain language is forceful. Teach the checkpoints, keep the tone curious and kind, never invent distress, and say plainly if the prospect cannot afford it or is not a fit.
- The claim that the pitch drives "up to 80%" of the sale is the instructor's, not a measured figure. Do not repeat it as data.
- Calls are rehearsed: read the script aloud 10 times, record own calls, role-play (use the Roleplay command), expect 10 to 30 pitches before it feels natural.
- Tracker: after every call log date, name, outcome, cash collected, payment terms, notes (World's Simplest Call Tracker, linked in the library index under Launch Convert 4. Action Steps). It doubles as the follow-up list for later launches.
- Diagnose closing only after about 30 calls (Scientific Method call). Above 20 percent close rate, look at calls booked instead. See `13-diagnosing-and-post-launch.md`.

## Follow-up after calls (from the Attract Call Recording call)

Daily for 3 days, weekly for 4 weeks, monthly for about 6 months, with relevant proof or a similar client case study, not generic nudges. A scheduled BAMFAM beats generic follow-up.

## Continuity and upsell (when the user asks about the next sale)

Continuity extends the same work (example in the notes: $3,000 for 3 months vs $5,000 for 6 months). An upsell is a new, higher-level offer. Raise price only after some sales validate the offer, change one variable at a time, and compare revenue, not only close rate (10 leads at 50 percent and $1,000 is $5,000; 10 leads at 30 percent and $2,000 is $6,000). These are individual cases, not a price card. Never supply prices for the user's offer.
