# Stage 3: Attract playbook (after the launch, and the booking tactics used inside it)

Read when the constraint is calls booked, or the user asks about outreach, content, positioning, a free community, partners, or follow-up. The library skill has 30 attraction call notes, each with scripts and numbers. Start from `aggregates/mistakes.md` and `aggregates/scripts.md`, then open the call notes named below. Note: the program's Attract course (module "3. Attract") was still locked when this archive was built, so this stage rests on the weekly Attract calls, not on a structured curriculum. Say so when the user asks for "the lesson".

## The order of work (what to do first)

1. Check the calendar. If next week has few or no qualified calls, booking calls is the priority. Calls booked is the metric most correlated with scaling in the notes.
2. Warmest people first, then colder. Order from "The Ultimate Client Attraction Checklist" call: people from past calls who did not buy, former clients (ask for referrals, consider a referral bonus), warm network, people who engaged with content, new free-group members, active community members, other communities, cold. Revisit warm contacts about monthly. A one-time 90-minute sprint through the list is recommended.
3. DM-to-call: ask an easy yes/no or this/that opener, learn the goal and bottleneck, connect a call to their named outcome, send the link with context, follow up once or twice (the next day, then once more only with a new reason). Do not coach for free in the DM; for colder prospects share proof from a similar client.
4. Outreach queue order ("Outreach Roasts" call): recent unread DMs, people offered a call or sent the link, prior bookers and no-shows, post commenters and likers, auto-DM replies, people online now, active members. Track "call offered, no link sent" separately from "link sent, not booked".
5. Protect focus: one task, phone off, Do Not Disturb, timer. The call claims 20 focused minutes beat two distracted hours.
6. Follow-up cadence after a sales call: daily for 3 days, weekly for 4 weeks, monthly for about 6 months, with relevant proof, not generic nudges.

## Positioning and message (strategy before tactics)

- Strategy first: who you help, what outcome, how you are different. Tactics (about page, outreach, titles, thumbnails) come second ("Strategy vs Tactics" call). The Backbone is the strategy.
- NAT profile test: Name the niche, state Authority or proof, describe the Transformation ("Broke Student to Financially Free" call).
- Message resonance worksheet ("Skool Will Soon Enter A New Era" call): five awareness levels (unaware, problem, solution, product, most aware) decide the hook and CTA; market sophistication decides whether to make a direct claim, a bigger claim, a new mechanism, or proof; belief sequence (problem worth solving, mechanism is the best way, this product is the best way). Unique mechanism formula: "Most [niche] try to achieve [outcome] through [old way]. The problem is [problem]. The new way is [mechanism], because [reason]."
- Outcome must be tangible and checkable (a number and a date), not "get in shape".
- Moat: a specific person, a specific problem, a specific way of solving it ("Attract Call Recording" call).

## Channels and assets (use only the ones that fit the user)

- Free community plus calls: new member DMs while they are active, onboarding video of 30 to 60 seconds plus a 10 to 20 minute orientation, target about 10 regulars (Scientific Method call; Self Licking Ice Cream Cone call).
- YouTube: one strong video per week once it is the chosen channel; direct link in description and bio (no link trees); call to action before audience drop-off; evergreen proof video built from career seasons, each with problem, story, lesson, evidence ("Make This Video" call).
- VSL: build after about 10 sales when proof exists and call conversion is the bottleneck. It filters and warms, it does not create traffic ("VSL Edition" call).
- Events and recordings: weekly live event, email the recording, DM attendees, commenters and no-shows; a 24 hour event with a booking link scheduled more than 24 hours ahead (Ultimate Checklist call).
- Instagram keyword-comment into DM; first message automated but edited to sound personal; give the resource after they reply; voice notes after they respond.
- Setters and tracking: split lead pools, track conversations, links sent, calls booked; a typical setter commission in the notes is 5 percent of cash (Diagnosing Constraints note). Setters get the script and an SOP.
- Joint ventures: build the relationship and solve their problem before pitching; a low-effort, reputation-safe promotion arrangement ("How to get a big creator to promote your Skool" call).
- Price increase campaigns: announce a real increase, re-contact earlier non-buyers, staged increases, never fake urgency ("Outreach Roasts" call).
- Qualification: ask goal and capacity in DMs and on the booking form; route non-fits to free material; do not open with "this is expensive". Income is an indicator, not a certainty.

## Rules the program repeats

- Execute the proven model before experimenting. The Skool Hotline, hybrid cohort, paid ads and similar items in "4 Brand NEW Skool Scaling Tactics" were early tests the hosts did not call validated.
- Competitor and AI research can estimate but not prove revenue; the hosts warn AI output needs verification ("Copy and Paste This AI Prompt" call).
- Platform-specific advice (Skool discovery keywords, Growth Boost ads, Skool DM limits, algorithm changes) was dated when recorded. Treat it as history and verify against the live platform.
- Examples with numbers are individual cases, not benchmarks. State the context whenever quoting one.

## Coach moves

Ask: what did you do yesterday for attention, how many conversations, how many calls booked this week, which channel produced the last 3 calls. Then place the user in step 1 to 6 above, pick the single missing step, and give a count and a date. Offer to run the Roleplay command for a DM or a call.
