# Stage 1B: Proof

## Purpose

The program's core claim: proof comes before the offer. "Proof is greater than promise." Proof is a demonstrated ability to get results, and the offer is created from it. The common mistake the program names is building an offer with no proof. You can only help people you have already shown you can help.

The program admits members on the basis that they have achieved some valuable outcome. "Even the smallest bit of proof can be leveraged." When in doubt, the user is their own first client: solving a valuable problem once for themselves qualifies them to help others.

The program's own example of a proof path: the host beat procrastination himself, sold help with it, grew a YouTube channel to 500K as a byproduct, then taught that growth and built a second $100K per month business. He noted that he only pivoted once the case study was strong; a few thousand subscribers or $2,000 per month would not have justified it. "The proof creates the author."

## Types of proof

Personal transformation. Client results. Audience or income growth. Repeatable frameworks. Before and after screenshots. Testimonials (the more credible the person, the better). Awards, scale numbers, named clients, launches, downloads, revenue, years of experience, things built.

## The proof database (worksheet output)

A list of the user's best proofs, each with an image or link where possible (payout screenshot, before and after, testimonial, press, metrics). The program wants a "proof collection machine": people pay $1,000 to $10,000 for undeniable proof, and images carry a lot of information fast. Write down even small proofs, "the best that you have". The database grows as the program collects more.

## Interview sequence

Use these in order and stop when the bar is met.

1. "List every result you have produced, for yourself or for others, that someone else would call valuable. No filtering. Include money, time saved, audience growth, health, skills, projects shipped, things you built, awards."
2. "For each one: what was the situation before, what did you do, what was the measurable result, and when?"
3. "Who else can confirm it? Is there a screenshot, a link, a testimonial, a number in your inbox?"
4. "Which of these would your future client most want for themselves?" (This links proof to niche and outcome.)
5. "Which of these did you get for other people, not only yourself?"
6. "If you had to pick the three that would make a stranger stop scrolling, which are they and why?"

## Quality bar

- Each proof has a number, a timeframe, or a named outcome. "Helped lots of people" fails.
- Each proof has evidence or a way to get it. Mark unverified items.
- At least one proof is about a result your future niche wants. If the best proof is impressive but unrelated, say that it may not carry the offer, and ask what the closest related proof is.
- Mix of types: at least one personal, one for others, one with a visual.
- Ranked: top three identified for use in posts.

## Common cases

- No client results yet: use the user as first client. Draft the transformation story: before state, what they did, after state, how long it took, what changed in numbers. Then plan to create a second proof fast (a free or low-cost pilot with one named person, with a testimonial agreed upfront).
- Many proofs across unrelated fields: do not delete them. Rank by fit to the likely niche (decided in 1C), and keep the rest for the backstory.
- Proof is old: ask for the most recent equivalent, and date every item.
- Impressive credentials but weak outcome link: ask "which of these shows the result the client wants?"

## Pressure test

- Would a skeptical stranger believe each item without trusting the user?
- Is anything stated that the user cannot back up?
- Is there a visual for the top three?
- Does the top proof match the outcome that 1C and 1D are heading toward?

