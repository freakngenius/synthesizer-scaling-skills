# Stage 1E: Backstory (the Dream Creator Setup)

## Purpose

The backstory communicates the "dream creator setup", which the program describes as a positioning concept with two conditions:
- The user's past situation is where the niche is now.
- The user's current situation is where the niche wants to be.
When both are true, authority becomes effortless. The audience thinks: "they used to be where I am and are now where I want to be, so they can help me." The process or mechanism behind the transformation is what creates the value.

If the user has not made the transformation the niche wants, they can use a case study of someone they have helped. Different backstories can be used for different offers or audiences.

Why stories work: emotion drives motion. A story that shows deep empathy for the niche and reveals a unique mechanism that changed the user's life is described as the story that sells better than anything else. The backstory is also the principle for building the product, not just a sales device.

## The five parts (worksheet fields)

1. Past situation. Where the user used to be. It should mirror where the niche is now. Describe a typical day.
2. Past problems. The rock bottom, the most painful turning point, the problems that held them back. These should match the problems the niche has today.
3. Past desires. What they deeply wanted to change, their core motivation. Matches what the niche wants.
4. Epiphany or breakthrough. The moment things changed, the belief or action that shifted everything. This should be the unique mechanism of the offer (1D pillar 3).
5. Current situation. What they accomplished as a result. This is the outcome of the offer and where the niche wants to be.

The program's example: past situation trying to make $10K per month and confused with no clear offer; problems of low traffic and low-ticket results; desires of time freedom and $10K to $100K per month; epiphany of discovering a program that taught high-ticket offers and scalable systems; current situation of financial and time freedom while teaching others the same.

## Rules

- Short and outcome-focused. Include only details that reinforce why the offer works. Compress to the essential parts and deliver the epiphany clearly. "Your audience does not care about fluff, they want the gold."
- It needs to be true. If it does not fit the user personally, use a client case study.
- The same story works in several formats: a worksheet-length version (one to three lines per part), a compressed paragraph version for the launch posts, and a post version in the celebration format (see `07-launch-prep.md`, post 2, and `08-launch-attract.md`, post 4).

## Interview sequence

1. "Think of the last time you were stuck where your niche is now. What was a normal day like?" Get specifics: time of day, tools, feelings, numbers.
2. "What was the lowest point? What did it cost you, in money, time, health, relationships?"
3. "What did you want so badly that you would have paid for it?"
4. "What was the turning point? What did you realize or do differently? Be specific about the idea, not the story around it."
5. "What does your life or business look like now, in concrete terms and numbers?"
6. "Which parts of this would your niche recognize in themselves?"
7. Draft the first pass in the user's own voice (long form). Then compress to the worksheet version (one to three lines per part). Then the compressed paragraph.
8. Test: read the past situation and problems aloud. Would the niche say "that is me"?

## Pressure test

- Match check: past situation equals niche's current situation, current situation equals niche's desired situation, epiphany equals the offer's mechanism. If any of the three fails, name which and ask what to change (the story, the niche, or the mechanism).
- Is the epiphany an idea the niche could try, not just an event?
- Is anything exaggerated?
- Is it short enough to say in under a minute?
- Do the numbers in the current situation match the proof database?
