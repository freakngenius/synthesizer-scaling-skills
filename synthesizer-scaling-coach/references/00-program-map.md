# Stage 0: Program map, mindset, placement

Read this at the start of a new user's first session, and any time the user asks how the program works or what comes next.

## What the program is

Synthesizer Scaling is a paid coaching program for educational creators (people with a valuable outcome they can teach: coaches, consultants, community owners, creators) who want to build a high-ticket business on Skool (the transcripts often spell it "School"). Hosts: Andrew Kirby (Synthesizer, free Skool community, large YouTube audience, leads the Attract calls) and Aidan (runs backbone reviews and the program). Matt Rice is head of customer success and coach. Olga is admin and content. Pricing at the time of the notes: $1,950 per month, marketed as a four-month program. No refund or cancellation terms were found in the materials, so the user should confirm those in writing if they are paying.

Goal the program names: "synthesizer freedom", three interdependent pillars.
- Income ($10K to $100K per month is the program's band)
- Impact (clients are glad they paid)
- Freedom (time and location)
The user may have to sacrifice one pillar short term to build the other two.

Mission stated in the welcome call: help educational creators get paid to share knowledge.

## The scaling system and roadmap

Three systems: attraction (a steady flow of qualified people), conversion (turning them into paying clients), delivery (results without eating all your time). The program says most creators fail by building these in the wrong order or all at once.

Roadmap, six steps, in this order:
1. Backbone (vision, proof, niche, offer, backstory)
2. Launch (the Rapid Launch Protocol, about 30 days)
3. Attract
4. Convert
5. Deliver
6. Scale and remove yourself from the business

Classroom, community, calendar are the three program surfaces. The calendar sequences live calls (backbone review, launch and track calls, attract calls on Sundays after launch, convert calls, implementation calls open to everyone). Lessons unlock just in time, so the user only watches what the current phase needs.

Early sequencing logic from the program: most people's bottleneck is "more eyeballs" (calls booked), not funnels. Building conversion systems too early is wasted effort. Results are the moat.

## Operating principles (quote these when relevant)

- Training light, action heavy. Watching produces no results.
- Follow the proven order, even when bored, overwhelmed or behind.
- Execute first, question later. The delay between hearing advice and installing it delays results.
- The one-source rule, 120 days: follow this program only. Unsubscribe from other gurus, remove distraction apps, add content blockers. Reason given: the "incompatible puzzle problem" (parts from different systems do not fit).
- Do the 100 per day: at least 100 minutes of primary lead actions (content, outreach) or $100 on ads.
- Make a weekly community post: what you did, what worked, what did not, questions. The most successful members post the most.
- Watch recordings within 72 hours. Attend as many calls as possible.
- Support ladder, in order: the program, weekly calls, community posts, search, the program's AI assistant, then the team (Matt or Olga). Most questions are answered by the next video.
- Google it, or ask ChatGPT or Claude, before asking a human.
- When you figure something out, post it for the person behind you.
- Excitement wears off within a week. Pushups count after you feel the burn.
- Everything is unscalable at first. Build the unscalable version, then scale it.
- When results lag, default to "skill issue" and improve the skill.
- No complaining. People finish in spite of litigation, injuries, and divorce.
- You are not special. Others have succeeded in every niche. Repeat the activities.
- Business is shockingly simple and surprisingly hard. The hard part is consistency.
- It will suck at first. It will take longer than expected. The only way to fail is to quit.
- The program promises systems and support, not income.

## Calibration: what the program's own results show

Use these to keep expectations honest. Source: the program's public results page for its previous cohort of 100 ("the100"), as recomputed by the owner in Sep 2026.
- The program advertises an average of about $70K made in 90 days. The recomputed mean was $70,021 and the median was $23,235.
- Median "total added" (90-day revenue delta) was $15,179. The welcome call quoted an average of $15,373 added MRR. Those are different measures, so ask which one is meant when a number is quoted.
- 17 of 99 scored members made $100K or more. 28 of 99 made under $10K. 5 were at zero or negative. The top three took about 40 percent of all revenue.
- Members in the AI niche (5 of 100) finished outside the top 30.
Tell the user these facts when they ask what to expect, or when they use hype language about their timeline. Do not use them to discourage action. Use them to set a median-based plan and to choose a first-month test measured in numbers (for example posts published, calls booked, paid conversions).

## Placement interview (new user, Stage 0)

Ask these in order, no more than four, then place the user:

1. "Where are you in the program right now? Which lessons or worksheets have you finished?"
2. "What have you already achieved that other people would pay to learn or have done for them?" (This tests the program's entry gate: a valuable outcome you have achieved.)
3. "Today, who pays you, how much per month on average over the last six months, and how do they find you?"
4. "What is the single biggest thing in the way of more income right now: not enough people reaching you, not enough of them buying, or too much of your time to deliver?"

Placement rules:
- No backbone yet, or only fragments: start at 1A Vision. If they cannot say what they would build a business around, run `clarity-mode.md` first, then return to 1A.
- Backbone drafted but never tested: run 1F stress test, then go to Launch Prep.
- Backbone done, no prep: go to 2A.
- Prep done: go to 2D (11 posts) and set a call-booking target.
- Calls happening: go to 2E.
- Customers delivering: go to 2F, then 3 to 6.

Then set the current focus in one sentence, and give the first action.

## Time and dates

Always put dates on the plan. The program's rule is that without a committed date a launch is unlikely to happen. Use the user's current date from the conversation. Do not assume a start date.
