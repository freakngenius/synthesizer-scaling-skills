# Stage 2A to 2C: Launch Prep (Rapid Launch Protocol, phase 1)

## The core idea

Look inward before outward. Sell to the warm audience first. The program says 99 percent of educational creators leave tens of thousands of dollars on the table by ignoring the people who already know them. Even with no formal audience the user has a warm network (social contacts, gym, hobby groups, past clients, email list). Reported examples: a $270K launch with no audience, steady $10K per month from a warm audience, and the program's own $350K and about $500K launches (the second reported as 119 calls booked).

Four phases: Launch Prep (plan, find message resonance, warm the audience), Launch Attract (book calls, nothing else), Launch Convert (sales calls), Launch Deliver. Prep and attract must be done before convert. The rapid launch maximizes existing assets before building new long-term audience in the later Attract phase.

## 2A: Choose the delivery model and the dates

Decision rule: estimate how many customers could be won in one month if going all out. Fewer than 10, start one-to-one coaching (easiest to sell and deliver). More than 10, run a cohort (group calls plus community). A course is built through a cohort later, not before. If unsure, choose coaching.

Timelines:
- 3-week launch (coaching): week 1 prep, week 2 attract (book calls, calendar closed), week 3 convert (calendar opens, calls taken), week 4 onward deliver while continuing to attract and convert.
- 6-week launch (cohort): week 1 prep, week 2 attract, weeks 3 to 6 an extended convert phase to fill the cohort, then deliver (selling stops during delivery so the user can focus on results). A cohort needs about 30 days of runway to fill. Count from today.

Worksheet (launch calendar): enter dates for prep start and end, attract, convert, deliver. Picking and committing to a date is critical. Without a date a launch is unlikely to happen. Replace the template links in the worksheet's launch links section with the user's own backbone, network audit and audience audit.

Interview: "How many clients could you realistically sign in 30 days if you went all out? How many could you serve at once? What date does prep start and end? When will the calendar open for calls?" Write the calendar in the ledger.

## 2B: Opportunity audit (highest-value exercise)

An opportunity is any place the user can reach their niche. Two lists.

### Network audit (people you know)
Goal: a list of people who could be clients or could introduce clients.
- Thresholds: 30 contacts is the bare minimum (red line), 100 is a good start (yellow), 300 or more is strong (green).
- Sources to scan: phone texts, WhatsApp, Telegram, Messenger, Discord, Gmail contacts (and past email threads), phone contacts, Facebook friends plus likers and commenters, Instagram followers plus likers and commenters, X followers, LinkedIn connections plus likers and commenters, Skool chats and free group members, email lists, past clients and customers. A tool for consolidating messaging apps was mentioned (texts.com).
- Columns: name, contact info, follow-up tracking. Each person could be a $1,000-plus sale. It is a volume game.
- The program credits single names from this list with very large deals, so do not prejudge who is a fit.

### Audience audit (places you can post)
List every platform and channel where the niche can see content: YouTube (videos, Shorts, community posts), Instagram (posts, reels, stories), Facebook (posts, stories), X, LinkedIn, Skool posts, email list, podcasts, joint ventures. Delete platforms you do not use and add missing ones. It doubles as the launch calendar: tick off posts as they go live. Even a small following is a good place to post, because warm audiences convert better than cold.

Both audits must be complete before launch. Launches are loud: post everywhere without fear of backlash.

Coaching moves:
- Have the user list their first 30 names in the conversation, then help them tag each as strong fit, maybe, or introducer.
- If they have an old or cold email list, treat it as cold. Recommend analyzing the list (with AI tools) to see who is on it and whether it matches the backbone niche before mailing. Wait for the launch posts before sending anything to it.
- If the network is exhausted, the program says to reach out to followers of relevant influencers in the niche.

## 2C: Message resonance

Message resonance separates the successful from the 95 percent who fail. Four types must align:
- Niche resonance: the audience feels spoken to directly, like hearing their name in a crowd.
- Outcome resonance: the result promised is deeply desired.
- Proof resonance: evidence that the user can deliver it.
- Mechanism resonance: the audience likes how the result is delivered.

When all four align, even a simple post draws inbound interest. Resonance is found through testing, not worksheets. The Three Cs, from fastest and shallowest to slowest and deepest:
1. Content: post variations of outcome, niche, proof and mechanism and compare response.
2. Chats: DM people to learn their desires and preferred mechanisms.
3. Calls: research calls to ask probing questions about struggles, goals and emotions.
Content and chats are mandatory for everyone, even without an audience. Calls are highly recommended and optional, but time-intensive and unpaid and useful for the data.

### Prep posts (minimum 3, on every platform in the audience audit)

Timing: post 1, then the backstory post 1 to 2 days later, then the validation post 3 to 6 days after post 1. Each is a data-collection tool, not a masterpiece. If a post does not resonate, change the proof or the outcome. Respond to everyone who engages by chat.

**Post 1: Broad message resonance test.** Purpose: learn which outcomes the audience wants from the user, before suggesting anything. Format: state a proof, then ask what they would want help with. Template options from the program:
- "I've [PROOF]. If I were to coach you, what would you want me to help you with?"
- "You get one wish. I will help you achieve it. What would it be?"
- "If I could help you achieve one result, what would you want my help with?"
Zero replies is acceptable. It is still data and practice.

**Post 2: Backstory (celebration).** Purpose: introduce the backstory and test its resonance. Template:
"Let's celebrate! I used to be [PAST SITUATION]. It sucked because [PAST PAINS]. But then I [EPIPHANY]. Which led me to [CURRENT SITUATION]. I couldn't be more grateful!"
Past situation should mirror the audience's current one. If it does not fit the user personally, use a client case study. Take the content from the locked backstory (1E).

**Post 3: Validation test.** Purpose: combine what was learned into a specific offer idea. Template options:
- "I've [PROOF]. I'm thinking about helping [NUMBER] [NICHE] to [OUTCOME]. Comment [WORD] if interested?"
- "After achieving [PROOF], it's my mission to help [NUMBER] [NICHE] achieve [OUTCOME] in the next [TIMEFRAME]. Who's with me?"
What to measure: engagement versus the user's normal posts. High engagement signals fit on proof, niche and outcome. Respondents move to DMs, then research calls, then sales calls. The program says this single post has generated six figures for some creators.

Coaching: draft the three posts from the locked backbone for each platform, adjust length per platform, and have the user rate their confidence. After posting, run a debrief: who replied, what words did they use, which outcome showed up most, who should get a chat.

### Prep chats (target 300 total, about 100 per day when possible)

Goals: get people on a research call, and find message resonance directly in chat. Who to contact: everyone in the network audit and everyone who engaged with the prep posts.

Principles: be a normal human. Follow curiosity. Build rapport. Frame expertise. Customize every message. Check their profiles for personal context. It is a volume game. Most people are glad to hear from you.

Program script (for each person, send in this order, adapting words):
1. Opener, one of: a personal check-in ("Saw you just had a baby! Congrats! How is the baby doing? How are you?"), a reconnect ("Hey! Long time no speak. How are you getting on?"), or a proof share ("Hey! I just [PROOF]. Super excited about it so wanted to let you know.").
2. Pivot question: "How are things going with [OUTCOME]?"
3. Ask for a research call, one of:
   - "I'm doing a few short calls to learn what people want help with in [topic]. Would you be open to a 15-min chat?"
   - "I'm thinking of helping [NUMBER] [NICHE] achieve [OUTCOME] in [TIMEFRAME]. Would you be against hopping on a quick call so I can better understand if this is a good idea?"
   - "I'd love to hop on a quick call with you to see if I can help you solve your problems. You down?"
4. Referral ask, added in the launch phase: "I'm working on a new [thing] that will help [number of niche] to [outcome]. Do you mind if I ask, do you know anyone who might be interested?"

Targets from the step summary: 300 outreach chats, up to 30 research calls (do as many as possible before moving on; move to attract if 30 is not reachable).

### Research calls (15 to 60 minutes)

Purpose: warm prospects so they are far more likely to buy, and find resonance. The program recommends them highly. Use the five-section script with probing questions throughout.

1. Framing and rapport. Light chat, then state the purpose: "The purpose of this call is for me to understand you as best I can and see if there is potential for me to help you. I will ask some questions about your life and goals. Sounds good?" This keeps the user in control and stops the prospect from flipping the questions.
2. Current situation. "What is your focus at the moment? What is going on in your life? How are things going with [topic]?"
3. Desired situation. "What are you trying to achieve? Where do you want to be in 12 months? If you could click your fingers and change one thing, what would it be? Why is that important to you?"
4. Current pains. "What is holding you back? What are you struggling with? How does that affect your life?" Painting the gap between current and desired state makes the ideal setting for a later offer.
5. Mechanism. "If there were a solution to that, what would your ideal solution look like?" (coaching, community, templates, worksheets)

Probes to use anywhere: "Why is that important to you?" "Why does that matter?" "How does that affect your life?" "What would happen if this did not happen?"

Close: "I have some ideas I think would really help, but I want to organize them first. Is it cool if I reach out in a couple of days?" Then follow up in a couple of days to share ideas and book a sales call. Record their own words for posts.

For the user's own record (coach scaffolding): after each call, capture in the ledger a line with the prospect type, their desired outcome in their words, top pain, preferred mechanism, buying signal (yes, maybe, no), and next action with date. After every five calls, summarize patterns and test them against the locked niche, outcome and mechanism. Propose specific edits.

Roleplay: when asked, play the prospect. Be realistic: ask counter-questions, drift off-topic, be vague. Debrief against the script (did the user frame, probe, and close?).

## Exit bar for Launch Prep

- Delivery model and dated calendar written.
- Network audit with at least 30 names (target 100 or more) and audience audit complete.
- At least 3 prep posts published on the audience audit's platforms.
- Chats started (target 300 total) and research calls booked (target up to 30).
- Resonance data summarized and any backbone edits applied.
- Call-booking goal chosen for launch (see `08-launch-attract.md`).

If the bar is unmet when the calendar date arrives, say so and ask whether to extend prep by a short, fixed number of days or to proceed. Do not let prep drift.
