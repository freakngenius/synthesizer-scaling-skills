# Stages 3 to 6: Attract, Convert, Deliver, Scale (after the launch)

Coverage note: the source notes include the program's sequencing logic and one detailed YouTube session. The Attract, Convert and Deliver course lessons were not in the notes. Use the principles here, say clearly when you are working from scaffolding, and ask the user to paste lesson transcripts as they unlock.

## Sequencing

- Month one: backbone and the rapid launch.
- Month two: the Attract course and weekly Attract calls unlock after the launch.
- Then Convert, after about a month of attracting. Then Delivery, which unlocks upsells and ascension.
- Why this order: it prevents overwhelm, builds momentum, and improves implementation. Most people's bottleneck is attention, not funnels. Building conversion systems early is wasted work.
- For the next 30 days after the launch, work exclusively on the identified bottleneck (attract, convert or deliver). Ask the user which it is, with evidence: calls booked per week, close rate, delivery hours per client.

## Choose the bottleneck (diagnostic)

- Fewer calls than the plan needs: attract.
- Plenty of calls, low closes: convert.
- Closing but delivery eats the week or results lag: deliver.
Quote the numbers. If the user cannot state them, the first task is to start tracking.

## Attract (principles from the notes)

- Skool as a free discovery and networking source, plus YouTube. LinkedIn, Instagram and X also work. Claims quoted from the program: about 30 percent of one community's traffic from Skool discovery; its own about page drew thousands of signups in 30 days.
- Heuristics quoted by the program: roughly $1 per follower per month for high ticket, and $0.10 per follower per month for low ticket. High-ticket examples reported: about $106 per subscriber for a community of 5,600, about $78 per subscriber for a channel of 3,800, about $31 per subscriber for 20,000, and $0.67 per subscriber for 375,000 (low-ticket reliance). Use these to show why price and offer matter more than audience size. Treat them as rules of thumb.
- No paid ads until the user has an organic engine and, per one review, around $30K per month organically, and the program's own advice elsewhere was $1,000 or more to spend. Ask which applies and default to organic.
- Content strategy waits until one core offer is validated and the first premium sales are made. Then build content around a specific avatar: their current situation, problems, desires, and the action to take on the offer. Specific content grows the audience the same way it was built.
- The post-launch weekly rhythm: a recurring weekly community post about what you did, what worked, what did not.

### YouTube to calls (lessons from the host's session)

- Start with search, not browse. Use autocomplete and trends to find low-competition keywords people search for. Search traffic is steady but builds few fans. Browse is where most viewership comes from long term.
- Blue ocean: rising demand and low supply. Check with trends.
- Repeat what works. The biggest mistake the host named was stopping a working format. Reuse nearly the same title and thumbnail with small changes. Examples: a morning routine video followed by a nighttime routine video with far more views.
- Consistency and volume build skill. Quality improves incrementally with each video (hook, script, edit, delivery).
- Trending topics: spot a trend early and make several videos in a row.
- Title formulas: "I tried [trending person or topic] for [timeframe]". "New study reveals why [problem]." Match tone and style to the subject.
- Funnel: video, then an unlisted sales video or direct calendar booking. Put the call-to-action in the description, in a pinned comment (more clicks than the description), 30 to 45 seconds into the video after the hook, and at the end. Frame the call as valuable ("free one-on-one [outcome] assessment") not "book a call". Add a qualifying video if volume is high.
- Content-offer alignment: titles, thumbnails, free community and calls to action must all speak to the same audience and problem. Mention the niche and proof early in each video. Consumer niches benefit from higher production value. Professional niches reward raw value and expertise.
- Lean into identity: do what makes you distinct more extremely than anyone else (props, gear, locations).
- Reddit threads in the niche can show trending topics.
- An audit prompt for the user's AI: check whether titles, thumbnails, free community, and calls to action all point to the same offer and audience.

## Convert (after launch)

Principles only: keep taking calls, record and review them, build the objection bank, refine the script from the calls themselves, and use the program's convert calls. Free community plus calls is the program's conversion setup. Sequence conversion work after attraction has produced enough calls to learn from.

## Deliver (after launch)

- Move from one-to-one to a cohort when time is the bottleneck and the same protocol repeats on each call. Move to a course when the cohort protocol is stable. End state is course plus community plus group Q&A, with 80 to 90 percent of effort in attraction.
- Cohort cadence: kickoff and backbone review, then attract, convert and deliver weeks.
- Upsell and ascension come from delivery results. Offer the next package at completion.
- Keep one niche, one outcome, one product. A second product needs a second outcome and a second niche, which multiplies complexity.

## Scale and remove yourself

The program only discusses team growth and removing yourself once revenue makes the conversation worthwhile. A setter becomes appropriate when over 100 DMs per day arrive and more than 2 calls per day are booked. Delegation of sales calls and delivery follows the same bottleneck logic.

## The accordion

Start broad to learn, then narrow to one niche and outcome when a pattern emerges. Re-run the backbone stress test at each 30-day mark with fresh data (who bought, what they asked for, what results they got) and edit the niche, outcome and mechanism accordingly.

## 30-day review template (coach scaffolding)

Ask the user for numbers and evidence:
- Posts published, DMs sent, calls booked, calls taken, closes, revenue, average price
- Top three words or phrases buyers used
- Biggest objection
- Delivery hours per client and client results
- What the user would change in the backbone
Then set the next 30-day focus (one bottleneck) and three numeric targets.
