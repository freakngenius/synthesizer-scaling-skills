# Clarity mode: for a user with no focus, purpose or niche yet

Use this when the user cannot state who they help, what they sell, or why they are here, or when they keep changing direction. The program's own position: nobody is 100 percent clear, the backbone is never finished, and clarity comes from taking action and collecting data. So this mode does not try to produce certainty. It produces a workable first hypothesis, a small test, and a date.

Everything here is (coach scaffolding) built on program principles: proof before offer, you are your first client, your niche is old you, sell to your network first, accordion strategy, action provides clarity. Tell the user when you are using scaffolding rather than program content.

## Rules for this mode

- Ask about the past before the future. Evidence of what has happened beats statements about what they want.
- One question at a time. Reflect back what you heard in their words. Look for the repeating pattern.
- Do not offer a niche until you have at least three data points from different parts of their life.
- Offer two or three candidate directions, each tied to evidence they gave you, and ask them to react. A forced choice is easier than a blank page.
- Keep a running "pattern list" in the ledger: themes, repeated phrases, energy highs, money signals.
- End the mode with a hypothesis (one sentence), a 7 to 14 day test, and the exit into Vision or Niche.

## Sequence

### Round 1: Where they already are (evidence)

1. "Walk me through the last 12 months of how you made money. Every source, who paid, how much, how they found you."
2. "What do people ask you for help with, unprompted? List the last five times."
3. "What have you changed in your own life or business that other people noticed, envied or copied?" (own transformations, the program's first client logic)
4. "What results have you produced for other people, however small?"
5. "What do you know that you take for granted and others pay hundreds or thousands to learn?"

Capture: sources of income, repeated help requests, personal transformations, client results. These feed 1B Proof directly.

### Round 2: Energy and fit (who they like and know)

6. "Which clients or people were easiest to help and left you with energy? Which drained you? What was different?"
7. "Which communities, groups, or industries do you already belong to or understand from the inside?" (old-you niche)
8. "Who in your network already looks like someone who needs this? Name ten without searching." (feeds the network audit)
9. "What type of person would you be happy to talk to every day for the next three years?" (the program: you must like them)
10. "Who has money and a problem that you can solve?"

### Round 3: Pain and desire (market side)

11. "Pick the three groups from above. For each, what is the most expensive problem they have, in time, money or stress?"
12. "What is the most desired result for each group, the one that would make them very happy if it came true?" Use the program's desire intensity idea: desire of 4 out of 5 supports a price in the thousands, 5 out of 5 supports much more. Ask for the rating and the reasoning.
13. "Can the result be stated as true or false, with a number or a date?"

### Round 4: Constraints

14. "How many clients can you personally handle per month, at what price, before time becomes the problem?" (feeds delivery model: one-to-one vs cohort)
15. "What will you refuse to do or deliver?" (who not to serve)
16. "What would make this fun enough to do for 120 days without quitting?"

### Round 5: Synthesis

Present a table of two or three candidate directions. For each: niche in one line, outcome in one line, proof from Round 1, network access from Round 2 (how many people they can name), desire strength from Round 3, delivery fit from Round 4. Say which you would test first and why. Ask the user to challenge it. Do not choose for them. If they push back, ask what evidence would change the ranking.

### Round 6: Make it testable

- Write a one-sentence hypothesis: "I think I can help [niche] to [outcome] because [proof]."
- Choose the smallest test from the program toolbox:
  - Post the broad message resonance test (prep post 1, see `07-launch-prep.md`) and watch the replies.
  - Send 20 chats from the network audit using the program's check-in script, and ask for five 15-minute research calls.
  - Make aggressive offers to the existing network for 30 days (accordion start) and note who says yes and what outcome they ask for.
- Set a date, a number and a place to record results. Add to the ledger.
- Exit: go to 1A Vision with the hypothesis in hand, or go straight to Launch Prep if proof is solid and the niche is chosen. The backbone gets refined by what comes back.

## When the user feels no purpose or energy

Do not turn this into therapy. Stay practical. Ask what they would be curious about for a year, what problems irritate them enough to fix, which past work they would repeat for free, what they are proud of. Then link the answers to the five elements of the backbone and a testable offer. If the topic is heavy, acknowledge it briefly and return to a small action.

## When the user has too many ideas

- Ask which idea already has paying evidence or a named buyer.
- Apply the program's rule: one niche, one outcome, one product at launch.
- Park the others in an "ideas later" list in the ledger with the trigger that would revive each.

## When the user is a beginner with no proof

- Use the "you are your first client" path: find a transformation they made for themselves that others want.
- If none exists, state the honest gap: the program requires some valuable outcome already achieved. Recommend a short proof-building sprint (help one named person get a measurable result, collect a testimonial), then restart at 1B. Do not fabricate proof or invent a niche to fill the worksheet.

## Signals to push the user to action

- They have rewritten the same section three times without new information.
- They have not talked to a potential customer in the last 7 days.
- They are researching other programs, tools or funnels.
Response: "A post, five chats or a research call will give you more clarity than another draft. Which one do you do by [date]?"
