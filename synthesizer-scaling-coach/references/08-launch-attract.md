# Stage 2D: Launch Attract (the 11 posts, DMs, call booking)

## Focus

The single focus of this phase: book calls. The program's central line: the number one metric to reach $10K to $100K per month is the number of qualified sales calls taken. Everything else is secondary. Prerequisites are a completed backbone and Launch Prep. If not complete, finish those first.

Call-booking targets and revenue bands from the program:
- Beginner: 10 calls booked, about $2K to $20K
- Intermediate: 30 calls booked, about $5K to $50K
- Advanced: 100 calls booked, about $20K to $200K
- The program's own second launch: 119 calls booked, about $500K
Have the user commit to a specific number and keep it in front of them (a member made "book calls" her phone wallpaper).

Rules for the phase:
- All calls to action point to booking a sales call or applying. Do not split attention with a free community during the launch. Send people to the free community after the launch closes.
- Post no more than one of the 11 posts per day. Spacing depends on the call-booking goal. Post on all platforms in the audience audit.
- Keep all content in the same niche and topic during the launch so the audience is not confused.
- Posts can be repurposed into videos to extend a launch. Repeat launchers reuse the posts with new mechanism details, dates and spots.
- This week is for booking calls only. Open the calendar for sales calls starting about a week later, filling days in order and opening more only when earlier slots are full.
- No sales page or contract is required. The offer is made on the call and payment is processed on the spot.
- Post client wins timed with posts where the offer is already revealed (around posts 6 or 7).
- For multi-platform creators, post on all platforms. Audiences rarely notice repetition.
- Momentum calls with the customer success coach are available if energy dips. Nervousness about the first post is normal. The only way through is through.

The program team writes the 11 posts customized to each member's offer and audience, using psychological triggers analyzed from top launches. This skill reproduces the structure so the user can write them with you. Ask the user to compare with the team-written set if they have it.

## Preconditions before drafting

Check the ledger. You need locked: niche statement, outcome, top proof (three), mechanism, backstory, offer (name, price logic, deliverables, guarantee, cap, close date), the platforms from the audience audit, and resonance language from prep (the words the niche used). If missing, go back. If the user wants posts anyway, draft with clear "unverified" markers and tell them which resonance data is missing.

Fill these variables once and reuse them in every post:
- NICHE (as the niche says it), OUTCOME, TIMEFRAME, OFFER NAME, NUMBER OF SPOTS, SPOTS TAKEN (true count), CLOSE DATE (true date), AUDIENCE SIZE or reach statement, PROOF LINES (top three), CTA (apply link or comment word), WORDS (comment keywords for posts 4 and 6, such as YES and NEW).

Do not publish placeholders. Every [BRACKET] must be replaced.

## The 11 posts, in order

Each entry: purpose, structure, rules. Write the final copy in the user's voice and keep platform norms in mind.

### Post 1. Tease
Purpose: raise curiosity and signal that something is coming. Structure: one or two sentences saying the user is working on a project that helps NICHE get OUTCOME, then a light prompt like "Anyone excited?" Add a picture.

### Post 2. Research poll
Purpose: gather data and engagement, and surface the niche's current situation. Structure: a question about how they currently handle the topic, a poll with four or five options that map to current states people are in (for example "doing it manually", "paying for something that is not working", "tried and it did not stick", "not doing it yet", "other, comment below"). The reply options come from the research calls.

### Post 3. Problem resonance
Purpose: show the user understands the niche's problem better than they do. Structure: title naming the problem; describe how most people in the niche handle it; why that fails (the cause, not the symptom); the consequences (wasted time, money, unfinished ideas); a one-line proof; what the user would do instead (the mechanism in one idea); close with a this-or-that question that lets the reader pick the better future.

### Post 4. Hero's journey
Purpose: the backstory, framed so the niche sees itself. Structure: "I used to be [past situation]. It sucked because [pains]. But then [epiphany]. Which led me to [current situation]." Then a comment-word call to action ("Want to know how I did it? Comment YES and I'll send the details.").

### Post 5. Proof
Purpose: credibility. Structure: title with a hook and the user's age or years in field if it helps ("I'm X. Here are my accomplishments"), three to five bullets of confirmed proof with numbers, then "Next up: helping NUMBER NICHE get OUTCOME in TIMEFRAME" and a link to apply. Use only items in the proof database that have evidence.

### Post 6. New mechanism
Purpose: tell the niche that there is a new way, and why the old way fails. Structure: "There is a NEW way to [outcome]." Describe what most people try and why it fails. Describe the new way (the mechanism) in two or three sentences. Why it is superior. A proof line. A short slogan. Comment-word call to action ("Comment NEW and I'll reach out").

### Post 7. Launch
Purpose: the official offer announcement. Structure: hook question for the niche; "this month I'm doing something I do not normally do"; announce the offer (name, who it is for, outcome, timeframe, number of spots); one paragraph of proof; the "You'll get" bullet list of deliverables (outcome-oriented, concise); why the cap exists (capacity, quality); spots already taken; application only, with a line asking unserious people not to apply; doors close on DATE with days left; audience statement; link to apply; a warm sign off.

### Post 8. How-to
Purpose: give value that demonstrates the mechanism, then offer help executing. Structure: title promising the outcome; proof line; step 1, step 2, step 3 of the mechanism in plain language; "If you want help executing this" and a bridge to the offer (name, what is included); link to apply; days left and spots left.

### Post 9. Guarantee
Purpose: remove risk. Structure: title with the outcome and "GUARANTEED"; who it is for; the promise; how it works in four numbered steps (apply, accepted, process, guarantee remedy); "I take all the risk" and why; call to action (apply or message a keyword); a PS explaining why the guarantee exists (it keeps the user honest about whom to accept). Guarantee conditions must match 1D.

### Post 10. Urgency and scarcity
Purpose: last call, based on true limits. Structure: title with days and spots; friendly reminder; spots total, taken, remaining; applications in review; doors close on DATE; what the user will do once the doors close (focus on members); recap of the offer and one proof line; a regret-avoidance line ("Do not be the person who looks back in six months in the same spot"); countdown line; call to action (apply or message a keyword). The numbers and the date must be true.

### Post 11. Sold out
Purpose: close the loop, build social proof and a waitlist. Structure: "SOLD OUT" title; the user closed doors (early if true); an apology to those who missed out; waitlist link; a note that focus now shifts to members; return to regular content. Only post if true. If not sold out, post a "doors closed" version with the true number of members and the waitlist.

## DM and call-booking flow (appointment-setting training)

Purpose: reliably book calls through DMs. The program credits this approach with multiple six figures sold in DMs and with the program's own launches. People wait for a sign to take action. Invite them, like inviting people to a restaurant. The user controls whether someone books.

Who to message, in order: people who engage with the launch posts (likes, comments), people in the network audit, Skool group joins, lead magnet opt-ins, then colder networking through comments.

Volume: easy mode is 10 to 20 new messages per day, medium mode 20 to 50, hard mode 100 or more. The program's own launch ran between medium and hard. Hire a setter when posts and opt-ins produce over 100 DMs per day and the user is booking more than 2 calls per day.

DM principles:
- Volume game. Every person and every follow-up can bring in thousands.
- Every DM builds trust and qualifies.
- Short messages, not paragraphs. Be human, joke, hold a conversation.
- Sell the call, not the offer. Position the call as a bridge to the goal.
- Use the AAA framework for about 95 percent of replies: Acknowledge, Add value, Ask.
- Never state the price in DMs. If asked, send two messages: (1) "Great question, I always appreciate someone who knows what they want and gets right to the point." (2) "That said, I'm not even sure we're a good fit yet, and I only make the offer to people I'm 100 percent confident we can help. Before we get into specifics, do you mind if I ask a few more questions to make sure it's a good fit?"
- If someone is uncooperative, rude, or silent after follow-ups, they are not qualified. That is fine.
- Try to stand out with unique language, pattern interrupts, and short custom videos.

Launch post conversation flow (six steps):
1. Conversation spark. Make it easy to reply. "Thanks for the love on my post! Curious, what resonated with you the most?" or "Thanks for the comment! What takeaways did you have?"
2. This or that. Early, non-threatening qualification: "Are you looking at [primary objective] or [secondary objective]?" or "Do you already have [solution related to the offer] or are you looking at setting one up?"
3. Desired situation. "What is the goal? Where do you want to be 6 months from now with that? What is your main priority right now with [topic]?"
4. Problems. "What do you think you need most right now to get [goal]?" or "What seems to be the biggest challenge in getting that done right now?" If it is what the user provides, they are a fit.
5. Offer help. "Would you like some help?" or "Would you be opposed to [seeing how we can get you your dream outcome] this week?"
6. Sell the call. Offer a time now ("Are you free right now?") or a calendar link ("Grab a spot here. I just opened a few extra spots for you."). Create a reason to book now: "I'll keep my window open while you choose a time. Mind telling me when you've found one?" or "Let me know what email you book with so I can make sure you're not double booked."

Follow-up norms: people drop off once qualification begins, and often reappear after two or three follow-ups. React to your own message to bump it. Examples: "I know how easy it is to lose track. Just bumping this one up." "Still interested in [goal], or is that not a priority right now?" "I just recorded a short video about how I can help with [what they asked]. Want me to send it?" "Just checking in, are you still interested in [what they asked for help with]?" A meme of someone waiting is allowed.

Whisper post flow: if someone comments interest on a short teaser, qualify with about three back-and-forth DMs, then book the call.

Two-call versus one-call close: beginners use a short research call (about 15 minutes) first to qualify, then a longer call to present the offer ("I can build you a custom plan, does Monday or Tuesday work?"). Experienced sellers can make the offer on the first call. For youth-focused niches, ask who else should be on the call (parents).

## Daily and weekly rhythm (coach scaffolding)

- Daily: one post (if the schedule calls for it), DMs to everyone who engaged, follow-ups, a count of calls booked.
- Evening check-in with the coach: posts live, DMs sent, conversations active, calls booked, and what words people used. Update the ledger.
- Mid-launch decision rules: if calls booked is on target, keep going. If under target by more than a third at the midpoint, check where the funnel leaks (impressions, comments, DMs, bookings) and fix the weakest step rather than redoing everything.

## Writing the posts with the user

1. Confirm the variables list above.
2. Draft post by post, in launch order, one at a time. Show the draft, ask "what is wrong or missing?"
3. Run a claims check: every number and credential must match the proof database. Remove anything unverified.
4. Run a voice check: does it sound like the user? Cut stock phrases. Keep it plain, specific and warm.
5. Place each in the calendar with a date, platform list, and call to action.
6. After posting, log results per post (views, comments, DMs started, calls booked).

## Exit bar for Launch Attract

- All 11 posts drafted, claims-checked and dated, with true spot counts and a true close date.
- Call-booking goal set and tracked daily.
- DM flow in use with a follow-up system.
- Calendar for sales calls open starting the planned date.
