# Stage 3 to 6 control loop: diagnose the constraint, run the weekly routine

Read when the launch is done (or the user has calls and sales data) and the question is "what do I work on now". Library notes: `notes/2-what-to-do-after-launch-diagnosing-constraints.md`, `2-what-to-do-after-launch-post-launch-protocol.md`, `attraction-calls-recordings-2-new-2026-scaling-tracker.md`, `attraction-calls-recordings-2-the-scientific-method-to-scaling.md`, `attraction-calls-uploaded-transcript-3-math-to-hit-your-goal-early-access-traini.md`, `attraction-calls-recordings-2-the-1-billion-prioritization-tool.md`. Templates: the Scaling Tracker sheet, the Math to $10K to $100K per month sheet, the prioritization roadmap sheet (links in the library `master-index.md`).

## Step 1: do the math backward from the goal

Revenue goal, price, show-up rate and close rate give the number of calls to book per month and per day. Ask the user for each input; never supply a price for them. Re-run when any input changes. The program's example: goal / price / close rate decides call count (a $100,000 goal with a $5,800 offer needed 72 calls in the notes' example, and a $10,000 goal with a $1,000 offer needed 42). Use the sheet, not mental math.

## Step 2: classify the constraint (attract, convert, deliver) before touching tactics

Rough diagnostic thresholds from the lessons (they are rules of thumb, not hard cutoffs):
- Under 2 calls booked per day: attract is the constraint.
- Show-up rate: 80 to 90 percent is the target, above 80 fine, below 60 urgent.
- Close rate: 20 percent is the floor, 30 percent or more is preferred. Do not judge close rate before about 30 calls. Above 20 percent, spend time on booking more calls.
- Plenty of views but few booked calls: funnel problem. Narrow the niche message, strengthen the call to action, track which content actually books calls, remove booking friction, call new leads immediately.
- About 5 to 10 clients and delivery eating over 50 percent of time (80 percent risks backsliding): delivery is the constraint. Protect the acquisition routine, get help, move toward a group model.

## Step 3: more, better, new (in that order)

Increase the volume of what already works, then improve how it is done, and only then add a new channel. A slow channel that works is a reason to refine it. One source for the program model: do not innovate on attract and convert, innovate on the product. Keep going 12 to 24 months (the Post Launch Protocol) and at least 12 months before pivoting (Math call).

## Step 4: the weekly routine (Post Launch Protocol, as written)

- Attract: 1 YouTube video per week (10 ideas, pick one, title, thumbnail, hook, brain dump, outline, record, edit, upload), Skool networking daily, 3 posts per week, 30 comments per day.
- Convert: free Skool group, 3 posts per week, 30 comments per day, a weekly call, 100 outreach messages per day, daily sales calls.
- Deliver: weekly program improvement, daily customer success, 2 group calls per week, 1:1 calls, weekly check-ins.
- Skool discovery setup is a one-time task. Skool allowed about 10 new DMs per week to non-contacts in one call; use Instagram or another platform beyond that.
- The routine assumes a Skool-based business. If the user's idea runs somewhere else, keep the structure (attention input, conversations, calls, delivery) and swap the channels, and tell the user you are adapting it.
- Sell a coaching offer immediately. For a cohort, finish the core program before selling.
- No sales from the launch: contact everyone you spoke with, personal check-in referencing their goal, then offer more information, then book a call. The protocol also suggests a steep (90 percent) discount for a testimonial. That is the program's suggestion; flag it as a business decision for the user, not a default.

## Step 5: track and review

Scaling Tracker: 11 metrics. Attract inputs (impressions or views, unique outreach conversations, posts, new free-group members) are typed in, sales outcomes (calls kept, calls taken, new customers, show-up rate, close rate, cash, revenue) come from the sales tab. Record cash collected today and total expected revenue separately. Compare input lines to calls booked to find which input correlates. Benchmarks in the notes: 100 unique outreach conversations per day, 1 to 2 posts per day across communities, 1,000 views per weekly video as a starting level, 5 to 10 new members per day, 1 kept call per day is a good start (2 great, 6 to 8 a full calendar). Daily morning stand-up (cash, calls, conversion, priorities, wins) is in the "7-Figure Morning Routine" notes.

Prioritization: keep a backlog of all ideas, a very short active list, finish and log what shipped (the $1 Billion Prioritization Tool). Ask "is this the most productive use of my time", not "is it productive".

## Coach moves

- Ask for current numbers first (calls booked last week, show-ups, closes, clients, hours on delivery). If they have none, send them to log the next 7 days in the tracker.
- Name one constraint and one action with a number and a date. Park everything else in the backlog.
- Contradiction check: if the user wants a new channel, ask which constraint it solves and whether the current channel has had more and better first.
