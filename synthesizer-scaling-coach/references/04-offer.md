# Stage 1D: Premium offer (seven pillars)

## Purpose

An offer is a proposal from the user to their niche. In this program a premium offer is one people pay $1,000 or more for (the term does not mean a membership tier). Validation threshold: five or more people paying at least $1,000. Even validated offers get 1 percent improvements after that.

A premium offer makes attract, convert and deliver all easier. The niche decides whether an offer is premium, not the user. Offer quality ladder from the training: bad offer means negative profit, decent means stagnation, good means okay, premium means strong profit. The target is an offer people "feel stupid saying no to".

Theory is simple, implementation is hard. The offer is created through conversations and sales calls, not a document. The training is light and the action heavy: the Rapid Launch Protocol is where the offer is tested.

## The seven pillars (worksheet fields) and quality bars

### 1. Outcome
Three tests: the user can deliver it, the market strongly desires it, and ideally competitors already succeed with a similar one. Bold enough to get attention, still deliverable. Desire is harnessed, not created ("you cannot change what people want, you can only present what you have as the solution"). Prefer boolean outcomes ("lose 20 pounds", "get a $X scholarship", "have an agent live in your business in two weeks") over "improve yourself". Pick the core desire that makes the person very happy when fulfilled. Transformation size equals value equals earning potential.
- Desire intensity sets price (training heuristic): 1 of 5 is about $5, 2 of 5 about $50, 3 of 5 about $500, 4 of 5 about $5,000, 5 of 5 about $50,000 or everything they have.
- Bar: one sentence, measurable, with a time frame if possible, something the user has proof for.

### 2. Pricing
Recommended range $1,000 to $10,000. If that feels uncomfortable, "increase the value, do not decrease the price". The program's reasoning: for about 95 percent of educational creators attract is the bottleneck, not convert or deliver, so higher ticket makes more money from the same traffic. Price inelasticity: doubling price loses around 10 percent of interest and still earns more. Pricing is art, then science: pick a price, test it, then optimize with data. There is no perfect price without data. The instructor's own ladder rose from $7 to $5,800 and beyond, and each increase earned more. Illustrative arithmetic from the training: 1,000 visits per month, 5 percent book a call (50), 20 percent close (10 clients) at $4,000 is $40K per month.
Price coaching from backbone reviews: members were repeatedly told they were underpriced. Frame price around ROI of the outcome (for example the value of a promotion, a scholarship, a retained client), and let prospects calculate their own ROI in discovery. Clients pay for speed and outcome, not for hours spent.
- Bar: a number in or near the range, a one-line ROI justification, and a plan to raise it after the first five buyers if demand holds.

### 3. Unique mechanism
Ask: what does the market currently do that fails, what do you know that they do not, what new method produces the result. Coaching, a course and a cohort are deliverables. The mechanism is a short argument for why the user's approach is new and better and why previous approaches failed, built to create an aha moment: "the problem is not you, the problem is the mechanism." "Insights equal value." Trends (high and rising demand, low supply) can be surfed early. Model, do not pioneer: "like X, but Y".
- Bar: two to four sentences. Names the failed old way, the new way, and why it works. Linked to the backstory epiphany (1E), which should be the same idea.

### 4. Deliverables
Three phases, from easiest to sell and hardest to deliver toward the reverse: one-to-one or done-for-you, then a cohort, then a course (with community and group Q&A). Start with one-to-one because it is easiest to sell and builds skill. Graduate when time runs out and the user notices repeating the same protocol on every call. End state: course plus community plus group calls, with 80 to 90 percent of effort on attraction. Sell packages of six weeks to six months, then resell at completion (monthly packages churn too much). Bottleneck rule: constrained by attract, stay in an earlier phase; constrained by delivery, move to a later one. Build as you go: build one week ahead of participants rather than before selling.
- Launch decision rule: estimate the customers possible in one month if going all out. Fewer than 10, start with one-to-one coaching. More than 10, run a cohort. If unsure, choose coaching.
- Bar: a short, simple list. The program flagged complicated deliverables as confusing. One core program, no mid-tier clutter. A continuity or next-step offer can sit on the back end.

### 5. Name
Call out the avatar plus a benefit. Keep it plain. Examples from the training: "Synthesizer Scaling", "The ADHD Reset", "Creator Monetization".
- Bar: a stranger in the niche understands who it is for and what they get.

### 6. Guarantee
Options: a 14-day no-questions refund; free work until the outcome is hit; refund only if requirements are met. A guarantee should signal confidence. Feedback from reviews: do not offer extended access as a safety net (for example 12 months of access), because it signals low confidence and turns the program into a 12-month commitment. An outcome-based guarantee is strongest when the user has exceptional proof.
- Bar: one guarantee, written in a sentence, with conditions that protect the user (client supplies access, information, and feedback on time; scope limits stated).

### 7. Scarcity and urgency
Pick a firm client cap (5, 10, 20 or 50; the program itself took 60). Set a firm close date (or seasonal, pricing or bonus urgency). The cap should be true because of delivery capacity. The date should come from the launch calendar.
- Bar: a number and a date taken from the user's capacity and calendar, never an invented countdown.

## Selecting among several offer ideas

Copy the offer selection table. Fill all seven pillars for each candidate. Rank by: proof fit, desire intensity, ease to sell, delivery fit with the user's capacity, price potential. Take the top candidates to the backbone review. The program says the decision is better made with a reviewer.

## Research and validation

- Speak with the niche to find the outcome they already want. Do not manufacture desire.
- Test different outcome language until one gets a clear response (see resonance posts in `07-launch-prep.md`).
- Study proven offers on Swipe.co and Udemy. Run incognito searches on YouTube, Google, Reddit and Facebook groups. Analyze competitors' funnels.
- Model a working offer as "like X, but Y".
- Five buyers at $1,000 or more validates.

## Common reviewer advice (from calibration, see `examples.md`)

- One offer. Remove mid-tier clutter. Raise the price. Add a back-end continuity upsell.
- Sell the end result (the promotion, the scholarship, the revenue) rather than the skill.
- If the user is already selling one-to-one successfully, push price up and push delivery to its limit on purpose. When delivery breaks and a pattern appears in who buys and what, that is the signal to engineer the scalable offer.
- A broad "get better at X" offer is harder to sell than an outcome-specific one.
- Existing low-ticket members: offer them the premium program first, credit what they paid, and leave the low-ticket setup alone until after launch.
- Freemium plus low ticket inside one Skool group converts worst. If a free community exists, use it as a funnel into a high-ticket offer, or go direct to paid.

## Interview sequence

1. "What is the one result your niche wants most that you can reliably produce? State it as something true or false with a number or a date."
2. "What proof shows you can deliver it? Point to the item in the proof database."
3. "How do people try to get that result today, and why does it fail?" (mechanism part one)
4. "What do you do differently, and why does that work?" (mechanism part two)
5. "How many clients can you handle in a month at full effort? What would you deliver with one-to-one, and what repeats on every call?"
6. "What is the result valued at by the client, in money, time or status? What price range fits that, and what price would feel too uncomfortable?"
7. Draft pillars 1 to 7. Ask the user to rate each from 1 to 5 on confidence, and probe anything under 4.
8. Pressure test and lock v1. Note which pillar is least tested and which launch activity will test it.

## Pressure test

- Would a buyer in the niche see the outcome as the answer to their top problem?
- Is the price a number the user is afraid of? If it is comfortable, ask if it is too low.
- Does the mechanism explain why past attempts failed?
- Is delivery something the user can do for the first ten clients without breaking?
- Does the guarantee match the confidence the proof supports?
- Are the cap and the date true?
