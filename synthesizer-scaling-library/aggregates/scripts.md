## Discovery Training (Stage: Launch Convert)
Source: notes/2-launch-convert-1-discovery.md

- Coach, framing: “I’ll ask you a couple of questions around where you’re at, what you’re working on, and where you want to go. Then if it sounds like we’re a good fit, I can walk you through what it looks like to work together.” Use after rapport and note-taking check.
- Coach, motivation: “What got you interested in booking this call in the first place?” If booked via DMs, preface it by acknowledging the prior conversation.
- Coach, clarify: “When you say ___, what do you mean?” or “Could you explain that in a little more depth?” Use when an answer is general or unclear.
- Coach, current situation: “What are you doing for work right now?” and “What do you sell? How do you price it?” Add niche-specific questions about present methods and systems.
- Coach, diagnose pain: “What do you think is the biggest challenge right now?” “Why do you think you haven’t solved it yet?” “What have you tried already?” “How long has this been going on?”
- Coach, goal: “What is your 12 month goal for the [thing]?” Follow with “What’s your motivation for getting to [goal]?” and “How would getting to [goal] impact other areas of your life?”
- Coach, inaction: “What happens if nothing changes over the next 6 months?” Use a supportive, curious tone, then ask about the cost in time, energy, or money.
- Coach, transition: “Before we potentially move on, what else should I know about you or your situation?” Use to invite concerns before pitching.
- Coach, fit and permission: “Based on everything you’ve told me I 100% believe we can help you because [reasons based on their situation and your proof]. Would you like me to walk you through what we do and how it works?” Replace the placeholder with verified evidence.

## Pitch Training (Stage: Launch Convert)
Source: notes/2-launch-convert-2-pitch.md

- Coach, transformation promise: “This is a 3-phase program to help you get from [current situation] to [desired situation] in about [timeframe].” Use as the opening sentence; fill with a verifiable result.
- Coach, start the pillars: “Would you mind writing down [1-3] in your notes? Just so we make sure we don’t miss anything.” Ask immediately before the pillar sequence.
- Coach, pillar 1: “The first pillar is called [name]. You see, after [proof] we’ve noticed that most people are doing [old way]... which leads to [problems], which results in [painful thing]. What we do instead is [pillar description], so you can [benefit] and [benefit]. What stands out to you about that?” Use for a foundational step and connect it to the prospect's issue.
- Coach, pillar 2: “Now that we have taken care of [pillar 1] you’ve already got [benefit] and are ready for our second phase, [pillar 2 name]. Here we’ve noticed after [proof] that most people need [description]. So that’s why we do [solution] so you can get [result]. What questions do you have about that?”
- Coach, pillar 3: “Now at this point in time you’re finally ready for [pillar 3]. Here, where we noticed people go wrong is once they have [pillar 1] and [pillar 2] set up they stop… This means they reach a ceiling they can’t get past. So what we do instead is [final mechanism]. This is what allowed one of my clients to [stop doing painful thing] and start [doing things they want], which ultimately resulted in [benefit]. Does that make sense?” Only use a true case result in the proof slot.
- Coach, delivery: “Now how this all works is first we will get you set up with [first deliverable]. This lets you get results right out of the gate. Now you also get [second deliverable] which means you will never get lost and will always have the [support] you need.” Use to answer how participation works, without listing every feature.
- Coach, dialogue: “What stands out to you about that?” or “What questions do you have about that pillar?” Ask after each section, not only at the end.

## 3. Objection Handling (Stage: Launch Convert)
Source: notes/2-launch-convert-3-objection-handling.md

- Salesperson, after “I need to sleep on it”: “100% my man. I definitely want you to think about this. I’ve actually noticed all our best clients are the ones who don’t rush into a decision. Now, what precisely do you need to think about that I can provide you more clarity on?” Use to acknowledge and diagnose, not to end the call.
- Salesperson, after an initial concern: “Okay, what else?” Use until the prospect identifies the other barriers.
- Salesperson, to isolate budget: “So if you had the money in your hand right now, you would 100% be ready to rock and roll?” Use after clarifying that money is the concern.
- Salesperson, collaborative problem solving: “How can we make this work for you?” Use after the prospect confirms the offer is a fit and money is the remaining obstacle.
- Salesperson, before installments: “Is it the full amount that's the hang up? Would it help if we looked at a payment plan?” Use after prioritizing payment in full.
- Salesperson, on spouse/partner concerns: “What questions would they potentially have for me? Would it be about the process, the price, or something else?” Use to surface concerns rather than speculate.
- Salesperson, on timing: “If we took time off the table, what else would hold you back from joining something like this right now?” Use to test whether time is the sole issue.
- Salesperson, to book a follow-up: “When do you think you would both be free to go over this?” Use to schedule a decision conversation with the other decision-maker present.

## Program Outline Training (Stage: Launch Deliver)
Source: notes/2-launch-deliver-1-program-outline.md

- **Creator, while extracting phases:** "What was the first fundamental quick win I needed to have/do in order to be ready for the transformation?" Use this to find the participant's first milestone.
- **Creator, while ordering milestones:** "What was the most important problem I needed to solve/achieve to make progress?" Then ask, "What was the next thing that ramped things up?" Use these prompts to keep the roadmap sequential.
- **Creator, while editing:** "What can I remove and the person will still be able to get the transformation?" Apply this to every phase or proposed lesson.
- **Worksheet format:** `[Name's] Program Outline`, followed by Phase, Modules, and Action columns. Phase prompts include `[Fundamental quick win they need first]`, `[most important milestone to make progress]`, and `[the core milestone that ramped things up]`. Fill phase names and descriptions first; do not fill in the later columns during this assignment.
- **Synthesizer example descriptions:** Business Backbone: "Crystal clear on business fundamentals (niche, offer, etc.)" Rapid Launch Protocol: "Prove your offer by getting the first 1-5 sales." Attraction Systems: "Dream clients know who you are. Tons of eyeballs." Conversion Systems: "Consistently converting eyeballs into clients." Delivery Systems: "Deliver results to clients at scale." Bonus Trainings: "Remove roadblocks as they come up."

Source: notes/2-launch-deliver-2-onboarding.md

- **Provider, connecting personal story to mission:** "And now it's my mission to help other [niche] get [dream outcome], too." Fill the brackets with the provider's audience and outcome. The example follows a story of prior struggle, the change that produced results, and the mission to help others.
- **Provider, one-to-one onboarding:** "What do you expect from working together?" Ask after explaining the purpose of the call, then record the client's words.
- **Provider, one-to-one onboarding:** "What would make this incredible for you?" Use to learn the client's criteria for a successful engagement.
- **Provider, co-writing a mission:** "What's your mission? If you had to put it in one sentence, what's your mission for this program?" Follow with a question about where the client wants to be at the end, then help them write a concise statement.
- **Provider, closing onboarding:** "I commit to following the program." Ask the client to say or write this, or adapt the wording to the program's commitment, as the final momentum step.
- **Provider, clarifying support escalation:** Go through the program first, attend a call if stuck, post in the community if still stuck, search for an existing answer, then send a personal message if needed. This is the sequence described for the Synthesizer community.

## Build as You Go Training (Stage: Launch Deliver)
Source: notes/2-launch-deliver-3-build-as-you-go.md

- **Program creator, while designing the next phase:** "What action steps does someone have to take in order to progress?" Use this before writing training content.
- **Program creator, deriving a module:** "What do they need to do?" Then ask what they need to know to take that action. Use the answer to decide the module, not to expand the program with extra topics.
- **Program creator, planning the next delivery week:** Identify what participants asked during the current week and add recurring answers as short FAQ guidance or training notes in the next week's materials.
- **Google Doc module template:** Title with the module name; add training with headings; place the action steps at the end. Record a walkthrough of the document and repeat for later modules.
- **Client-facing weekly delivery sequence:** Present the phase on a live call, share prerecorded training for the modules, then assign specific actions. The source provides this as a process, not a verbatim script.

## 2.1 Launch Prep Call + Worksheet (Stage: Launch Prep)
Source: notes/6-founding-cohort-2-1-launch-prep-call-worksheet.md

I'm going to ask you a few questions about what you need, what you're working on, what your goals are, and then at the end, if I think we're going to be a good fit, we'll get you rebooked into the official strategy call... There's no offer that's going to be made on today's call.” Use to explain a short discovery call and schedule a later sales conversation.
- **Coach, transition existing clients to cohort:** Explain the new cohort as a better or more valuable format, offer existing one-to-one clients a free transition in thanks for their prior investment, and say it replaces the old delivery model. ## Synthesizer Scaling Sales Script (Stage: Launch Convert)
Source: notes/sales-script-template.md

- Salesperson, opening: “Hey [name], great to connect! Where are you calling from?” Use for brief rapport.
- Salesperson, framing: “I’ll ask you a couple of questions around where you’re at, what you’re working on, and where you want to go. Then if it sounds like we’re a good fit, I can walk you through what it looks like to work together and at the end you can decide if you want to be part of it or not! Sound good?” Use after checking note-taking.
- Salesperson, motivation: “What got you interested in booking this call in the first place?” For a DM lead, mention the prior chat but ask again.
- Salesperson, vague answer: “When you say ___, what do you mean exactly?” Use to get specifics.
- Salesperson, goal: “What is your 12 month goal for the [thing]?” Use after current-state and problem questions.
- Salesperson, inaction: “What happens if nothing changes over the next 6 months?” Use to explore consequences.
- Salesperson, transition: “Before we potentially move on, what else should I know about you or your situation?” Use before summarizing fit.
- Salesperson, confidence: “What would it take to make this a 10 for you?” Use if their score is below 10.
- Salesperson, objection clarification: “When you say ___, what exactly do you mean by that?” Ask before explaining a feature.
- Salesperson, yes/close: “I’ll send over a custom payment link in the chat of this call. Then we can get you immediately inside so we can take action on [next step] right away.” Use after a yes.
