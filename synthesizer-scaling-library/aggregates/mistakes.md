# Mistakes, warnings and do-not rules, aggregated by source

Each block is copied from the distilled notes for one call or lesson. Source notes file is named in each heading; open it for full context.

## Discovery Training (Stage: Launch Convert)
Source: notes/2-launch-convert-1-discovery.md

- Do not use a rigid sequence of complex, programmed questions. The script is a set of checkpoints that guides a natural conversation.
- Do not avoid pain questions merely because they feel awkward, but do not turn the call into an interrogation. The lesson advocates an inquisitive, gentle tone and says not to take pain agitation to an extreme.
- Do not read the script word for word or treat showing up as entitlement to a sale. Read it, visualize the conversation, rehearse, role-play, and take calls until the checkpoints feel natural.
- Do not focus on closing before you understand the gap. Accurate diagnosis is the purpose of this part of the call.
- The instructor uses forceful metaphors about intensifying pain, including making a prospect cry. This is the instructor's framing, not a requirement to pressure someone or invent distress; the same lesson also calls for authentic dialogue and a supportive tone.

## Pitch Training (Stage: Launch Convert)
Source: notes/2-launch-convert-2-pitch.md

- Do not confuse the prospect with a long or complicated pitch. The lesson says confusion kills sales and recommends simple, short, easy-to-understand language.
- Do not use fancy mechanism names as a substitute for a distinct process. Use names that explain what the offer does.
- Do not rely on pitch slides. Be able to explain the offer without screen sharing.
- Do not focus on features instead of benefits and results. Explain what a deliverable enables the client to do.
- Do not turn the pitch into a monologue. Make it a conversation and check the prospect's response after pillars.
- Do not state a vague, non-tangible promise or make unsupported proof claims. The training calls for specific results, timeframes, and proof attached to each pillar.
- Do not deliver the same word-for-word pitch to everyone. Customize based on the prospect's current situation and stated goal, while preserving the offer's actual scope.

## 3. Objection Handling (Stage: Launch Convert)
Source: notes/2-launch-convert-3-objection-handling.md

- Do not wait until price to discover objections, agree to a vague promise to reconnect, or answer a feature question without exploring the concern behind it.
- Do not argue with or shame the prospect. Acknowledge and clarify before responding.
- Do not assume one concern is the only concern. Ask “What else?” and verify the objection is isolated.
- Do not jump to installments before handling other objections and confirming that full payment is the remaining barrier.
- Do not invent price anchors, scarcity, guarantees, outcomes, or terms. Use an authentic anchor and only guarantees the business offers.
- Do not leave a follow-up vague. BAMFAM means choosing a specific time and sending the calendar invite.
- The transcript encourages strong persuasion and describes objection handling as “regaining control,” but gives no boundary for a poor-fit prospect or someone who cannot safely afford the offer. (source limitation)
