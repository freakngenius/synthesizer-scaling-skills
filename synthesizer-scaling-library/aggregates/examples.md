## Client attraction call on entry requirements and fit (Stage: Attract)
Source: notes/attraction-calls-uploaded-transcript-2-school-games-recap-hormozi-skool-communit.md

- Synthesizer Mastermind required either 10,000 subscribers or $10,000 monthly income. When launched to a Synthesizer audience of about 15,000, the presenters realized 95% to 98% of that audience did not meet the requirement. They had to find prospects elsewhere to fill the program. They use this as a warning against setting a threshold too high.
- A member changed her content examples to business owners earning $10,000 who wanted $20,000, or $30,000 aiming for $50,000. She said new paid-community members began arriving in those categories. The presenters describe this as a “quiet barrier” created by messaging rather than a written rule.
- A member serving women entrepreneurs who had tried multiple approaches but remained stuck asked whether to use a specific sales threshold or a broader transformation criterion. The advice was to use the “achieved a valuable outcome” standard that Scaling uses, then refine after launching.
- Lucas had previously disliked high-ticket work because an earlier business had drained him. The coach sold him on resolving uncertainty about what to sell and building the right systems, rather than promising high ticket. They found his primary bottleneck was the product he fulfilled. He later reported $50,000 in the last seven days, though the transcript's timing and attribution details are not further specified.
- A strength-training consultant had spent over 10 years building recognition in a specialized methodology and estimated the market at under 5,000 studios, with about 800 contacts from a network audit. The presenters advised serving that niche deeply before testing adjacent markets. They cited another entrepreneur who thought a niche was saturated after making about $80 million, illustrating that market exhaustion can take far longer than expected.

## Post-launch scaling: call targets, outreach, and the weekly routine (Stage: Attract)
Source: notes/attraction-calls-uploaded-transcript-3-math-to-hit-your-goal-early-access-traini.md

- **Sulav:** Reported sending up to 400 to 500 new DMs per Instagram account, using at least four accounts and doing thousands of messages in a day when follow-ups were included. He said he got about four clients in the first launch week, had over 11 clients later, and charged $5,000.
- **Price and call volume:** The calculator comparison used $100,000/month at $5,800, requiring 72 booked calls, and $10,000/month at $1,000, requiring 42. The higher goal required less than twice the calls.
- **Close rates:** A member reported 60% on warm leads. The presenter says warm launch prospects can close higher, then rates tend to fall as traffic expands. He calls 30% good for large sales teams. These are observations, not promises.
- **Presenter's early business:** The presenter says he had no revenue for over six months while waiting for content to bring buyers. After following the listed actions, he made a first sale in month one and reached $10,000/month in under three months.
- **Synthesizer relaunch:** After improving delivery and raising its price, the presenters say close rate rose and the relaunch needed one launch post, compared with many posts in the first launch. Exact price and rates were not given.
- **Community growth:** A presenter says the Skool community grew from about 4,000 to 6,000 members to 32,000 in roughly a year and a half after focusing on the recurring routine.
- **Member's GIF outreach:** A member used GIFs and a mystery-box interaction to keep a prospect curious; the prospect joined after initially delaying.

## Positioning: become known for a specific outcome (Stage: Attract)
Source: notes/attraction-calls-uploaded-transcript-5-strategy-vs-tactics-and-positioning.md

- **Ski coach:** The presenter describes a ski academy whose coach uploaded two YouTube videos with 75,000 and 42,000 views. The coach was already known as a good ski coach, so the videos got attention despite imperfect titles and thumbnails. He won the Skool Games; the presenter did not know the academy's exact scale.
- **Presenter's position and proof:** He narrowed from business and online education to helping educational creators monetize on Skool. Evidence cited includes 650,000 subscribers, first Skool investor, hosting the Hormozi Skool Games, Jenny Hoyos earning $36,000 in seven days, Hamza reaching $200,000 (measure unclear), work with MrBeast, and Brian Doris growing from zero to 100,000 subscribers. In a 100-person Hormozi cohort, average members added 15,373 monthly over 90 days; currency unstated.
- **Bass guitarist:** A member estimated he was in the top 10% as a bass player, had worked professionally, and did not expect to be the best technical player. His positioning idea was helping people diagnose the next step in playing bass and sounding good in church.
- **Ultimate frisbee creator:** A member had around 11,000 Instagram followers, many younger fans asking for basic lessons. He preferred mindset, nutrition, and preparation. The presenter suggested targeting more advanced players, who may pay more but are fewer, and selling improvement at ultimate frisbee while using mindset as part of the method.
- **Glamping developer:** A member said he was not the best operator, but could teach landowners to turn property into revenue-generating assets.
- **Fundraising member:** A participant said his fundraising work produced more than $1 million from one client, but withheld the exact figure for tax reasons. He described examining the skills and resources behind that result.
- **Offer-making before choosing a niche:** The presenter describes trying an agency that trained people in Uganda to do cold email, then making progress after shifting to offers, including writing coaching, appointment setting, and ghostwriting.
- **Time Theory:** The presenter says he closed the business because helping people beat procrastination did not fit his interests; educational-creator and Skool work fit better.

## Synthesizer Scaling Sales Script (Stage: Launch Convert)
Source: notes/sales-script-template.md

This is a fill-in template, not a transcript of a member's call. It uses placeholders for proof, client results, mechanisms, delivery, and outcomes; users must supply their own evidence. The investment example uses an anchor of 1.5 times the amount and mentions a limited group of five, but these are editable sample words, not a universal price or confirmed offer. Later guarantee examples include a money-back period and a high-ticket-client target. They are examples to replace with the seller's actual terms.
