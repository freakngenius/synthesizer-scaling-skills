# 4.1 Launch Convert Call + Worksheet
Type: worksheet   Stage: Launch Convert   Length/size: 301 lines, 2,408 source words
## One-paragraph summary
This launch-convert resource is a sales-call script and checklist, covering preparation, framing, discovery, pitch, delivery, commitment, investment, and objections. The seller researches and records the call, diagnoses fit, and asks about the prospect's situation, obstacles, goals, and cost of inaction. The pitch presents a tailored three-step system and deliverables, checks confidence, then states investment. Sample objections cover budget, partner approval, hesitation, timing, and guarantees. The file contains placeholders, not actual offer terms, pricing, or case studies.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Pre-call prep:** Tidy appearance and background, choose a quiet room, use headphones, print the script and pen, review the prospect's application and social profiles, record the call, and take six physiological sighs. Release attachment to closing and diagnose accurately. Join on time; target 45 minutes, within a 30- to 60-minute range.
- **Call frame:** Brief small talk, establish time constraints, check for note-taking, and explain the agenda. Explore situation and goals first, then explain the service if it appears to fit; the prospect decides whether to join.
- **Discovery sequence:** A. Motivation: why they booked. B. Current situation: work, offer and pricing, current customer acquisition or other relevant process, systems, and satisfaction. C. Pain: main challenge, why it remains unsolved, prior attempts, and duration. D. Desired situation: 12-month goal, motivation, impact on other life areas, and what would change if the goal were reached. E. Cost of inaction: impact of the problem, what happens if nothing changes over six months, and costs in time, energy, or money. Confirm commitment and invite anything else important before pitching.
- **Diagnosis-to-pitch transition:** Summarize the prospect's current state and goal, check the summary, and connect fit to reasons grounded in what they said and the seller's proof. Ask permission to explain the service.
- **Three-pillar pitch:** Describe a customizable three-step system that takes someone from current situation to desired result in a stated timeframe. Ask them to write down the one to three pillars. For each pillar, explain the common ineffective approach and resulting problem, what the program does instead, and the benefits. Include relevant proof and check understanding or questions.
- **Delivery explanation:** Describe three deliverables and how each gets the client started, provides support, or supports continued progress. Ask what stands out and which part feels most compatible.
- **Commitment and objection sequence:** Ask the prospect to rate confidence from 1 to 10, why the rating is not lower, and what would make it a 10. If they raise a concern, acknowledge it, ask what they mean, clarify the actual objection, respond to that specific issue, then check whether the answer helped. If they do not say yes, identify and address the remaining objection rather than ending with an unclear maybe.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Before the call, review the application and relevant social profiles; set up a quiet room, headphones, printed script and pen, and call recording. The checklist recommends six physiological sighs: inhale, hold, add a sharp nasal inhale, then exhale slowly through the mouth.
2. Join on time. Open warmly, establish the time available, check that the prospect can take notes, and explain the call structure before discovery. Ask why they booked, including when they first contacted you in DMs.
3. Discover motivation, current work and offer, pricing, current systems, biggest challenge, prior attempts, how long the problem has lasted, and 12-month goal. Ask why the goal matters, what changes if they reach it, what happens if nothing changes in six months, and what the problem costs in time, energy, or money. Confirm commitment and ask what else matters.
4. If an answer is vague, ask what they mean or invite more detail. Before pitching, summarize their current situation and goal, confirm your understanding, state why you believe you can help using their answers and relevant proof, then ask permission to explain.
5. Present a customized three-pillar system and timeframe. Ask the prospect to note one to three points. For each pillar, describe the ineffective approach and its problem, what the program does instead, proof where relevant, and benefits. Explain the actual deliverables and what each enables.
6. Ask what stands out and which part feels compatible. Check confidence on a 1-10 scale, why the rating is not lower, and what would make it a 10. Address concerns before stating investment.
7. The template anchors a notional usual price at 1.5 times the offer price, then offers an introductory price to the next five or another specified number of customers. It suggests giving a truthful reason such as gathering case studies. After saying the price, stop talking and maintain confident eye contact rather than justifying it.
8. If the prospect says yes, send a custom payment link in the call chat and guide them to immediate access and next steps. Otherwise ask what is holding them back, then “What else?” until the concern is isolated. Acknowledge it, clarify what they mean, answer that concern, and check whether your answer helped.
9. For budget objections, ask what else would prevent a yes if money were not an issue, whether they would join if they had the money, why they want the result, and how acting earlier might have changed their situation. Ask how to make it work. If the upfront amount is the barrier, ask whether a payment plan would help and propose actual terms; no split is prescribed.
10. For partner approval, include the partner in the call or rebook when both can attend. For “I need to think,” ask what feels least compatible or what information is missing; if needed, ask how long and book a follow-up by sending a calendar link. For timing concerns, ask what makes the timing wrong, what else holds them back if time is removed, and how much time they will invest.
11. If hesitation remains, test whether a guarantee would let them act today and confirm readiness before presenting it. State the actual terms. The example is 100% money back within 30 days, but policy is not defined.
12. Call length is 45 minutes by default, with 30 to 60 minutes as the stated range. The discovery script refers to a 12-month goal and six-month inaction horizon.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Seller, call frame:** “I’ll ask you a couple of questions around where you’re at, what you’re working on, and where you want to go. Then if it sounds like we’re a good fit, I can walk you through what it looks like to work together and at the end you can decide if you want to be part of it or not! Sound good?” Use after greeting to set expectations.
- **Seller, motivation:** “What got you interested in booking this call in the first place?” Ask even if the prospect first contacted you in DMs.
- **Seller, discovery:** “What do you think is the biggest challenge right now?” “What have you tried already?” “What is your 12 month goal for the [thing]?” Use to explore problem, prior attempts, and desired state.
- **Seller, cost of inaction:** “What happens if nothing changes over the next 6 months?” “What is that costing you in time, energy, or money?” Use to establish the prospect's view of impact.
- **Seller, transition to pitch:** “Based on everything you’ve told me I 100% believe we can help you because [reasons based on their answers and your proof]. Would you like me to walk you through what we do and how it works?” Use after summarizing discovery and checking fit.
- **Seller, confidence check:** “On a scale of 1-10… where would you sit in terms of confidence on that right now?” Follow with “Why not a 1?” and “What would it take to make this a 10 for you?” Use after explaining the offer.
- **Seller, price:** “The investment is only [$X].” The template suggests a price anchor and limited introductory spots beforehand, then silence instead of justification.
- **Seller, isolate objection:** “What is potentially holding you back from joining something like this right now?” Follow with “What else?” until the issue is clear.
- **Seller, payment plan:** “Is it the full amount that's the hang up? Would it help if we looked at a payment plan?” Use if the buyer identifies upfront cost as the barrier.
- **Seller, schedule follow-up:** “How long do you think you need to think about this?” Then send a calendar link and book a time.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- The seller is told to release attachment to closing and diagnose accurately; avoid beginning discovery with a pitch.
- Do not proceed with a canned pitch without tying it to the prospect's situation, desired outcome, and relevant proof. Ask permission before presenting it.
- The script says not to accept an unclear answer between yes and no; clarify what is preventing a decision and identify the actual objection.
- The objection templates include forceful language: they describe “I need to think about it” as a possible smokescreen, invoke past hesitation, and contrast repeating past mistakes with investing. The source also suggests a payment plan and guarantee. These are the script's recommended responses, not measured outcomes.
- The partner guidance says to involve the partner early or reschedule, and says the objection may be a smoke screen. Avoid claiming the prospect has authority to buy until the relevant decision-maker is included.
- The script's introductory discount, anchor, payment plan, and guarantee fields must be filled with actual terms. The source does not provide standard pricing, discount limits, payment schedules, or guarantee policy.
