## One-paragraph summary
This call prepares members who completed the Business Backbone and Launch Prep to run the Attract week of a launch. The focus is booking qualified sales calls through tailored launch posts and direct conversations. Members are asked to prepare 11 posts, adapt them to their business and platforms, reach out, and book calls. For people new to sales calls, the proposed sequence is to focus on booking this week, study the sales process, and begin taking calls the following week, while continuing to sell throughout the launch. The coach also gives a live teaser-post exercise and answers questions about prospects requesting one-off help, adapting posts for different platforms, manual payment renewals, and a potentially high-upside unpaid opportunity.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Launch sequence:** Complete Backbone and Launch Prep, then Attract to generate leads and book calls, Convert to make offers and enroll clients, and Deliver results. Launch action can clarify the audience, offer, and outcome, so do not wait for perfect certainty before proceeding.
- **“Cook balls” means “book calls”:** The joke points to the main operating metric. Posts, outreach, and conversations should lead toward qualified sales calls. Qualified sales calls are the key scaling metric.
- **Attract-week focus:** Create launch content, send outreach, talk with prospects, and book as many calls as possible. Members new to sales calls are advised to focus on booking this week, then study the sales process and take calls the next week. This sequence supports focus; it is not an instruction to stop selling during the launch.
- **Eleven launch posts:** The posts should build beliefs that support a purchase: make the audience feel understood, show the desired outcome, establish proof, and make the opportunity feel timely. Examples include problem resonance, Hero’s Journey, proof, mechanism, program-live, and how-to posts. The shared drafts are tailored to each member’s Backbone, but members should still correct and adapt them.
- **New-opportunity offer:** Present the offer as a new opportunity or vehicle for the desired result, rather than merely an improvement to something the audience has already tried. The “100 competition” was a new challenge and frame around similar program content; the coach said it felt distinct and drew applications.
- **Learning or earning:** A call or pitch can teach a repeatable skill even if it does not immediately generate a sale. Compare progress with your own starting point.
- **High-upside strategic exception:** The coach supported Sunny’s unpaid certification work because it could provide access to a global CDO network and a later hosting role. He described the potential as 10 to 100 times the current state, while warning that the opportunity might fall through. He also supported continuing Sunny’s own offer and outreach. This was a case-specific judgment, not a general free-work rule.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. **Choose a specific call goal.** Set a number for the length of the launch, whether two, four, or six weeks. Members named individual goals from 10 to 182 calls; these were personal targets, not universal quotas.
2. **Prepare and adapt the 11 posts.** Go through Launch training and find the drafts tailored to your Backbone. Search the shared document for your name. Expand or correct drafts using what you know about your business, offer, and audience.
3. **Budget for writing.** The coach estimates three to four hours per post, or 33 to 44 hours total for 11 posts. He recommends studying 10 to 20 large launches and identifying psychological triggers to emulate, starting immediately.
4. **Book calls during Attract week.** Use the appointment-setting script in Launch Attract / Launch Chats. Send outreach, have conversations, and book calls. Get support from the community and attend program calls. Move into Launch Convert training as soon as possible to learn to pitch and sign clients.
5. **Publish a teaser post.** Post on a platform where you are active, such as LinkedIn, Instagram, or Skool. The template is below. It is meant to create energy and curiosity. In the live exercise the coach asked members to post, put “done” in the chat, and share the link; he said he would wait for at least 10 confirmations.
6. **Adapt content to the channel.** For Instagram, use text as a script or prompt for a Reel, or turn it into image slides. LinkedIn can take the text directly. Adapt for TikTok; format email as email. Expand a Hero’s Journey post into a longer YouTube video by riffing on its points.
7. **Respond to one-off requests with a fit call.** If someone asks for a one-time consult but the intended offer is a three-month high-ticket program, invite them to a free call to see how you can help. If there is a fit, present the high-ticket solution through the sales process. Do not add a mid-ticket product merely because a prospect asks for another format; the coach says prospects may not know which solution will produce results.
8. **Renew a three-month plan manually.** The call says Skool did not then offer quarterly recurring payments as a subtype. Near the end of the term, ask whether the client wants to continue, then manually change or extend the plan. Monthly, annual, another three-month term, upgrades, and downgrades were mentioned. A Loom explaining the change may exist.
9. **Assess unusual unpaid opportunities individually.** Sunny described an unpaid foundation role running a worldwide CDO certification, potential access to CDOs, and a possible hosting role when certification went live in one or two years. The coach recommended pursuing it while recognizing that it could fall through.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not delay publishing until the copy, timing, or launch feels perfect. The coach says he delayed an early post for five days and still found an error after publishing.
- Do not treat the team’s tailored drafts as final or assume they know the member’s business better. Adapt the posts.
- Do not try to master booking and unfamiliar sales calls at the same time if doing both prevents focus. Use the suggested sequence, then continue selling as the launch proceeds.
- Do not build a new offer just because prospects ask for a different format. Diagnose what result they need and present the solution designed to get it.
- Do not compare progress as if members have the same starting point. Earlier experience and compounding differ.
- Do not generalize Sunny’s unpaid opportunity into a rule to work for free. The endorsement depended on unusually large possible access and upside, and the coach acknowledged uncertainty.
