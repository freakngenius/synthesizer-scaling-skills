## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- Ruthless focus on the current stage: finish the Business Backbone before moving on if it is incomplete. Complete Launch Prep, then focus on booking calls. Begin conversion as suitable calls arrive, but keep booking. 
- Launch call targets: choose one goal for the entire launch, not a weekly quota. The call presents 10 calls as beginner, 30 qualified calls as intermediate, and 100 calls as advanced. Choose based on stage and capacity. Research calls do not count toward these sales-call targets.
- 11-post launch sequence: use the custom posts prepared from the Business Backbone. The sequence builds understanding and belief, presents the premium offer, and includes calls to action. Copy them or adjust wording to fit the member. Post in order, but not necessarily on consecutive days; the sequence can take a month or longer.
- New opportunity framing: avoid opening by criticizing the prospect or saying they need to improve. Explain why earlier approaches did not produce the result, then present a new mechanism or opportunity and connect it to the desired outcome. Position the coach alongside the prospect in solving the issue.
- Research calls and the accordion method: when unsure about niche or offer, speak with people across potential audiences before building a product or narrowing. Use their responses to identify whom you can help and refine the offer. The speaker started broad across athletes, business professionals, and online entrepreneurs, then narrowed after research calls.
- Research call to sales call: honor the promise used to book the call. If framed as research or low-stakes discovery, do not suddenly pitch. Thank the person, reflect the need, then invite them to a separate sales call.
- Offer pillars: explain three parts of delivery as a roadmap to the dream outcome. Examples: fitness, exercise/movement, nutrition, accountability; peak performance, education, tools, personalization; Synthesizer Scaling, Business Backbone, Rapid Launch Protocol, Scaling Systems. Show how each pillar contributes to the outcome, not just a list of modules.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Finish the Business Backbone and Launch Prep. If research reveals a mismatch, update the Backbone before a full launch. Ask for review in a Skool community post tagging Matt and Aidan, or attend the weekly Monday Business Backbone session.
2. Set a full-launch call target: 10, 30 qualified, or 100 calls. The trainer recommends a seven-day initial focus on booking, not stopping attraction after seven days. Research calls are separate; invite suitable research participants to a sales call.
3. Prepare the 11 custom posts and follow the Launch Attract classroom training. The speaker estimates independent writing could take 3 to 4 hours per post, 33 to 44 hours total, and recommends studying 10 to 20 major launches for persuasion triggers. The program supplied custom posts and sales scripts based on each member's Backbone.
4. Publish posts in sequence on relevant channels: Facebook, Instagram, YouTube Shorts, email, and Skool broadcasts are mentioned. Use familiar formats such as talking-head video, carousel, story, image with caption, or text. Keep creative simple; the call suggests five to six carousel panels.
5. Post no more than once per day, or every other day / two to three days apart. The sequence may take 30 days or longer. Repurpose across channels if bandwidth allows.
6. Follow engagement with outreach to book calls. Use the actual link in a copied post, not a placeholder such as “[link].” The custom sequence already includes calls to action.
7. Use warm contacts, including past/current clients and people previously spoken with, especially before a break when the member cannot take calls. The speaker suggests contacting warm people first and setting expectations about the program's actual start date; then schedule public posts around availability.
8. Base revisions on what research participants say. Summarize research, update the Backbone, and ask the team for new posts or a sales script. The cited call counts are examples, not requirements.
9. For a price question in DMs, acknowledge it, say the price depends on support needed, and invite a call. If the person keeps asking, provide a range or price transparently. The range given is $2,000 to $20,000. Do not offer a credit immediately; if a $37/month member objects to a $1,000 offer, one suggested response is to credit three months, making the balance $900.
10. At the end of a research call, invite a separate meeting by offering two specific times. Video is preferred for recording and transcripts; phone and in-person calls are acceptable. Use a simple background.
11. For a cohort, set a shared close date and start date when practical, then run a group kickoff/onboarding. See Launch Deliver training for implementation details.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- Coach, first teaser post: “I’m working on an exciting new project.” Use as a “whisper” post before the full launch sequence.
- Coach, price question in DM: “That’s a great question. It depends on the level of support you need. Would it make sense to jump on a quick call and I can break things down?” If pressed, share the actual price or range.
- Coach, end of research call: “Thank you so much for the info. I’m really confident that I can help you.” Then ask, “This Thursday at noon work or Friday at one o’clock, which of those is better?”
- Coach, new opportunity frame: “Every diet that you’ve tried before didn’t work due to X, Y and Z. It’s not surprising why you didn’t get the result, but I have this new vehicle, this new outcome that will get you to that six pack.” Adapt the structure to the member's mechanism and outcome.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not skip the Business Backbone or rely on internal speculation when research calls can test the offer.
- Do not treat the seven-day initial attraction focus as the full launch. Keep booking calls while taking sales calls; the 11-post sequence can span 30 days or longer.
- Do not post and wait passively. Follow engagement with outreach.
- Do not pitch on a call booked as research or low-stakes discovery. Invite a separate sales call.
- Do not overproduce content or use AI creative that looks fake. Match the member's usual style and avoid crowded carousels or distracting backgrounds.
- Do not claim scarcity, limited capacity, or a start date unless it reflects the actual offer. Filling a cohort by post four or five is not guaranteed.
- Do not discount before the prospect raises an objection. Do not assume someone who cannot pay now will never buy; early offers also give the coach practice.
