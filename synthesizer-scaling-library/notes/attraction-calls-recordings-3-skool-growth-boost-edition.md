# Skool Growth Boost Edition
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 2,133 lines, 16,531 words
## One-paragraph summary
The call explains Skool's newly introduced Growth Boost as an off-platform Meta ads program, then shows how community owners can improve their chances of being promoted. Skool aims to acquire members for communities and collect a recurring 30% commission on members attributed to the Skool Network, enabling it to reinvest ad revenue. The speaker's practical advice centers on making community positioning and creative clear to both people and Meta, then improving the whole funnel: ad click-through, About-page conversion, paid conversion, and member retention. He also advises against changing a free-to-high-ticket model merely because of the launch, and frames decisions about the commission around each business's bottleneck and capacity. Attribution rules and rollout details are discussed as they were understood at launch, with several points explicitly described as uncertain or subject to later updates.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Skool as a two-sided marketplace:** Supply is communities; demand is members. The speaker describes Skool's earlier effort as increasing community supply, then frames Growth Boost as a shift toward bringing members to communities.
- **Growth Boost flywheel:** Skool runs off-platform ads on Facebook, Instagram, and Threads; members join communities; Skool receives a commission on attributed transactions; it can reinvest that income in further member acquisition. Ads use a catalog of communities uploaded to Meta. Meta allocates more budget to groups whose ads show promise, rather than equally distributing promotion.
- **NOMAD About-page framework:** Niche, Outcome, Mechanism, Authority, Deliverables. Niche states whom the group helps; outcome names what they can achieve; mechanism says how; authority gives credibility; deliverables make the contents tangible. For Growth Boost cover art, the speaker prioritizes niche and outcome, with mechanism also useful where it fits.
- **Growth Boost performance funnel:** 1) Meta recognizes the intended audience from text and imagery. 2) The creative earns clicks. 3) The About page converts visitors into members. 4) The group moves some members toward paid offers. 5) Members stay long enough to produce revenue and value. The speaker's hypothesis is that strong results throughout this chain make continued ad spend more likely.
- **Platform incentive alignment:** Treat platforms as gatekeepers. A creator who delivers a good experience and supports the platform's growth has a better chance of distribution. For Skool, the speaker argues that paying commission can align the community owner's growth with Skool's ad investment.
- **Bottleneck-based decision:** Identify whether the business needs more attracted leads, has a conversion or sales-capacity constraint, or cannot deliver more clients. Growth Boost may help an attraction-constrained business; it may be less useful when sales or delivery capacity is already full.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Review the community cover image as an ad, not only as branding. State the audience and desired result in words that a cold prospect can understand. Include relevant images or recognizable logos where appropriate, because Meta analyzes both imagery and text.
2. Populate About-page image slots with assets that could work in ads. The speaker said these images were expected to be used as ad assets soon; this was a forecast during the call, not a confirmed universal feature.
3. Keep the creative, title, and About page aligned. Make the intended group explicit and ensure the About page matches the promise that earned the click. Attention without joins may lead Meta to reduce promotion.
4. Track the whole path, not just clicks: click-through, About-page join rate, conversion to paid, and retention. The call gives no numeric target for these rates.
5. Use the main search keyword in the community title if discovery search ranking matters. The speaker showed an example where adding a keyword moved a group from 76th to second, and another example where a 13-member group with the query keyword outranked a 1,900-member group without it. Treat this as a reported platform observation, not a guaranteed outcome.
6. If a cover image underperforms, update it. The speaker said updated cover art is sent to Meta again and that the system keeps testing, initially with smaller budgets and more investment when it detects promise. He described clicking one's own ad as a possible CTR nudge but explicitly said he did not know whether it worked; this is not a proven tactic.
7. Before deciding whether to enable Growth Boost, identify the actual constraint. If the business needs more prospects and can serve them, the speaker sees commission as a possible acquisition cost. If conversion capacity or delivery is full, consider turning the feature off or, when delivery constrained, moving toward cohorts.
8. For high-ticket sales, the speaker says a call-based sales process gives the owner a choice about the transaction path. Group links in posts, comments, and DMs were described as excluded from Skool Network attribution, while discovery, profiles, and suggested communities were included. He cautioned that edge cases can be complicated and attribution is last-touch based.
9. Preserve an existing funnel that is working while adding new assets. In response to a question about testimonial videos, he recommended keeping effective videos and adding images rather than removing proven material based on an untested feature.
10. When a free group has many courses, do not dump everything into the first visit. Rework a low-ticket group's design if making it free, and sequence course access so new members are not overwhelmed. Refer to the program's classroom lesson on low-ticket group design and the Start Here module.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not switch a free group to low-ticket solely because of the Growth Boost announcement. The speaker said evidence was insufficient and reiterated the program's free-to-high-ticket approach.
- Do not optimize the cover image for clicks alone. Misleading or curiosity-only imagery can attract irrelevant visitors who do not join.
- Do not remove converting case-study videos merely to make room for static images before there is evidence those images perform better.
- Do not assume every community receives equal ad spend, or that a low discovery rank prevents Meta ads. The call says communities meeting eligibility criteria could be advertised, with spend varying by performance.
- Do not assume that turning Growth Boost off leaves discovery visibility unchanged. A demo showed a warning that traffic would fall by 90% and rank from 6 to 212; this was one group's on-screen estimate, not a universal result.
- Avoid violating Meta's rules or promoting illegal or disallowed material. The call used a cannabis group as an example of content that might be removed.
- Distinguish estimates from confirmed mechanics. The call occurred at rollout; the speaker repeatedly said the feature had not fully launched and that the effect of commission on ad allocation was a thesis.
## Numbers and benchmarks (conversion rates, call counts, prices, targets, time frames, with the context for each)
- **30% recurring commission:** Described as applying to Skool Network attributed transactions, similar to an affiliate commission. The speaker said it also applies to later transactions while attribution persists. He stated the payment processing fee is additional, yielding about 32.9% total for most users.
- **Attribution categories:** Discovery browse/search, profile visits, and suggested communities were described as Skool Network. Links in group posts, comments, and DMs were described as excluded. Last-touch attribution was the stated basis.
- **Free vs. low-ticket group conversion:** Claimed averages were 20% for free groups versus 4% for low-ticket groups, in support of a free-to-high-ticket model. No underlying sample, method, or timeframe was supplied.
- **Growth Boost thresholds:** The speaker described eligibility as at least six members, excluding groups disallowed by Meta or Skool policies.
- **Opt-out example:** One displayed group had received 979 members and 3,000 visitors from Skool in 30 days; the interface warned of an immediate 90% traffic decrease and rank change from 6 to 212 if disabled.
- **Turn-off adoption:** The speaker said 92 of roughly 200,000 groups, or about 0.05%, had opted out at the time he checked on Friday.
- **Pricing example:** Increasing a $3,000 offer to $4,000 was presented as a possible 30% increase that might retain most buyers; this is the speaker's general assertion, not an observed result in the call.
