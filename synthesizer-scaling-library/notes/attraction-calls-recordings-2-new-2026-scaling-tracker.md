# NEW 2026 SCALING TRACKER! (Client Attraction Call)
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: not stated in transcript
## One-paragraph summary
The call introduces a Google Sheets scaling tracker intended to make post-launch work measurable and help coaches diagnose the current business constraint. Its core premise is to track a small number of actions that lead to calls and a small number of sales outcomes, then compare their trends rather than guessing from memory. The sheet combines daily attraction inputs with sales activity and automatically compiles daily, monthly, and yearly views. Use the measures to decide whether to focus on getting attention and starting conversations, improving show-up or close rates, or handling delivery, while continuing to prioritize whatever already works.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- The 2026 Scaling Tracker: Three sections correspond to Attract, Convert, and Deliver. The Attract section is entered manually. Convert and Deliver metrics are derived from the sales tracker. The tracker is described as a post-launch operating rhythm, or “heartbeat,” rather than a one-off launch plan.
- The 11 operating metrics: Four attraction inputs plus seven sales outcomes. Attraction inputs are content impressions/views, unique outreach conversations, Skool/community posts, and new free-community members or leads. The seven sales measures shown are calls kept, calls taken, new customers, show-up rate, close rate, cash collected, and revenue generated. The speaker presents these as a compact set that can reveal where growth is constrained.
- Bottleneck diagnosis: Review the numbers over time, compare them with the stated benchmarks, and decide which metric needs attention. If attraction inputs or calls are low, focus on Attract. If enough qualified calls happen but few show, address show-up. If calls happen and people show but few buy, review sales, the offer, and traffic quality. If calls and sales are healthy, keep the focus on the most relevant remaining constraint. Do not make conversion the focus when attraction is plainly the gap.
- Warm-to-cold prioritization: Spend outreach time first on the people most likely to book, then move to colder groups only after the warmer groups have been addressed. The call names recent non-buyers and no-shows, active community members, people engaging with posts, new members, and other leads as examples. Do not let the notifications feed set the day's priorities.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Open the shared Google Sheet, choose **File > Make a copy**, and rename the copy with the owner's name. Use the copy as the working tracker. Keep the 2026 sheet clean if starting the year; adding earlier data is possible, but the presenter prefers a fresh start.
2. Enter sales calls in the Sales Tracker tab. Use a recognizable date such as “Feb 1” so Sheets parses it as a date. Record the prospect's name, outcome (customer, no-show, or no deal), cash collected now, total revenue expected after all scheduled payments, and useful notes such as agreed payment terms or follow-up context. Cash is money collected today; revenue is the full contract value, including later installments.
3. Let the overview formulas compile results. Expand the controls at the left of the monthly overview to see daily totals; collapse them to return to a monthly/yearly view. The sheet summarizes calls and sales outcomes, and the sparklines show how metrics move over time. Compare lines such as views and calls kept to notice possible correlations; the sheet helps identify patterns but does not prove causation.
4. Enter the attraction inputs daily when possible: views or impressions; unique outreach conversations; posts across the user's free community and other Skool communities; and new members in the free group. If tracking daily was missed, enter monthly totals to restore an overview. Replace YouTube views with Instagram impressions, LinkedIn impressions, or another fitting measure when appropriate. Leads or email subscribers can stand in for new Skool members if there is no free group.
5. Keep the platform measure aligned with the user's actual channel. The presenter recommends mastering one platform before adding more. Continue a channel that already works; build another channel alongside it rather than abruptly abandoning the existing source. Combine outreach totals from multiple platforms into the one outreach field. Outreach means unique conversations across platforms, excluding customers and friends. Direct messages count as outreach.
6. If adding a metric or channel column, insert a column and copy/drag the monthly formula across all months so totals continue to calculate. Comments can be tracked if they clearly correlate with calls, but the presenter advises keeping the standard sheet simple otherwise. Comments were described as having a weaker general correlation than the selected inputs.
7. If a setter and closer are involved, give the closer access to enter sales outcomes. The setter should report daily conversations, call links sent, and calls booked. Use those three numbers to inspect the link-to-book rate and conversations-to-booking rate. A low booking rate after many links can indicate weak qualification, insufficient trust, or an unclear diagnosis. The setter should include these measures in an end-of-day report; the standard sheet does not contain all three DM measures.
8. For DM workload, clear active replies and prioritize qualified, warm leads. Track conversations, call links, and bookings daily, using a CRM, Scoot, the tracker, or a manual log. If there are many unread messages, get the replies under control, but continue opening new conversations so there is no multi-day gap in the pipeline. Calls may take a couple of days of conversation to book.
9. Compare time spent with calls produced. When unsure whether outreach or content is a better use of time, test two to three hours of focused outreach per day for a week, then compare bookings against the content-only period. Existing audience owners can prioritize content and people who engage with it; direct outreach is especially useful when there is no audience or content reach is unreliable.
10. Batch work where useful. One suggested schedule is sales calls on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, with content on Tuesday and Thursday. Another option is content in the morning and sales in the afternoon. The presenter prefers blocking by day but says to test based on personal working style. Use a timer and a fixed 30-minute daily window for community responses if notifications are consuming the day. Scoot's Focus Mode can hide notifications during deep work.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Setter, end-of-day report:** “How many conversations did you have? How many call links did you send? How many calls did you book?” Use daily to inspect setter activity and booking conversion.
- **Coach, bottleneck review:** “What is your constraint? What's your close rate?” Use when reviewing a member's tracker; read the actual totals rather than relying on estimates.
- **Creator, YouTube call to action:** “Free community about [topic] to help [audience] achieve [outcome]” and a separate link to book a call. Use both links when some viewers are ready for a call and others need more nurturing.
- **Creator, short video end screen or lower third:** “Click the link in the pinned comment to book a call.” Use when viewers miss the pinned comment or description.
## Examples and case studies (member or company, what they did, the numbers and result; only what the transcript says)
- The presenter demonstrates a sample tracker using fictional produce names. A $10,000 offer is paid in two installments; the example records $5,000 cash collected and $10,000 revenue. A no-show has zero cash and revenue. A later no-deal brings a displayed close rate from 100% to 50%. A further installment sale brings the illustrated February totals to $8,000 cash and $20,000 revenue.
- A member's January tracker shows 3,000 YouTube views, 112 outreaches, 23 Skool posts, 114 new members, 34 calls kept, 88% show-up rate, 27% close rate, $4,000 cash, and $8,000 revenue. The coach calls show-up and close rates healthy and recommends concentrating on audience growth and connections, not changing conversion.
- One Scaling launch generated $350,000 with one post, according to the call. The speaker uses this as an example of outreach's contribution, not as a guarantee that one post will produce the same result.
- A member who used four or five Instagram story sequences and several videos in January reportedly booked about 70 calls from an audience of roughly 50,000 followers interested in Japanese. The coach attributes the result to a specific, engaged audience.
## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not estimate call rates from memory when the tracker can calculate them. Do not confuse cash collected with full revenue due on payment plans.
- Do not count networking calls as sales calls. Count a qualified sales opportunity once even if it involves two calls.
- Do not add columns and complexity without a reason. Track extra metrics only when they help answer a concrete question, such as whether comments produce calls.
- Do not switch away from a working traffic source merely because a new platform is popular. Do not broaden to several platforms before mastering one.
- Do not mistake high impressions or virality for qualified audience growth. Prioritize valuable reach and leads who can benefit from the offer.
- Do not spend all day answering notifications just to clear them. Prioritize qualified prospects and conversations that can lead to calls. If a lead is not a fit, it is not a priority.
- Do not treat the sparkline as proof that one activity caused another. It is a visual cue for possible correlation.
## Numbers and benchmarks (conversion rates, call counts, prices, targets, time frames, with the context for each)
- Show-up rate: 80% to 90% is the target range. Above 80% is considered fine; below 60% is urgent; 60% to 80% calls for comparison with other constraints.
- Close rate: 20% is the stated floor, 30% or more is preferred. Below 20%, focus on sales skill, offer quality, and warmer or better-qualified traffic.
- Outreach: 100 unique conversations per day is described as a good amount. Skool/community posting: one to two posts daily across communities is a reasonable target.
- YouTube: 1,000 views per weekly video, about 5,000 per month, is described as a useful starting benchmark, not a ceiling.
- New members: Five to ten a day is a good starting level. Synthesizer is given as about 1,000 new members monthly, or roughly 33 daily. The call says 5% of new members booking is common, with 10% to 20% as a strong target when new members are contacted and nurtured.
- Kept calls: One per day is a good starting point, two is “amazing,” three can support roughly $80,000 to $100,000 monthly in the example, and six to eight per day is a full calendar. An illustrative calculation uses eight kept calls per day, a 90% show rate, 30% close rate, and a $3,000 offer to estimate $6,480 per day and $194,000 per month (using 30 days).
- Payment mix: The speaker says a typical pattern is approximately 50% paid in full and 50% on payment plans.
- Setter example: If 70% of people sent a call link book, that gives a reference point for assessing the setter's link-to-book performance. This is presented as an example, not a universal standard.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use this with a coach, educator, or consultant who has completed a launch and needs a reliable weekly or daily attraction rhythm, or with someone who cannot identify why calls or revenue are inconsistent. Before prescribing a metric, ask what offer they sell, which channel currently produces leads, whether they use a free community, how many qualified calls they book and take, how many attend and buy, whether the offer has payment plans, and whether they have a setter or closer. Also ask what records they already keep so the tracker can remain simple and avoid duplicate admin.
