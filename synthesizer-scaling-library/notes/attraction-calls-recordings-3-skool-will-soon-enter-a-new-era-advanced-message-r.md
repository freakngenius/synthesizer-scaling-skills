## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Five market-awareness levels:** Completely unaware people do not recognize a problem, so use story, symptoms, and pain points without leading with the product. Problem-aware people feel pain but do not know a solution; “kick their bruised knee” by naming the cost of staying stuck. Solution-aware people know options but not this offer; sell the unique mechanism. Product-aware people know the offer but need differentiation and proof. Most-aware people already want the offer; give them a clear CTA and a deal or deadline. Identify the one or two stages that describe most of the audience. The call says early audiences are usually problem- or solution-aware.
- **Market sophistication sequence:** First to market, make a direct claim. When competitors arrive, make a bigger, faster, or easier claim. When generic promises lose trust, introduce a new mechanism. When mechanisms multiply, prove yours. In a saturated market, differentiate through identity, belief, or a movement. Do not keep escalating claims after prospects have become skeptical.
- **Belief sequence:** Buyers need to believe (1) the problem should be solved, (2) a mechanism is the best way to solve it, and (3) this product is the best way to use that mechanism. More upstream beliefs create a broader but harder sale. Most businesses can focus on the last two. Scaling's example was “high-ticket is the best way to grow a Skool” and “Scaling is the best way to do high-ticket.”
- **Unique mechanism formula:** “Most [niche] try to achieve [outcome] through [old mechanism]. The problem with this is [problem]. The new way to [outcome] is [new mechanism]. This is because [logical explanation].” It must sound new, simple, effective, and believable.
- **Movement and outcome:** Connect a personal goal to a shared purpose. State the offer as “You pay [price], you get [outcome],” using one tangible, observable result. “Get in shape” is subjective; “bench 225 pounds,” “get a girlfriend in 90 days,” or “reach a 35% close rate” is more verifiable.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not use engagement farming or low-value posts to chase discovery rank.
- Do not design a cover image only for people already familiar with you; cold visitors must understand it.
- Do not lead with a product to an unaware audience or use a hard CTA for prospects who do not recognize the problem.
- Do not use ever-larger generic promises after the market has become skeptical. Introduce a mechanism and proof instead.
- Do not try to persuade the entire general population if two later beliefs are enough to sell the offer.
- Do not make the mechanism so novel that it becomes unbelievable. A new claim still needs a plausible explanation and evidence.
- Do not accept every call for practice indefinitely. Once call volume has supplied enough practice, protect time through qualification.
- Do not try to extract money from prospects who lack the means to buy. Offer them useful free material.
