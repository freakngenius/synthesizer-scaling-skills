# Post-launch scaling: call targets, outreach, and the weekly routine
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 1h24m

## One-paragraph summary
For members who have finished Rapid Launch, this call turns the evergreen business model into measurable call targets and a repeatable weekly routine. Members use revenue, price, close rate, and show-up rate to spot the bottleneck, then prioritize attraction, conversion, and customer delivery. The presenters add outreach as a core mechanism, recommend starting with warm leads, and urge members to execute the system consistently while using creative effort to improve the product rather than redesigning the business model.

## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Goal-to-calls math:** Enter the monthly revenue goal, offer price, close rate, and show-up assumptions in the shared calculator. It outputs the monthly booked-call target. Revisit when a goal, price, or rate changes; the sheet can also be used backward from current calls to projected revenue.
- **Constraint diagnosis:** No booked calls means attraction is the constraint; booked calls without attendance point to show-up; attended calls without sales point to conversion. The presenters use 20% close rate as a threshold: below it, work on conversion; above it, prioritize more booked calls. Closing almost everyone may mean price is low, qualification is too strict, or call volume is too low.
- **Expanded scaling system:** Attract through YouTube and Skool. Skool discovery and networking lead to a free group or directly to calls; the group also leads to calls. Add outreach as its own mechanism, especially without a large audience. Calls sell the premium offer, delivered through coaching, a cohort, or a course. Customize channels and the group type to fit the business.
- **Post-launch weekly routine:** Attract: one YouTube video weekly and Skool networking daily. Convert: run the free or low-ticket group, do outreach, and take sales calls. Deliver: improve the program weekly and do customer success daily. Skool discovery is a one-time setup task, not a weekly action. The routine is a guide; batch calls and deep program work on different days if that helps focus.
- **Outreach priority order:** Start with new group members and other inbound signals, then continue open conversations, then message people engaging with posts, and finally contact people in other communities or cold prospects. Inbound contacts have joined, messaged, or engaged, giving a natural reason for outreach. Outbound fills the remaining call-booking gap.
- **Theory of relative productivity:** Ask whether an activity is the most productive use of limited time, not merely whether it is productive. Complete the core routine before optional work such as spending hours on YouTube comments.
- **One-source rule and product focus:** Execute the program's proven attraction and delivery model instead of constantly changing channels or offer models. Put creative effort into improving the product and customer results. The presenters link better outcomes to happier customers, referrals, case studies, inbound leads, and more calls. They recommend staying with the system for 12 months rather than pivoting after early setbacks.

## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Duplicate the revenue-planning sheet with **File > Make a copy**. Enter the monthly goal and assumptions in the blue cells, read the call target, and test changes to price, close rate, and show-up rate.
2. Calculate close rate as customers won divided by calls held over the same period. Guess temporarily if tracking is missing, then begin recording it. Use the 20% threshold before deciding whether to prioritize conversion or attraction.
3. Edit the Canva system diagram to reflect the actual business: replace generic group and delivery labels, and use Instagram, LinkedIn, or another existing channel instead of YouTube where appropriate.
4. For attraction, publish one YouTube video weekly. Steps: generate and select an idea, choose title/thumbnail/hook, brain-dump, simplify to an outline, record, edit (even just trimming the beginning and end), and upload. Network in Skool daily. Three posts per week in other communities is a rough starting point; 30 to 100 comments a day is an illustrative range, adjusted to the member's circumstances.
5. For conversion, make three weekly posts in the member's free group, comment there, and run a weekly call. The presenters recommend combining that call with a YouTube video when following their training. Use a low-ticket group in place of a free one if that is the model.
6. Do 100 outreach actions daily, beginning with new group joins, then people engaging with posts/comments, then other Skool communities. The call cites a Skool limit of 10 new DMs per week; after that, use another platform such as Instagram. Personalize messages, respect limits, and protect the relationship.
7. For a detailed public comment, acknowledge the question and invite the person to a call or DM instead of delivering extensive free coaching. Ignore repeated requests for substantial unpaid help when needed.
8. Deliver through daily customer support and weekly check-ins with inactive members. Run cohort calls or one-to-one calls according to the offer. Improve the program weekly by reviewing its outline, sequence, training, and over-delivery.
9. Members still in Rapid Launch should use the Rapid Launch master tracker, not this evergreen routine. Members without clients or a group can direct the available time to outreach, content, or calls.
10. To keep sales calls within 30 to 45 minutes, state the time limit after small talk, explain the agenda, review recordings for questions that cause tangents, redirect politely, and learn the script. The presenter says he is behind if he has not reached the pitch by about minute 30.

## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Presenter, after someone likes a post:** "Hey Isaac, saw you liked my post on how Kuhn hit 10K with his piano community. Thanks for the support. Quick question. Are you currently looking to grow your own Skool, or just checking things out?" Use a specific engagement as the opener.
- **Presenter, after a new member joins:** "Hey, welcome to the community." Send promptly while the person is still engaged.
- **Presenter, after a poll vote about burnout:** "Hey, noticed you voted that you're a little overwhelmed and burned out right now. Sorry to hear that. Do you mind if I ask a couple of questions to see if I potentially could help? Maybe I have some resources I could send your way." Use the poll as the reason for starting a DM.
- **Presenter, redirecting a detailed comment:** "Wow, this is such a good question. Thank you for asking it. However, it's much too detailed to respond to in a comment. Why don't we hop on a quick call and we can talk about it in more detail." Use to invite the person into a sales conversation without giving the full answer publicly.
- **Presenter, opening a sales call:** "I know we only have 45 minutes, so let's make the most of the time. You ready to jump in?" Then explain that you will ask about their journey and goals, explain the program if there is a fit, and let them decide at the end.
- **Presenter, redirecting a tangent:** "Do you mind if I interrupt you really quick? Because I want to just make sure we stay on topic and make the most of the time." Then name the point to return to.
- **Presenter, text-expansion shortcut:** Set a shortcut such as `/call` to insert the calendar link and ask the prospect to confirm a booking. Text Blaze and SkoolCRM are named as tools.

## Examples and case studies (member or company, what they did, the numbers and result; only what the transcript says)
- **Sulav:** Reported sending up to 400 to 500 new DMs per Instagram account, using at least four accounts and doing thousands of messages in a day when follow-ups were included. He said he got about four clients in the first launch week, had over 11 clients later, and charged $5,000.
- **Price and call volume:** The calculator comparison used $100,000/month at $5,800, requiring 72 booked calls, and $10,000/month at $1,000, requiring 42. The higher goal required less than twice the calls.
- **Close rates:** A member reported 60% on warm leads. The presenter says warm launch prospects can close higher, then rates tend to fall as traffic expands. He calls 30% good for large sales teams. These are observations, not promises.
- **Presenter's early business:** The presenter says he had no revenue for over six months while waiting for content to bring buyers. After following the listed actions, he made a first sale in month one and reached $10,000/month in under three months.
- **Synthesizer relaunch:** After improving delivery and raising its price, the presenters say close rate rose and the relaunch needed one launch post, compared with many posts in the first launch. Exact price and rates were not given.
- **Community growth:** A presenter says the Skool community grew from about 4,000 to 6,000 members to 32,000 in roughly a year and a half after focusing on the recurring routine.
- **Member's GIF outreach:** A member used GIFs and a mystery-box interaction to keep a prospect curious; the prospect joined after initially delaying.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not confuse the suggested 100 daily outreach actions with sending 10 generic DMs. Keep quality and platform limits in view.
- Do not spam, send generic copy-paste messages, or use deceptive misspellings to trick people into correcting their name. Personalize and build a relationship.
- Do not spend hours on optional tasks before completing the higher-priority routine. Do not assume longer sales calls close better.
- Do not substitute this post-launch tracker for the Rapid Launch master tracker while a member is in the launch.
- Do not keep reinventing the business model instead of executing the system and improving the product.

## Numbers and benchmarks (conversion rates, call counts, prices, targets, time frames, with the context for each)
- 20% close rate is the stated threshold for deciding whether conversion is the main bottleneck; above it, book more calls. 30% is described as good for larger sales teams; a member reported 60% on warm leads.
- Routine benchmarks: one YouTube video weekly; three posts weekly in other Skool groups as a rough start; daily networking; three posts weekly in one's own group; weekly group call; 100 outreach actions daily. The earlier 100-person cohort changed its target from 100 DMs, 100 comments, and 100 content minutes to 100 minutes chatting with people daily after members made low-quality contacts.
- Skool DM limit cited: 10 new DMs per week. Sales calls: aim for 30 to 45 minutes and reach the pitch by around minute 30.
- Sulav's outreach example: up to 400 to 500 new DMs per Instagram account, at least four accounts, thousands of total messages in a day, over 11 clients later, and a $5,000 price.
- The presenters recommend a 12-month commitment. The spreadsheet example compares a $100,000 monthly goal/$5,800 offer/72 calls with a $10,000 goal/$1,000 offer/42 calls.
- Outreach's share of the last launch was not calculated. The presenter estimates it at 40% to 60%, then restates it as 40% to 69%; treat the share as uncertain.
