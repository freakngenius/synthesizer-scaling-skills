# 3. Objection Handling
Type: lesson   Stage: Launch Convert   Length/size: about 57 minutes; transcript runs to 56:29
## One-paragraph summary
This lesson teaches objection handling as a process that starts before the price is presented. The salesperson should use discovery, transition, pitch, and commitment to surface and resolve doubts early, then present the investment plainly and deal with the remaining issue. An objection is defined as a doubt or concern expressed as a reason not to act, and should be treated as an opening to learn what the prospect fears or needs clarified. The core response is to acknowledge the concern, reinforce the prospect's caution where appropriate, and ask a question that reveals the underlying objection. The instructor recommends isolating concerns one at a time, re-asking for the sale after handling each one, prioritizing payment in full, and booking a follow-up meeting from the call when the prospect cannot decide immediately.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- Sales process: pre-call preparation, intro and framing, discovery, transition, pitch, commitment, investment, then objections and close. Discover the prospect's goals, current situation, pain, desired outcome, and cost of inaction before the price stage.
- Commitment phase: a 2-5 minute dialogue after the pitch. Check confidence, conviction, and clarity, then identify remaining concerns about fit, process, support, or timing. The instructor recommends reaching an 8-10 confidence rating before stating the investment.
- Investment: state the price simply and use an authentic anchor. The anchor must be true, not a fabricated comparison or a false one-day discount. After naming the price, stop talking rather than defending it with a list of deliverables.
- Objection definition: a doubt or concern expressed as a reason not to act. Treat it as information about what the prospect fears or needs clarified, and as an opportunity to see whether the offer can help.
- AAA framework: (1) Acknowledge the concern so the prospect feels heard. (2) Add to it and associate it with buying behavior, such as thoughtful buyers taking decisions seriously. (3) Ask a clarifying question that surfaces the concern beneath the first answer. The instructor says the “add” step need not appear in every response.
- Double tap: ask again in a more precise way after the first objection. After “I need to think about it,” for example, ask if the uncertainty is about process, price, or something else.
- “What else?” and isolation: ask what other concerns remain until you have identified them all. Handle one at a time, then confirm whether the prospect would move forward if a specific barrier were removed. Do not assume the first concern is the only one.
- BAMFAM means “Book a Meeting From A Meeting.” If the prospect or decision-maker cannot decide on the call, schedule a specific follow-up, put it in the calendar, and send the invite rather than leaving a vague promise to reconnect.
- BANT categorizes common objections as budget, authority, need, and time. Use it to investigate the reason behind a stated objection before offering a solution.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. After the pitch, spend 2-5 minutes checking the prospect's view. Ask what stands out and what feels compatible. Use the 1-10 confidence scale, where 10 means ready to join. Ask “Why not a 1?” and “What would make this a 10?” Resolve concerns, then check again.
2. Before price, invite remaining questions, including next steps. The instructor's target is 8-10 confidence, ideally with price as the only open question. State the investment clearly with a truthful anchor, then stop and allow the prospect to respond.
3. If the prospect does not say yes, ask what is holding them back and keep asking “What else?” until the remaining barriers are clear. Handle each one separately and re-ask for the sale.
4. For affordability, acknowledge the concern and ask what else would prevent joining if finances were removed. If nothing else, ask whether they would be ready with the money and why they would choose the offer. Have them explain the value in their own words, consider the cost of repeating the current situation, then ask collaboratively how to make it work.
5. Keep payment in full as the first option. Only ask about a split after the prospect confirms the full amount is the remaining barrier. Establish what they can commit to. Examples include two payments or, if needed, a longer schedule such as three or six months. Do not default to installments to avoid seeming pushy.
6. For a spouse or business partner, identify the decision-maker during qualification and invite them to the call or reschedule for a time when both can attend. Ask whether the partner is supportive and what questions they may have about process, price, or another concern. If nearby, invite them into the conversation; if not, book a specific follow-up and send the invite.
7. For “I need to think,” affirm the need to decide carefully. Ask what part feels least compatible or what questions may arise after the call. If they repeat the phrase, ask what makes them think the offer could solve the problem, and whether delaying a decision about something they believe would help contributes to staying stuck. Re-ask for the sale. If they still need time after discussing the real guarantee, ask how long and book the follow-up.
8. For “not the right time,” ask what makes timing wrong, then set time aside hypothetically and ask what else would prevent joining. If time is the sole concern, discuss whether they can pay and receive access now but start later, only if the business can offer those terms.
9. If no concern resolves, ask whether a specific guarantee would let the prospect act. Confirm readiness if it existed, then describe only an actual guarantee. The transcript gives a 30-day refund and a “high ticket or bust” guarantee tied to 10 clients in six months as examples, not universal terms.
10. Practice the script aloud, in a mirror, and with other members. Treat low-fit calls as practice while still applying the process. The instructor describes spending about 30 minutes practicing objection handling on one call.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- Salesperson, after “I need to sleep on it”: “100% my man. I definitely want you to think about this. I’ve actually noticed all our best clients are the ones who don’t rush into a decision. Now, what precisely do you need to think about that I can provide you more clarity on?” Use to acknowledge and diagnose, not to end the call.
- Salesperson, after an initial concern: “Okay, what else?” Use until the prospect identifies the other barriers.
- Salesperson, to isolate budget: “So if you had the money in your hand right now, you would 100% be ready to rock and roll?” Use after clarifying that money is the concern.
- Salesperson, collaborative problem solving: “How can we make this work for you?” Use after the prospect confirms the offer is a fit and money is the remaining obstacle.
- Salesperson, before installments: “Is it the full amount that's the hang up? Would it help if we looked at a payment plan?” Use after prioritizing payment in full.
- Salesperson, on spouse/partner concerns: “What questions would they potentially have for me? Would it be about the process, the price, or something else?” Use to surface concerns rather than speculate.
- Salesperson, on timing: “If we took time off the table, what else would hold you back from joining something like this right now?” Use to test whether time is the sole issue.
- Salesperson, to book a follow-up: “When do you think you would both be free to go over this?” Use to schedule a decision conversation with the other decision-maker present.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not wait until price to discover objections, agree to a vague promise to reconnect, or answer a feature question without exploring the concern behind it.
- Do not argue with or shame the prospect. Acknowledge and clarify before responding.
- Do not assume one concern is the only concern. Ask “What else?” and verify the objection is isolated.
- Do not jump to installments before handling other objections and confirming that full payment is the remaining barrier.
- Do not invent price anchors, scarcity, guarantees, outcomes, or terms. Use an authentic anchor and only guarantees the business offers.
- Do not leave a follow-up vague. BAMFAM means choosing a specific time and sending the calendar invite.
- The transcript encourages strong persuasion and describes objection handling as “regaining control,” but gives no boundary for a poor-fit prospect or someone who cannot safely afford the offer. (source limitation)
