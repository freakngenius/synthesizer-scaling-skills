# Grow Your Skool While Working Less (OUTREACH ROASTS)
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 1,544 transcript lines; runtime not stated
## One-paragraph summary
This attraction call teaches price-increase campaigns, referral incentives, focused outreach and follow-up. The core rule is to identify the action that reliably books calls and do more of it with fewer interruptions before adding tactics. The call gives a priority order for Skool DMs, examples of simple openers and respectful follow-ups, and live reviews of outreach messages. It also explains how to manage daily work queues, use proof with colder prospects and simplify the computer and workspace.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Announced price increases:** Do not raise price quietly. Tell prospects about a real increase and recontact prior call-bookers who did not buy. Use smaller staged increases to create multiple honest campaigns rather than one large jump.
- **Incentives shape behavior:** Create rewards for different levels of participation. A top prize motivates strong referrers; an attainable reward motivates the average member; a deadline encourages quick action. The call cites Charlie Munger's phrase, “the ants go where the sugar lies.”
- **More, then better, then new:** When an input reliably produces an output, first increase and improve that input before adding a new channel. For outreach, prioritize active conversations and protect focused time.
- **Outreach queue order:** Start with recent unread DMs; people offered a call or sent a booking link; prior call-bookers and no-shows when relevant; post commenters/likers; AutoDM replies; people online now; active members; and engaged members who may fit. Track “call offered, link not sent” separately from “link sent, not booked.”
- **DM to call:** Use an easy yes/no or this/that opener. Learn the prospect's goal and bottleneck before inviting them. Connect a call to a specific outcome. For colder prospects, establish trust with relevant proof, but do not provide a full coaching plan in the DM.
- **Follow-up trade-off:** One or two follow-ups is the default, often the day after a booking link. More may produce replies but risks harming goodwill and brand. Go beyond two only for a genuine new reason or an established conversation.
- **Daily queues and focus:** Handle administrative queues, such as email, once daily when possible. Outreach is different: respond promptly to active conversations to move them toward calls. Focus through removal: turn off the phone, enable Do Not Disturb, hide distracting Skool elements, close unused apps and tabs, and work on one task at a time.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Announce a real price increase and contact interested non-buyers. Mention the prior goal or obstacle, the increase and ask whether they would consider another call. Example steps are $2,000 to $3,000, then $4,000, then $5,000, each promoted as a separate change.
2. For referrals, create tiered rewards. The free-group test spent about $30 on hats for five leads, about $6 per lead. In the paid community, referral payment was tied to a customer; in the free group, the reward was for a lead. The example included a hat for five referrals in 24 hours and a first-to-five referral prize involving a 30-minute one-on-one. Some prize wording is unclear, so confirm the actual offer before reuse.
3. Start outreach with recent unread DMs and people who have just replied. Work in batches of about five so you can return to people while they are online. Then follow up with people offered a call or sent a calendar link.
4. During a launch, contact people who previously had calls or booked but did not attend. Then contact post commenters and likers. A two-step post can offer a resource for a keyword comment; DM those who comment, confirm access, share the resource and continue the conversation.
5. Reply to AutoDM responses promptly. Save the member list sorted by last active as a bookmark, and contact relevant people online now. High community levels, such as 5 to 8, can indicate engagement but do not guarantee fit.
6. Before an outreach block, turn off the phone, enable laptop Do Not Disturb, open the relevant channel and start a timer. The speaker says 20 minutes of focused outreach can outperform two hours of distracted outreach. If unable to focus, stop and return later.
7. Use Skool's three-dot Focus setting to hide the community feed and, if needed, notifications or tags. Close unneeded apps, tabs and extensions; keep active bookmarks and a clean desktop. Identify physical distractions and adjust the workspace.
8. Maintain new conversations so the next day has active prospects. If you can manage multiple live chats, continue the replies; if not, handle one at a time and move on when it goes quiet.
9. Work backward from the user's own call and client targets. The call explicitly warns that Olga's 1,000-message example is not a quota for everyone.
10. Connect each call invitation to the prospect's named goal. With colder contacts, share evidence from a similar client before inviting them.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Outreach operator, AutoDM:** “Are you looking to grow? I might have some resources for you.” Use as a simple opener; adapt “grow” to the audience's goal.
- **Outreach operator, call permission:** “Would it be helpful to jump on a call with someone on our team?” Ask before sending a calendar link.
- **Coach, reconnecting with a past prospect:** “I saw you booked a call a couple months ago. You were interested in learning how to scale to [goal], but [obstacle]. Our price is increasing, so if you're still on the fence, would you be open to another call to see if now is a better time?” Use only with accurate context and a real increase.
- **Coach, simplicity test:** “Can they reply to this question if they were sitting on the toilet scrolling on their phone?” Use to remove friction from the first question.
- **Outreach operator, personal follow-up:** Ask a real question about a detail in the person's profile or community, such as “How did you come up with that name?” Use instead of a generic “bump.”
- **Coach, proof:** “We had someone dealing with something similar, and this is what they achieved once we helped them solve that problem.” Pair with a relevant screenshot, interview or link; do not teach the whole method in DMs.
- **Creator, two-step post:** “Comment [keyword] below and I'll send you [resource].” DM commenters to confirm they received it and continue with questions.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not announce fake scarcity or a price increase that is not real.
- Do not spend a long block on inactive contacts while people are online and replying.
- Do not send a booking link immediately after an unqualified invitation. Ask permission and connect the call to the prospect's outcome.
- Do not overload the opening DM with questions or instructions.
- Do not treat one ghosted conversation as evidence that outreach fails. Nonresponse is normal; keep volume appropriate to the goal.
- Do not automate follow-ups indiscriminately. Repeated automated messages can burn relationships.
- Do not follow up endlessly. Two is the default; continue only for a relevant reason or established relationship.
- Do not optimize conversion before there is enough volume to learn. Keep the workstation and digital setup free of distractions.
