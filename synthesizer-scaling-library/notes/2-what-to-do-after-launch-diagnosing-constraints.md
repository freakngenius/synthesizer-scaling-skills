# Diagnosing and Solving Constraints
Type: lesson   Stage: Scale   Length/size: Video transcript, 31:01, plus accompanying lesson document
## One-paragraph summary
This lesson teaches a post-launch operator to identify the single business bottleneck before changing tactics. It groups constraints into attract, convert, and deliver, then maps common symptoms to the next actions. The core operating rule is to do more and better of what already works before adding a new channel or system. Early-stage members should prioritize Skool networking, outreach, free work when needed, and follow-up; members with calls but weak closing should take more calls and practice sales; members with audience attention but few bookings should improve their funnel and remove friction; members with too many clients should protect the working acquisition routine and get support before trying to scale delivery. When uncertain, the instructor says to ask for help on implementation calls or in the community rather than guessing.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Attract, Convert, Deliver constraint map:** First locate the main bottleneck. Attract covers finding prospects and booking calls. Convert covers turning attention into booked calls and calls into customers. Deliver covers serving clients and preserving enough time for acquisition and sales. The transcript gives general diagnostic thresholds: fewer than 2 booked calls per day points to attract; a close rate below 20% points to sales conversion; getting many content views but few calls points to funnel conversion; having around 5 or more clients while delivery consumes over 50% of time points to delivery. The document separately says 10+ clients, possibly as low as 5, so the count is a rough indicator, not a hard threshold.
- **Opportunity per lead:** Improve the number of opportunities produced by each lead before trying to find a new traffic source. Follow up, respond quickly, make a clear call to action, and ensure a lead can find a suitable calendar slot. The lesson applies this to both early attraction and a weak booking funnel.
- **More, better, new:** Increase the amount of the current working activity, improve how it is performed, and add a new channel only after those changes cannot address the constraint. A slow but working source such as Instagram, YouTube, or LinkedIn is a reason to refine it, not immediately abandon it.
- **Constraint-specific action paths:** (1) No customers: work for free when necessary to earn proof, then pitch; use networking and audits. (2) Some customers but too few calls: refine current content, calls to action, and appointment setting. (3) Already using YouTube or lacking an attraction system: complete the YouTube Growth Thesis and publish one strong video each week. (4) Calls but low close rate: take more calls, practice, and check whether the offer itself is viable. (5) Attention but few bookings: improve niche specificity, tracking, calls to action, lead response, and calendar access. (6) Delivery overload: avoid adding acquisition channels and prepare for a more scalable group model only when appropriate.
- **Top-level diagnosis before root-cause analysis:** The instructor says to decide first whether the constraint sits in attract, convert, or deliver. Do not spend time guessing every small cause before identifying the broad category. Then test the matching actions and request community feedback if the diagnosis remains unclear.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Write down the largest constraint as attract, convert, or deliver. Compare call volume, close rate, content attention versus booked calls, client count, and delivery time against the rough thresholds above.
2. If there are no customers, do not start YouTube as a rescue tactic. Revisit the Skool networking training; join relevant groups, comment, start chats, and book calls. Complete the network and opportunity audits, follow up with every lead, offer free work if needed to build proof, and pitch interested people after the work. The instructor says to complete these steps before progressing to other tactics.
3. If there are one or two customers but not enough calls, continue the channel already producing some results. Add clear calls to action to a calendar and free Skool group, set notifications, and call new leads when they arrive. Attend implementation calls and post for help. Do not add YouTube unless it is already a primary attraction source.
4. If YouTube is already the channel or there is no attraction system, complete the YouTube Growth Thesis, prepare the process before filming, and commit to one strong video per week. The sequence remains more, better, then new.
5. If closing is below 20%, take more calls and accept more leads for practice, including leads that are less qualified. Rewatch sales training, record yourself reading the script, practice the script and objection handling, visualize conversations, and review old calls. Check whether the offer is being sold successfully elsewhere in the market. If you cannot tell whether the offer is the issue, post it for feedback instead of guessing.
6. If attention is high but bookings are low, narrow broad content to a specific niche problem and review the Growth Thesis training. Track which content actually books calls, not views, likes, impressions, subscribers, or followers. The lesson mentions Bitly, UTM links, and RevTracker for YouTube as tracking options. Strengthen calls to action and consider a free audit or review as the reason to book; deliver it on the call, then continue with discovery, transition, pitch, and the ordinary sales process.
7. Call new leads immediately. Remove booking friction such as hard qualifying questions, multiple steps, and email sequences. Add calendar availability, because a prospect who clicks but sees no workable time may leave. If lead follow-up is consuming too much time, bring in a setter, give the setter the script and a simple SOP, and review their conversations.
8. If delivery is taking over, do not add new channels or steps. Continue what has already produced clients, attend implementation calls, ask for help, and prepare for a group delivery model when the current model requires it. The instructor says most members are not yet at this stage.
9. Before any of these steps, finish the rapid launch if incomplete: complete network and opportunity audits, contact the network, make launch posts, and perform three follow-ups to everyone in the network.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Instructor, diagnostic prompt:** “What is your biggest bottleneck?” Use this before choosing new work.
- **Instructor, booking-call frame for a free audit:** “Do you mind if I ask you a few follow-up questions to make sure I have a good understanding of what might work best for you here and how to solve this?” Use after giving a promised free audit on a booked call, before moving into discovery and the sales conversation.
- **Instructor, setter KPI:** “respond to every DM every day, so end of the day, there’s no DMs left on the funnel.” Use as the setter’s daily operating standard. The lesson also sets a target of 10 calls booked per week.
- **Member, practice request:** “Hey guys, who wants to do a sales role play call with me?” Use as a community post when close rate is below 20% and the member needs practice.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not misdiagnose a bottleneck and spend time on activities that do not solve it.
- Do not start YouTube simply because there are no clients or calls. Do not switch away from a channel that is currently working.
- Do not optimize for views, likes, followers, or impressions when the problem is booked calls. Track the content that produces calls.
- Do not assume a weak close rate proves the offer is bad. Check sales practice, call volume, and whether the offer works elsewhere.
- Do not add qualification and booking steps when the goal is more calls. A lower qualification or close percentage may still yield more sales if total calls increase.
- Do not outsource sales as the first response. The instructor says the owner should keep selling as long as possible to retain sales skill and market understanding.
- Do not build onboarding or work through delivery training before having customers who need delivery. Ignore training that does not address the present constraint.
- Do not ask how to scale delivery before delivery is genuinely overloaded; the instructor says to wait until the current model is breaking.
- The lesson describes recruiting “young, hungry guys from third world countries” from DMs as a setter source. This is the speaker's recruitment suggestion, not a requirement or documented hiring standard.
## Numbers and benchmarks (conversion rates, call counts, prices, targets, time frames, with the context for each)
- Fewer than 2 calls booked per day: general indicator of an attract constraint.
- Close rate below 20%: general indicator of a sales-conversion constraint; the speaker advises additional calls and practice.
- Around 5 clients, or 10+ in the worksheet, with delivery over 50% of available time: delivery constraint. At 80% of time, the instructor says the business may start to backslide.
- One great YouTube video per week: suggested pace once YouTube is the appropriate attraction focus.
- Three follow-ups to everyone in the network: rapid launch completion rule.
- Setter commission: generally 5% of cash collected. The transcript mentions 5% to 10%, says 5% is the recommendation, and says 10% may be considered for offers below $5,000 or when lead flow is low.
- Setter target: 10 calls booked per week. The instructor estimates that this could yield about $1,000 per week in commissions if maintained, without detailing the assumptions.
- The lesson uses “99%” and “10x” rhetorically when describing misdiagnosis and potential growth, not as measured results.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use this with a business that has launched and needs to choose its next priority, especially when the owner is tempted to add a channel, build a funnel, hire, or redesign delivery. Before applying it, ask how many calls the user books per day or week, what share of calls close, whether content attention is turning into calls, how many clients are active, what share of work time delivery consumes, which acquisition activity already works, and whether the rapid launch tasks are complete.
