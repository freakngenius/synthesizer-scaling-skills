# How To Grow + Monetize Your Skool (Client Attraction Call)
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 1,842 transcript lines
## One-paragraph summary
This attraction call covers community relationships, lead quality, simple follow-up systems, and booked calls. It gives a post-call nurture setup, explains why small conversion samples fluctuate, and covers content, search, delivery promises, and free versus paid Skool groups.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Three levels of relationships:** Build relationships with people behind you who learn from or buy from you; people ahead of you who can teach you or open doors; and peers at a similar stage who share problems, learning, and accountability. The call emphasizes the often-neglected peer level. Make the advice operational with an immediate, small action rather than ending with the general instruction to network.
- **Simplify before automating:** Use a minimum viable process that handles the practical need without building a website, complex funnel, or automation. Improve the process only when the added work has a stronger effect than higher-leverage activities such as audience growth and delivery.
- **80/20 versus high quality:** For attraction and delivery, invest in making the system or product as good as possible. For conversion mechanics and supporting processes, use an efficient 80/20 solution when a small improvement would take time away from reaching more people. A higher volume of qualified attention can outweigh a small increase in close rate.
- **Qualified lead attraction:** Speak directly to the intended customer in content, including their characteristics and desired outcome. Let relevant prospects self-identify through engagement or a keyword response. Start outreach with the warmest, most engaged people and move toward broader, colder audiences as needed.
- **Research call versus sales process:** A research call can gather data about message resonance, niche, and offer. A second use is as the first step of a two-call sales process: discover and qualify, then book a sales call. Add investing-capacity questions only when moving into the sales funnel.
- **Customer results and product-market fit:** Track whether customers reach the promised result and identify the actions or constraints separating successful clients from those still stuck. Improve delivery before abandoning a niche that is already making sales. Promises about a result and time frame should reflect what can be delivered consistently.
- **Attract, Convert, Deliver:** The call repeatedly returns to doing more of the core growth process. Attract includes content, outreach, Skool networking, and a free group; convert is DM conversations and sales calls; deliver includes improving the program and customer success. Operations become more relevant when the business builds a team.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. **Make peer networking concrete.** During a call, give participants a minute to DM three community peers using a brief personal opener. Find people through the members tab, online list, or recent posts.
2. **Build a simple post-call nurture path.** Send booked prospects to a Skool thank-you post linking a YouTube playlist of three proof or teaching videos. Turn off comments so the post does not keep resurfacing. Add the playlist to Calendly reminder emails. If prospects cannot see the group, link the playlist or a Loom thank-you video directly. The example used Skool and Calendly, not a website or email automation.
3. **Match proof to objections.** In a DM, identify the prospect's concern and share an existing relevant interview or member example. Reuse existing content before producing new videos. The goal is to build trust and answer concerns before the call.
4. **Read conversion data cautiously.** If traffic is low or changed, do not assume the page caused a conversion-rate drop. Review qualified lead count and traffic source. Quick edits may simplify paragraphs, remove fluff, move a strong story higher, and explain what members receive. The speaker did not recommend a VSL for a free group.
5. **Prepare for launch.** Create content and reconnect with people in your network. Help them for free and build goodwill rather than pitching prematurely. Complete Backbone and launch-prep work.
6. **Call out qualified prospects.** Make content for the intended audience and state who it is for. A Manychat post can name a qualifier and outcome and ask people to comment a keyword. This lets prospects self-identify instead of requiring manual follower-list searches.
7. **Work from warm to cold.** Contact people who watch or engage first, then consider broader group members. Membership alone is not qualification. Exhaust warmer leads before reviewing the entire group.
8. **Improve setter qualification.** Review one to five unqualified calls per week. Clarify weak booking-form answers live, ask relevant follow-up questions, and selectively dial. Lower call volume can be preferable when prospects fit better.
9. **Add three search keywords.** Choose terms your dream customer would search and include them on the About page and group description. Balance search volume and niche relevance. The speaker said capitalization can affect results and suggested using uppercase and lowercase variants.
10. **Choose the right group arrangement.** One-to-one can stay in a free group. Cohorts usually work better in a separate group for recordings and discussion; a single group may work for about five or 10 participants. A large warm audience can go straight to calls, though a free group can warm leads and collect contact details. Sell before setting up another group.
11. **Set a content pace that fits the creator.** Weekly or every-other-week publishing may fit high-production creators; daily work may fit a scrappy style. Make several reps to learn the creator's style.
12. **Sell to the audience you have.** If the group already has enough potential clients, pause additional content and book 20 to 30 calls before adding a low-ticket tier or another product.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Member, peer DM:** “Hey, saw you in Synthesizer. Thought it'd be cool to connect.” Use to open a friendly conversation with a peer.
- **Coach, GPS prompt:** “What is my goal? What is holding me back from my goal?” Use to identify a useful next question or task.
- **Researcher, two-call close:** “If I created a custom plan for you to overcome [problem] and get to [result], when would you want to get started?” Follow with an investing-capacity question and invite qualified prospects to a longer call.
- **Creator, Manychat:** “If you're [qualifier] and want [outcome], comment [keyword].” Use to let relevant prospects self-identify.
- **Coach, realistic job promise:** “I will help you write a CV and walk you through my three-step process to maximize the likelihood of you landing a job.” Use when the coach cannot guarantee placement within a fixed time.
- **Creator, community pivot:** Explain why the niche is changing and what members can expect; invite those who are not interested to leave.
## Examples and case studies (member or company, what they did, the numbers and result; only what the transcript says)
- **Synthesizer Scaling:** It reached more than $300,000 monthly recurring revenue in under a year. The speaker used the milestone to link free help and community activity with business impact.
- **Selene's YouTube growth:** Her channel had a long flat period and then a sudden spike while she uploaded every day. The speaker used it to show that consistent effort may precede visible growth; members were told to study the video that took off and make more like it.
- **Sam Howe:** After networking with community members, he said his results grew from occasional $2,000 clients to about $17,000 per month. He had previously tried to do the work alone.
- **Simple thank-you system:** Scaling used a Skool post, a three-video YouTube playlist, and Calendly reminders instead of a website or automation system. The post's comments were disabled because comments bump a Skool post back to the top of the feed.
- **Discovery banner and search:** The speaker said conversion increased after Synthesizer replaced a generic banner with his face. He hypothesized this helped people searching for his name recognize the group. He also said about 70% of members Skool sends to groups came from discovery search.
- **Job-coaching offer:** A member had nine clients and one job placement after about three months, with a stated four-to-five-month promise. The advice was to improve delivery and examine what the successful client did differently before changing the niche. The coach had helped about 30 people get jobs previously, but said that year had been harder.
- **Setter dialing:** A team was making around 200 calls per day through a dialer, resulting in more booked calls but lower qualification and a slightly lower close rate. The team discussed adding questions, reviewing calls, and prioritizing fit over raw call volume.
## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not treat a small sample as a reliable conversion trend. Traffic volume and audience qualification can change the rate even when the About page has not changed.
- Do not spend hours searching a follower list for qualified prospects when content can call out the audience and engagement can reveal interest.
- Do not use comments just to bump a post. Genuine replies can raise a post, but the speaker warned that empty bump comments may lead to a ban.
- Do not use gift wars or other engagement contests without considering community quality. They can increase discovery rank while adding noise and attracting lower-quality activity.
- Do not optimize call volume if setters are booking unqualified prospects. Review actual conversations and booking forms, then add relevant qualification questions.
- Do not change a niche because early clients are slow. Check fit, timeline, delivery, and controllable blockers; promise only results and time frames you can support.
- Do not add products or content to avoid selling. Test the current offer with calls when the audience is sufficient.
- Do not build a complex follow-up system before using the basic version.

- A member with over 600 free members and only two or three sales calls was advised to book 20 to 30 calls before making more content or adding a tier.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use this with a creator whose attraction work feels complicated, whose leads are poorly qualified, or who is busy building an audience while not booking enough sales conversations. Ask how many relevant visitors, members, DMs, research calls, and sales calls they have; which source produced each lead; what prospects say in conversations; and what proof is available to address their objections. Before advising a group structure, ask whether the offer is one-to-one or cohort-based and how many people have bought. Before changing a promise, ask what results clients have reached, over what period, and what steps the provider can control.
