# Synthesizer Scaling Sales Script
Type: worksheet   Stage: Launch Convert   Length/size: 45-minute call; stated range 30-60 minutes
## One-paragraph summary
This is a customizable call script for selling a premium coaching or consulting offer. It organizes the call into preparation, opening and framing, discovery, transition, pitch, delivery explanation, commitment, investment, objection handling, and close. The salesperson first diagnoses the prospect's current state, problem, desired outcome, and cost of inaction, then presents the offer as a sequence of three customized pillars and explains how support works. Before stating the price, the script asks the prospect to express confidence and surface concerns. It directs the salesperson to state an authentic anchored investment, pause without justifying it, and then respond to objections using questions. The objection section groups common concerns by budget, authority, need, and time, with a guarantee and a booked follow-up as later options if the prospect does not decide.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- Pre-call preparation: create a quiet setting, review the prospect's application and relevant information, have the script and pen ready, record the call, regulate nerves with six physiological sighs, and join on time. Aim to help and diagnose accurately rather than fixate on closing.
- Intro and framing: build brief rapport, set the time expectation, check note-taking, explain the question-and-pitch sequence, and confirm the prospect will decide by the end whether to join.
- Discovery: learn (A) why they booked, (B) current work, offer, pricing and customer-getting process, (C) main challenge and prior attempts, (D) desired 12-month outcome and why it matters, and (E) the cost of inaction over the next six months. Confirm the gap and their commitment to changing it.
- Transition: invite missing context, explain why the seller believes the offer fits based on what the prospect said and the seller's proof, then ask permission to present.
- Three-pillar pitch: state the transformation and timeframe, have the prospect write down one to three points, then explain three logical stages. Tie each stage to proof, a problem or old approach, a mechanism, and benefits.
- Delivery explanation: describe the first setup or deliverable, the support model, and a later resource or capability that supports continued progress.
- Commitment before price: ask what stands out, what feels compatible, and the prospect's 1-10 confidence score. Ask why the score is not lower and what would make it a 10. Resolve process and fit questions before naming the investment.
- Objection handling: acknowledge, associate with reasonable buying behavior when useful, clarify with questions, isolate all concerns, respond to each, and re-ask for the sale.
- BANT categories: budget, authority, need, and time. If a same-call close does not happen, discuss a real guarantee or book a meeting from a meeting (BAMFAM).
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Before the call, tidy the background, dress neatly, choose a quiet room, use headphones, print the script, and review the prospect's application and relevant information. Record the call. Do six physiological sighs: deep inhale, hold, sharp additional inhale through the nose, slow exhale through the mouth. Join on time.
2. Aim for a 45-minute call, within the stated 30-60 minute range. Start with a friendly location question, explain the call plan, and confirm the prospect can take notes.
3. Ask why they booked. For a lead from DMs, acknowledge the prior chat but ask the motivation question again. If an answer is vague, ask what they mean or request more detail, then continue discovery.
4. Map current state: work, what they sell and price, how they currently get customers or the desired result, whether a system exists, and whether they are satisfied. Ask the main challenge, why it remains unsolved, previous attempts, and how long it has lasted.
5. Ask for the 12-month goal, why it matters, and what would change in life. Explore the cost of inaction: impact of the problem, what happens over six months if nothing changes, costs in time, energy, or money, and whether a roadmap six months earlier might have changed today's position. Ask how committed they are.
6. Before pitching, invite anything else the seller should know. Summarize why the offer seems suitable, using the prospect's situation and relevant proof, then ask permission to explain it.
7. Present the transformation as a three-step system with current state, desired outcome, and approximate timeframe. Ask the prospect to write down one to three points. Customize the name, proof, obstacle, mechanism, and benefits for each pillar, and show how the stages lead into one another.
8. Explain delivery after the pillars. Name the first setup, support, and later resource, connecting each to a client benefit. Address relevant concerns before moving on.
9. Check commitment with a 1-10 scale. If below 10, ask why it is not 1 and what would make it a 10. Clarify any concern before answering it, explain the relevant feature, and ask whether that answered the question.
10. Before price, ask what other questions remain and explain the next steps if the person joins. State the authentic anchor and actual investment, then pause and maintain confident eye contact. Do not justify the price.
11. If the prospect says yes, send a custom payment link in the call chat, collect payment, provide access, and explain the immediate next step. For any other response, ask what is holding them back; keep clarifying until you know whether it is a yes, no, or an objection to handle.
12. For budget, ask what else would prevent joining if finances were removed. If nothing else, ask whether they would proceed with the money, why they would do it, and whether the offer would have improved their position six months earlier. Ask how to make it work. Consider a payment plan only after confirming the full amount is the barrier.
13. For spouse/partner authority, include the decision-maker on the call or reschedule for when both can attend. Ask whether the partner is supportive and what questions they may have. If nearby, invite them in; otherwise book and invite both to a specific follow-up.
14. For “I need to think,” ask what feels least compatible and what questions may arise after the call. If needed, ask how the offer could resolve their problem and whether hesitation has contributed to staying stuck. Re-ask for the sale, then discuss only the actual guarantee. If they still need time, ask how long and book the follow-up.
15. For timing, ask why it is not right, then hypothetically set time aside and ask what else would prevent joining. If time is the only issue, discuss paying and receiving access now with a later start only if the business can offer it. Recheck commitment and ask for the sale.
16. If no close, test whether a specific guarantee would give the prospect confidence. Describe only a guarantee the business actually offers. If they still need time, ask how long, send the calendar link, and book a specific follow-up.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- Salesperson, opening: “Hey [name], great to connect! Where are you calling from?” Use for brief rapport.
- Salesperson, framing: “I’ll ask you a couple of questions around where you’re at, what you’re working on, and where you want to go. Then if it sounds like we’re a good fit, I can walk you through what it looks like to work together and at the end you can decide if you want to be part of it or not! Sound good?” Use after checking note-taking.
- Salesperson, motivation: “What got you interested in booking this call in the first place?” For a DM lead, mention the prior chat but ask again.
- Salesperson, vague answer: “When you say ___, what do you mean exactly?” Use to get specifics.
- Salesperson, goal: “What is your 12 month goal for the [thing]?” Use after current-state and problem questions.
- Salesperson, inaction: “What happens if nothing changes over the next 6 months?” Use to explore consequences.
- Salesperson, transition: “Before we potentially move on, what else should I know about you or your situation?” Use before summarizing fit.
- Salesperson, confidence: “What would it take to make this a 10 for you?” Use if their score is below 10.
- Salesperson, objection clarification: “When you say ___, what exactly do you mean by that?” Ask before explaining a feature.
- Salesperson, yes/close: “I’ll send over a custom payment link in the chat of this call. Then we can get you immediately inside so we can take action on [next step] right away.” Use after a yes.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not enter without reviewing the prospect and preparing the room, script, and recording setup.
- Do not fixate on closing; the preparation notes say to help and diagnose accurately.
- Do not pitch before learning the person's situation, problem, goals, and cost of inaction.
- Do not leave the three pillars generic. Customize the mechanisms, proof, outcomes, timeframe, and delivery.
- Do not give the price while fit or process questions remain. Do not justify the price after stating it.
- Do not treat “maybe” or “I'll get back to you” as a decision. Clarify to a yes, no, or scheduled follow-up.
- Do not fabricate an anchor, scarcity, claim, payment arrangement, or guarantee. Replace every sample with true proof and terms.
- Do not offer installments before confirming that full payment is the remaining concern, and do not leave spouse/partner decisions unaddressed.
