# 4. Action Steps
Type: lesson   Stage: Launch Deliver   Length/size: Video transcript, 6:15, plus worksheet/checklist
## One-paragraph summary
This wrap-up closes the Launch Deliver training and gives the learner four actions: watch the full training, outline the phases of the program, record a welcome video after the outline is clear, and build the program one week at a time while working with clients. The initial program outline does not need to be fully populated. It should make the process visible so the creator can explain what participants will do. The welcome video introduces the process and supports onboarding across eight named outcomes: validation, vision, identity, outcome, clarity, confidence, expectations, and momentum. The instructor warns against delaying delivery to create a polished or complex course; a Google Doc and one-to-one guidance can be enough for the first version.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Launch Deliver action sequence:** (1) Watch all Launch Deliver training. (2) Create a program outline showing the phases. (3) Film a welcome video once the outline is clear enough to explain. (4) Build one week at a time as the work unfolds, based on the outline and participant needs.
- **Program outline as a map:** The outline provides the phases of the customer process. At this point, it is not a complete course or a fully populated curriculum. It gives the creator a clear picture of the process and helps them explain what they will guide participants through.
- **Onboarding pillars:** The welcome/onboarding should address validation, vision, identity, outcome, clarity, confidence, expectations, and building momentum. The transcript names these as the results onboarding should accomplish, but does not give definitions or a prescribed order for them.
- **Build as you go:** Create only the next material needed to help a client progress. Use actions from the program outline to make modules, publish or share them, work through the material with clients, then make the next part. The first version can be simple and delivered personally rather than through a finished course platform.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Watch all Launch Deliver training. The instructor says it is acceptable to watch at 2x speed. The training is intended to increase knowledge even when the learner is not ready to use every lesson immediately.
2. Open the program outline template and write the phases on the left side. Do not attempt to fill the entire document. Use the offer, pitch, and promised deliverables already developed to identify the process participants will follow.
3. Once the phases form a clear enough process to explain, record a welcome video. Use it to explain what the participant is doing and orient them to the onboarding process. The video should address the eight onboarding pillars named in the lesson, though the source does not specify a required length, script, or recording tool.
4. Start delivering using the outline. Walk clients through the material one-on-one if needed. Identify the next action they need to progress, turn those actions into a module or lesson, make it live, and work through it with participants.
5. Continue building one week at a time. Each phase may map to a week, but it does not have to. The transcript gives a six-week accelerator with six phases as one possible arrangement and also mentions four phases as another example. Use the program's actual duration and process rather than forcing every phase into a week.
6. Keep the first version simple. The instructor says it can be a Google Doc that the creator walks through with people. Deliver the basic material, observe how clients use it, and develop the next component as they progress.
7. Complete the checklist: watch training, create the program outline, film the welcome video, and build one week at a time as the program runs.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not fill in the entire program template before beginning. The immediate task is to get the phases clear.
- Do not delay delivery to build a polished or “crazy insane course.” The first version may be a Google Doc and guided one-to-one delivery.
- Do not assume every phase must equal one week. A six-week program with six phases is one option, not a standard imposed on every offer.
- Do not build future modules without considering what clients need next. The lesson's sequence is to make the material needed to progress, work through it, then create the next part.
- The instructor refers to an earlier “Build as you go” training as the model. This lesson does not reproduce its full instructions, so do not infer missing production or platform steps.
