## One-paragraph summary
This call covers Launch Attract, the step after the Business Backbone and Launch Prep. The immediate goal is to book calls for the premium offer, using an 11-post sequence and a simple DM-to-call process. The hosts explain how to set a call target, schedule booking and sales calls, adapt posts to existing channels, and avoid overbuilding course content or community infrastructure before customers arrive. Q&A covers payment, call attendance, and tailoring the process to existing audiences.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Rapid Launch Protocol:** Complete the Business Backbone, then Launch Prep, including the opportunity audit, resonance, and warming. Move to Launch Attract and book calls, followed by Launch Convert and Launch Deliver. If the Backbone is incomplete, return to it before following this call.
- **“Book calls” priority:** Focus on qualified sales calls as the main growth metric. The chef story is a mnemonic: the chef fills his restaurant by “cooking balls,” meaning “book calls.” Keep launch energy on booking and taking calls, rather than unrelated program activities or future delivery plans.
- **Booking and selling sequence:** Newer callers can spend about a week booking, then open a few call dates and fill them before adding more. Experienced callers can start taking calls sooner while continuing to book. A separate example is to book for seven days before selling. Sam Rathling reportedly stayed in Launch Convert for six to eight weeks before delivery; this is an example, not a required timeline.
- **11-post belief sequence:** Use the supplied, offer-specific posts to position the creator as an expert, describe the audience's painful problem and previous failed attempts, explain why those attempts did not work, then introduce the offer as a new opportunity. Keep the subject consistent, but adapt the creator's familiar hooks, voice, and visual style.
- **Two-call close:** Use DMs to book a short research call. Ask the supplied questions and assess fit. If you can help, invite the prospect to a longer call to present a custom plan and offer. Set the frame at the start so you guide the conversation. The call names a one-call close alternative but does not explain its procedure.
- **Build as you go:** Sell and talk to customers before building a full course. Learn what they need, deliver the next portion, then improve it from customer feedback. Solve the next problem in front of you instead of planning for hypothetical future problems.
- **Simple community setup:** Start with the current community or direct coaching if it works. Add a separate paid community when cohort size or member feedback makes it necessary, not as a default.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Confirm the prerequisite. Complete the Business Backbone. If Launch Prep is unfinished, make that the immediate task; the hosts say a small delay in Launch Prep is fine.
2. Set a specific call-booking target and keep it visible, such as on a note by your computer. Use the number to plan posting and calendar capacity.
3. For a new caller, focus on booking for about one week. Then open a small set of dates, fill them, and add dates as needed. The example opens the 18th, 19th, and 20th, then adds the 21st and 22nd when those fill. Keep booking while taking calls.
4. Complete Launch Attract training and its daily actions. Use the 11 posts supplied for your offer and audience. Publish the first post the same day as the call; default to no more than one post per day. Share where your audience already is, including social channels, email, and YouTube. Keep regular posting on-topic.
5. Adapt format to the channel. A post can become a YouTube video or multiple videos. Keep an existing cadence, such as Monday, Wednesday, Friday, if the launch needs three or four weeks. Weekends are optional. Keep familiar hooks and visual style; experiment if unsure what works.
6. Use the call-planning spreadsheet shared in the call chat. Choose File > Make a copy, then edit price, client target, show-up, and close assumptions. If you do not know your numbers, use the call's defaults: 30% close and 80% show-up.
7. For a two-call flow, book an approximately 15-minute research call using the DM script. Ask the provided questions. If the prospect is a fit, invite them to a longer call, explain that you will map a custom plan, and give two day options.
8. Ask whether anyone else should join the call, especially when the prospect may not decide alone. Confirm a time that works for all attendees, and remind them 24 or 12 hours before. For high-school students, afternoon slots may work better if available.
9. A sales page or complete course is not required before an interested prospect can buy. Explain the program on a call, make the offer, and send a payment link after agreement. Skool can process payment; Wise was suggested as an alternative if Skool declines or is unavailable. Add cohort clients to the community; arrange one-to-one access directly.
10. Avoid a second community unless needed. For cohorts, the presenters usually consider a separate space above 20 clients. Skool can limit classroom access to paying members. Review the existing low-ticket offer after the launch rather than changing everything in advance.
11. For a repeat launch, reuse posts and adjust the mechanism, dates, and available spots. An experienced member need not repost all 11. For a brand with multiple relevant channels, use the sequence on both; the hosts say people may not see every post.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not enter Launch Attract before completing the Business Backbone. If Launch Prep is unfinished, handle it before adding more tasks.
- Do not interpret low early engagement as proof the launch has failed. The hosts say one booked call is a win; the revenue examples are not guarantees.
- Do not post unrelated topics during the sequence. Keep the offer recognizable and post no more than once daily by default.
- Do not wait for a sales page, fully built course, or separate community before speaking to prospects. Avoid creating a second community unless it solves a current problem.
- Do not offer to someone you cannot help. Make the offer when you believe the prospect is a fit.
- Do not let the research-call prospect take over the conversation; explain the frame and guide the questions.
- Do not get overwhelmed by attending every program call during the launch. The presenters recommend focusing on booking and taking calls, and asking the community for help when needed.
