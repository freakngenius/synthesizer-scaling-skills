## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Goal-to-math launch planning:** Set a specific revenue or customer target; enter offer price, close rate, and show rate; calculate customers, calls taken, and calls booked; translate the total into a daily target over the calendar time available.
- **Conservative planning:** Build in room for lower show or close rates and missed opportunities. Plan the volume that makes the goal likely even if assumptions underperform.
- **Input/output prioritization:** Compare an action's expected benefit with the time, effort, money, and other inputs it consumes. Strategy is choosing where to allocate scarce attention.
- **Launch's two operating channels:** Posts create public attention and explain the launch; chats/outreach turn attention into conversations and calls. Use a clear “big idea” repeatedly so the audience recognizes the launch.
- **Warm pipeline reactivation:** The warmest leads are people who booked a call, attended or missed, canceled, or declined. Revisit them every couple of months and especially during a new launch. Sales reps were expected to report daily follow-ups alongside calls and sales.
- **Big fish in a small pond:** The case study shows that the same content skill and proof can perform much better in a smaller, less competitive language or geographic market. Choose a niche where your credibility is strong relative to competitors.
- **Demand channeling and awareness:** “Breakthrough Advertising” is cited for the principle that marketers channel existing demand rather than manufacture it. Address the audience at the awareness level they already occupy. The attached worksheet defines stages from completely unaware through most aware.
- **Constraint-first growth:** Work backward from target sales. Check close rate, then call volume, then the sources and conversion steps feeding calls. Solve the narrowest limiting step before changing the offer, content mechanism, or traffic channel.
- **Message consistency audit:** Once a month, review every public surface from the first ad or post through bio, video description, group About page, calendar page, and CTA. Ensure the audience, outcome, and mechanism match throughout.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Write one measurable goal, such as a monthly revenue amount, customer count, or cohort seats. “Sell out” is not specific until translated into a number.
2. Use a calculator or sheet with offer price, target revenue, close rate, and show rate. Calculate required customers, calls taken, and calls booked. Divide calls by days with actual calendar availability to establish a daily booking target.
3. Add a buffer. In the launch example, the team wanted 60 customers and a 40% close rate. The arithmetic minimum was 150 calls, but the plan used 200 calls to allow for failure. Then translate to daily targets based on the 18 available sales days.
4. List the likely marketing actions and rank them by benefit relative to required input. One low-effort action in the example was a repeating calendar event announcing a price increase. Events were set to repeat every other day, last 24 hours, and send an email reminder one day before; this worked around a stated Skool email limit of one email every 72 hours. Each event linked to call booking.
5. Create a launch's central idea. Examples in the call include an upcoming price increase, a new platform feature, a new model, or a new market opportunity. Repeat the same idea rather than assuming people saw the first announcement.
6. In a joint venture, ask the host to introduce you before pitching. Teach something useful, establish relevant credibility, recognize the host's authority, and follow up with people who engage.
7. Use launch posts and private chats. Follow up with people who engage and book calls with suitable prospects.
8. Every couple of months, revisit prior calls, no-shows, qualified group members, social engagers, ongoing conversations, then colder audiences. Use phone or email when available.
9. For ongoing one-to-one coaching, run a larger public launch every few months with a defined number of spaces. Continue selling privately through DMs between launches. The speaker suggested launches roughly four times a year, with six as a possible maximum.
10. When content takes off, increase outreach, then scale with ManyChat for new followers/commenters and follow-ups. Add story CTAs. Reply first to active, engaged leads.
11. Choose a group with a clear identity and shared hangouts. Search Skool and Facebook groups, then LinkedIn. Use repeated engagement to build recognition.
12. Monthly, align the audience, outcome, and mechanism across bios, video titles, About page, and booking calendar.
13. For a free course inside a group, add a short CTA at the top of each lesson. The call says Scaling had placed this in each lesson but gives no exact copy.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not define a goal as “as many customers as possible” or “sell out” without a number and deadline.
- Do not plan only for the ideal scenario. Build a buffer for missed calls, no-shows, and lower conversion.
- Do not mistake a preferred strategic issue, such as needing a unique mechanism, for the actual constraint. Follow the funnel data.
- Do not neglect people who previously booked calls but did not buy; they are warmer than cold leads.
- Do not switch from a proven one-to-one or high-ticket offer to a different model simply to reduce the owner's sales workload. The call suggests hiring a sales rep when demand and calendar volume support it.
- Do not confuse a broad audience that has not recognized a problem with one already seeking a solution. It is generally harder to persuade unaware or problem-aware people.
- Do not infer that one viral post guarantees durable growth. Felix's results were an early snapshot.
- Do not make false scarcity or price-increase claims. The launch examples depend on a real deadline or actual price change.
- Do not let a new audience or offer coexist with stale bios and funnel copy.
