## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- Business Backbone worksheet: record six Vision prompts (why the user is here, current problems, current income, consequences, desired situation, desired income, and mission); three Proof items, with images suggested; Niche (who is served, current situation, problems, desires, and who is not served); Premium Offer (outcome, price, unique mechanism, deliverables, name, guarantee, scarcity/urgency); and Backstory (past situation, pains, desires, epiphany, current situation).
- Launch Calendar: add start and end dates for Prep, Attract, Convert, and Deliver. The worksheet is a planning shell; it does not prescribe fixed launch dates or durations.
- Sales call sequence: pre-call preparation; introduction and framing; discovery; transition; pitch; commitment; investment; objections and close. The script supplies suggested language for each, and separate training resources cover discovery, pitch, and objection handling.
- Objection-handling pattern: identify the actual concern by asking what else is holding the prospect back; acknowledge it, explore what they mean, and address one concern at a time. If they do not decide, schedule a specific follow-up meeting, called BAMFAM in the script.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Prepare the call: tidy the background; be presentable; use a quiet, interruption-free room and headphones; print the script with a pen; review the application, social profiles, and prior messages; record the call; do six physiological sighs (inhale, hold, add a short nasal inhale, then exhale slowly through the mouth); release attachment to closing; join on time.
2. Plan for a 45-minute call, with 30-60 minutes given as the usual range. Open with brief rapport, ask if they have something to take notes on, and explain that you will ask about their situation and goals, then present the offer if it appears to fit. The prospect decides whether to join.
3. Discover the prospect's motivation, current work and offer, current approach, main challenge, attempted solutions, how long the issue has lasted, desired 12-month result, motivation, life impact, and cost of staying in the same position for six months. Adapt the questions to the niche rather than asking irrelevant stock questions.
4. Before pitching, ask what else you should know about the prospect or their situation. State why you believe you can help, using their words and relevant proof, then ask permission to explain the offer.
5. Pitch using a three-step system from current situation to desired result in a stated timeframe. Explain each pillar and its benefit; pause for the prospect's reaction and questions. Describe the delivery in terms of the support and progress it enables, not just the feature list.
6. Ask what stands out, what feels compatible, and for a 1-10 confidence rating. If the answer is below 10, ask why it is not a 1 and what would make it a 10. Resolve concerns before asking for the investment.
7. Present the investment plainly, then stop talking. The template suggests a limited early-client price based on a higher reference price, but its fields are placeholders and do not authorize a specific discount. If the prospect says yes, send a custom payment link in the call chat and explain the immediate next step.
8. If they raise an objection, ask what else is holding them back until a specific concern is isolated. For a partner decision, invite the partner into the call or schedule a follow-up when both can attend. For "think about it," ask what part feels least compatible and what information is missing; if unresolved, ask how long they need and book a follow-up. For a time objection, ask what makes the timing wrong and what time they will invest. A payment plan or guarantee is conditional on the seller actually offering it.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- Coach, call framing: “I’ll ask you a couple of questions around where you’re at, what you’re working on, and where you want to go. Then if it sounds like we’re a good fit, I can walk you through what it looks like to work together.” Use after brief rapport.
- Coach, opening discovery: “What got you interested in booking this call in the first place?” If booked through DMs, acknowledge that you have already chatted before asking.
- Coach, diagnose a vague answer: “When you say ___, what do you mean exactly?” or “Could you explain that in a little more depth?”
- Coach, uncover the challenge: “What do you think is the biggest challenge right now?” Follow with “Why do you think you haven’t solved it yet?”, “What have you tried already?” and “How long has this been going on?”
- Coach, desired outcome: “What is your 12 month goal for the [thing]?” Then ask about the motivation and how reaching it would affect other areas of life.
- Coach, transition: “Before we potentially move on, what else should I know about you or your situation?” Ask before presenting the offer to surface concerns early.
- Coach, pillar check-in: “What stands out to you about that?” Use after explaining a pillar.
- Coach, confidence check: “On a scale of 1-10… where would you sit in terms of confidence on that right now?” Follow with “Why not a 1?” and “What would it take to make this a 10 for you?”
- Coach, follow-up when a decision is deferred: “How long do you think you need to think about this?” Then send the calendar link and book the agreed follow-up time.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not arrive late, distracted, unprepared, or unaware of prior DM context. Re-asking questions already covered can signal that you did not pay attention.
- Do not become attached to closing. The script frames the seller's role as helping and diagnosing accurately.
- Do not accept vague non-answers in place of a clear yes or no. Clarify the concern, without presuming every delay is a genuine objection.
- Do not offer payment terms or guarantees that are not part of the actual offer. The template's examples are placeholders.
- If a spouse or business partner is genuinely part of the decision, involve them or book a joint follow-up instead of leaving the decision unresolved.
