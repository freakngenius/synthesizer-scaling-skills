# The Ultimate Client Attraction Checklist
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 1,730 transcript lines
## One-paragraph summary
This call crowdsources and ranks ways to book more qualified calls, starting with the warmest people and moving toward colder audiences and slower channels. The core operating principle is to maximize call bookings by following up with people who already know the coach, then expanding outreach deliberately. The session covers prior prospects and clients, warm networks, Skool calendar events, live events, direct messages, Instagram automations, YouTube links and calls to action, community engagement, and basic tracking. It also advises people still preparing for launch to follow the program sequence rather than copy tactics intended for post-launch members.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Warmest-first outreach:** Start with people who already know, trust, or have interacted with you. Work outward to colder prospects only after following up with the warmer groups. This includes prior calls that did not convert, former clients, warm contacts, and people engaging with content.
- **Call-booking checklist, ordered from quick wins to longer-term channels:** Re-contact former clients and unconverted calls; ask satisfied clients for referrals; message the warm network; nurture prior contacts with relevant content; use social platform CTAs and DMs; optimize profile links and video descriptions; reach people inside a free Skool group; build relationships through useful comments; and use longer-term content such as YouTube. A one-time 90-minute sprint through the list was recommended as an implementation session.
- **Content as nurture:** Content often moves prospects along rather than converting them on first exposure. A typical path may include seeing a short video, following, engaging, joining a group, receiving a DM, and watching a longer training before booking. Use events and recordings to build familiarity, then personally follow up with attendees, commenters, and people who did not attend.
- **Conversation re-entry:** The immediate goal of a follow-up is often to reopen a conversation, not force a sale. Offer a relevant resource with a low-effort yes/no question, deliver it after they respond, then ask about their stated goal. A simple response makes the next exchange easier.
- **Minimum viable tracking:** Build a system only when the operational problem is clear. Prioritize a list of qualified people and a way not to lose follow-ups. A spreadsheet is useful but not mandatory if a person already has a reliable way to work through a list.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Check the calendar for calls booked in the upcoming week. If empty, prioritize qualified-call booking.
2. Start with warm leads: people from past calls who did not buy, former clients, trusted contacts, and people who engaged with content. Ask about the goal previously discussed and whether it remains a priority. Revisit warm contacts about once a month.
3. Ask satisfied clients for referrals and consider a referral bonus. Contact former clients whose circumstances may have changed.
4. Make booking easy in Skool by adding a call link in settings/plugins, relevant classroom lessons, and the calendar. Create a 24-hour event with a booking or application link and schedule it more than 24 hours ahead to allow the one-day reminder email. The example description offered 10 spots. Repeated events can display a live banner and send reminders; the speaker said this can work around the 72-hour broadcast interval.
5. Hold useful weekly live events. Email the recording to the group, then DM attendees, commenters, and people who missed it. The speaker reports that weekly events generated customers in his community.
6. On Instagram or another DM-enabled platform, invite people to comment a keyword for a resource or free group. Use ManyChat for the first DM if useful, but edit automated text to sound personal. Ask whether they got access or want it sent; give the resource after they respond and continue the conversation on that platform.
7. Use voice notes after a prospect responds. Start with a simple text that is easy to answer, then use voice when it fits the conversation and audience.
8. Put direct links in YouTube descriptions and social bios, pointing to a booking page or free Skool group. The speaker recommends no link trees. Add a video CTA before audience drop-off, using analytics to choose placement.
9. For YouTube, upload a VSL or Skool about-page video as unlisted, link video end screens to it, and put the booking link in its description and pinned comment. Use an image-and-link community post to promote it; the speaker posted these three to four times weekly in an earlier business.
10. Contact free-group members through Skool DMs, email, phone, or text. Call promptly when a phone number is available. A short phone call can replace a week of DM back-and-forth; call no-shows if appropriate.
11. For a large opportunity audit, rank lead sources from warmest to coldest, choose one to begin, and list prospects in batches of 10. A spreadsheet helps track follow-ups but is optional if another reliable system works. The goal is contact, not data entry.
12. If a live challenge takes substantial work but produces no calls or clients, test simpler lead-generation methods or repurpose the material as evergreen classroom content.

## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Coach, re-engaging a warm contact:** “Hey, haven't spoken with you in a while, how is it going with [specific outcome]?” Use when checking in with someone who knows you.
- **Coach, following up with an unconverted prospect:** “How's it going? I just saw you did this amazing thing on your profile. I've been to Spain as well. It's beautiful. By the way, how's it going with your social confidence? Are you as socially confident as you want to be or are you still working towards it?” Personalize the opening and use the goal question to learn whether the issue remains.
- **Coach, offering a relevant video:** “I just filmed this piece of content about how to get more done in less time, so you can scale your business while working a full-time nine to five. Did you get access to that yet or want me to send it over for you?” Use after producing content tied to a known prospect problem.
- **Coach, after sending the resource:** “Is [goal] still a priority for you right now? Or did you [achieve goal] already?” Use to reopen discussion about a previously stated goal.
- **Coach, ManyChat keyword follow-up:** “Saw you commented [keyword]. Did you get access to that yet or need me to send it over?” Use when a person comments for a lead magnet.
- **Coach, Skool calendar event:** “Limited time, 10 spots open to book a call with me to help [niche] achieve [outcome]. Click the link to apply/book a call.” Use in a time-limited event description; replace the niche and outcome with accurate details.
- **Coach, basic follow-up when a message may have been missed:** “Bumping this up.” The speaker calls this generic, suitable only when no more personalized follow-up comes to mind.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not start with cold outreach when warmer people are available. Follow up with previous prospects and clients before expanding.
- Do not assume a CTA, freebie, or one DM converts everyone. The purpose of content and lead magnets also includes nurturing and starting conversations.
- Do not over-automate when the actual bottleneck is doing the outreach. Automation may lower conversion quality; use it when lead volume exceeds personal capacity and accept that trade-off.
- Do not build complex tracking or tech systems before the problem is clear. Keep the stack simple and contact people.
- Do not send YouTube traffic to a link tree. The recommended destinations are a call-booking page or a free Skool community.
- Do not place the only YouTube CTA late in a video without checking retention. Use pinned comments because descriptions can be hidden on mobile.
- Do not keep running an exhausting challenge if it fails to produce clients or calls. Consider turning a useful live challenge into evergreen classroom material.
- Do not add a second traffic channel when one already supplies more calls than you can handle.
- For members before launch, follow the program sequence: watch “The 100 Details” and “Start here,” introduce yourself, complete and review the Business Backbone, complete Launch Prep, build goodwill, and keep creating useful content.
## Numbers and benchmarks (conversion rates, call counts, prices, targets, time frames, with the context for each)

- Phone setting calls can take about five minutes, compared with a week of back-and-forth DMs in the example.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use this checklist when someone has completed a launch or has a free group, an audience, past prospects, prior clients, or underused social traffic, and needs more qualified calls soon. Ask which channels currently bring in leads, how many qualified calls are booked this week, who has already had a call or been a client, what follow-ups are outstanding, what call link or free community they want to promote, and whether they are before or after their launch. If the user has no warm list, identify one source of likely prospects and begin with small batches.
