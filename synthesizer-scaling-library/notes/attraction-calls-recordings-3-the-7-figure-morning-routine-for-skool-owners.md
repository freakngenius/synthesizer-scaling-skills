# The 7-Figure Morning Routine for Skool Owners
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 1,623 lines, 15,303 words in source file
## One-paragraph summary
This attraction call presents the team's daily stand-up as a repeatable morning review for tracking cash flow, calls, sales conversion, priorities, and customer wins. The same practice can be simplified for a solo operator and is intended to keep attention on measurable constraints. The call then answers questions about improving booking and show rates, nurturing booked prospects with proof, creating launch urgency, choosing a webinar promotion window, and deciding when to focus on audience growth versus a launch. It reinforces a test-and-learn loop: review what generated calls or sales, repeat what works, and adjust what does not.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Daily stand-up:** A short regular meeting built around a fixed checklist. Team members report what they did yesterday and what they will do today. A solo owner can use the same sequence as a morning reset.
- **Daily dashboard:** Review cash flow or MRR; calls and calendar availability; yesterday's bookings and today's target; sales conversion and objections; roadmap priorities; and customer wins. Team follow-up sessions are Monday/Thursday for setters, Tuesday/Friday for sales, and Wednesday for customer success.
- **Measure the whole funnel:** Calls and cash flow are leading operating measures. People who reached the booking step have already moved through the earlier funnel, so maintain availability, clarify the call's value, and follow up on bookings, cancellations, and no-shows.
- **Booking-source review:** List recent booked prospects and identify the source or action that produced each booking. Repeat actions linked to bookings and reduce effort spent on activities that do not produce them.
- **Practice and review loop:** Do repetitions, identify what worked, and do more of it; identify what failed and do less. Apply to content, outreach, call setting, sales, and video editing.
- **Launch and audience sequence:** During a launch, first work with the people who already know you. Continue attracting new people during launch and delivery if capacity allows, so a fresh audience exists for the next offer opening.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. **Run the morning review:** Check cash flow daily; detailed bank-account review can be weekly. Check bookings and calendar slots, then record yesterday's calls and today's target. A solo review can take about two minutes.
2. **Check Calendly and Google Calendar:** Review upcoming calls and the public booking page. Keep reasonable slots available across time zones; add slots or another caller if needed.
3. **Review sales:** Use the tracker to assess close rate and repeated objections. Review won and lost calls. The speaker's rule is at least 30 calls before treating the close rate as a useful measure.
4. **Trace call sources:** For at least five recent bookings, note each person's name and source in one or two words. Expand to all available calls, potentially 100, then do more of the activities producing calls.
5. **Place proof after booking:** Keep the booking page focused. Put multiple testimonials on the thank-you page and reminders. Use video on the thank-you page and short text testimonials in reminders sent 72 hours, 24 hours, or one hour before the call.
6. **Structure the call funnel:** A VSL can precede the booking page to pre-sell. After booking, confirm the call, state its benefit, and share a case study.
7. **Choose call format by stage:** A two-call close can ease early sales through an initial discovery call. A one-call close is simpler when sales skill is stronger and reduces drop-off between calls.
8. **Work the final launch days:** Use Skool calendar events, reminders, broadcasts, deadline posts, and accurate spot counts. Examples include a five-day countdown or updates at 20, 10, and five spots. Honor calls booked before the deadline even if they occur afterward when promised.
9. **Limit reminders:** Excess notices can trigger unsubscribes or disabled notifications; an always-visible banner can be ignored. One example was using event reminders for four days each month, not a universal rule.
10. **Promote webinars for five to seven days:** Aim for two or three broadcasts. Three days was considered too short; four days permits two broadcasts 72 hours apart. Estimate capacity before scheduling. One caller typically takes eight to nine calls daily, up to 10 to 12 when pushing.
11. **Transition content gradually:** Add niche-specific posts alongside broad content, accepting fewer views. Repeat a successful video idea within its series rather than waiting.
12. **Convert existing video traffic:** Use YouTube Studio's bulk editor to add a niche-and-outcome CTA to the first description line on videos still receiving views. Community posts can use the thumbnail as an image with a link.
13. **Review DMs:** Ask Cynthia, the program AI, to analyze a conversation or suggest a next reply. Setters can exchange reviews or ask for feedback in the community.
14. **Respect community norms:** Observe what admins promote and help them, for example by sharing member wins or promoting their offer appropriately. Repeatedly posting against their preferences can get you removed.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not focus only on the front end while neglecting booking availability, the thank-you page, reminders, cancellations, and no-shows.
- Do not crowd the booking page with links that distract from booking; deliver most testimonials after the prospect schedules.
- Do not infer a close rate from a tiny sample; the speaker uses 30 calls as the rule of thumb.
- Do not assume a one-call or two-call close is always superior; fit the format to sales skill and funnel drop-off.
- Do not use false scarcity or leave an outdated number of remaining spots posted.
- Do not send so many reminders that people unsubscribe or disable notices.
- Do not judge niche content by views alone; it may get fewer views but attract more relevant prospects.
- Avoid edits that cut mid-sentence. Review the recording, identify jarring transitions, and practice.
- In another Skool community, do not keep posting against the admins' preferences. Learn what they want and contribute accordingly.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use this call when the business owner is losing track of daily priorities, has inconsistent calls, is unsure where bookings come from, or wants to improve booked-call follow-up and launch execution. Ask for current cash flow or MRR, weekly booked and attended calls, close rate and sample size, calendar availability, and sources for recent bookings. For launches, ask the closing date, true capacity, current registrations, calls available per day, and whether late-booked calls can be honored. For content, ask which videos still get views, what the content is meant to attract, and whether a successful topic can become a series. For a solo user, remove team-specific tracker responsibilities but keep the daily metric and action review.
