# 4. Action Steps
Type: lesson   Stage: Launch Convert   Length/size: about 7 minutes; transcript runs to 7:31
## One-paragraph summary
This lesson closes the Launch Convert sequence with a simple sales-call tracker and a short set of actions. After each call, enter the date, prospect name, outcome, cash collected, payment terms, and useful notes. The tracker automatically totals calls, customers, close rate, current cash, and remaining cash against a goal. Copy the provided tracker rather than requesting edit access to the template. Watch the Launch Convert trainings, duplicate and personalize the sales script, read it at least ten times, practice the pitch, then make the pitch on a call and post about the attempt in the community, even if no sale results. The tracker also functions as a follow-up list for future launches because it preserves context about people who did not buy and why.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- World's simplest sales tracker: one row per call, with date, name, outcome, cash collected, terms, and notes. The sheet calculates totals for goal cash, current cash, remaining cash, calls, customers, and close rate. Record calls consistently so the business can see the results of sales activity and retain a usable lead list.
- Call outcomes: use the sheet's outcome field to classify the result. The transcript names BAMFAM (book a meeting from a meeting), no deal, no offer made, customer, no show, and canceled. “No offer made” means the person was not qualified to receive an offer. The tracker is intended to record these basic states and avoid unnecessary extra tracking.
- Launch Convert action sequence: (1) Watch all Launch Convert training, including discovery, pitch, objection handling, and action steps. (2) Make a personal copy of the sales script template and tracker. (3) Fine-tune and practice the pitch. (4) Deliver the pitch on a call. (5) Post the attempt or win in the community.
- Tracker as a learning and follow-up asset: call records preserve prospect names, outcomes, payment information, and notes. When someone does not buy, notes can help identify an objection that was not handled. The same list can be revisited during a later launch, including for past customers who may be relevant to a new offer.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Make a copy of the tracker before entering data. The instructor says to use File, then Make a Copy, name the copy with your own name and “Sales Call Tracker,” and use that copy rather than requesting edit permission on the shared template.
2. Set the goal cash cell to the amount you want to collect in the launch or in the next six months. The default example is $100,000. The instructor says to customize this based on the goal from the Business Backbone. Examples mentioned include $200,000, $300,000, and $350,000.
3. After every call, add the date and person's name, then select the outcome. Add cash collected and terms when applicable, such as an installment amount and a five-month payment plan. Use the notes field for details that will help interpret the outcome or follow up later.
4. Use the outcome options consistently: customer when the person buys, no deal when they do not, no offer made when they were not qualified, no show when they do not attend, canceled when they cancel, and BAMFAM when another meeting is booked from the current meeting. The sheet automatically updates aggregate counts and cash figures.
5. When a customer result appears, share the win in the community. The instructor explicitly invites members to post when they close, so the group can celebrate the result.
6. Watch all Launch Convert trainings, not only the action-steps lesson. The course contains the sales script, discovery training, pitch training, and objection-handling training. Use the videos to refine judgment and improve execution, even if the member already has sales experience.
7. Duplicate the sales script and adapt it to the offer. Read it at least ten times before relying on it in a call. Practice until the pitch feels familiar enough to deliver without reading word-for-word. The lesson says to use practice opportunities on program calls to fine-tune the pitch.
8. The weekly goal is to make a pitch on a call and post about it, even if the prospect does not buy. Count delivering the pitch as a win for this assignment. The source does not require a sale as the completion condition.
9. Keep the tracker after the launch. Revisit previous prospects in later launches. A past non-buyer may reconsider after the original concern is understood, and previous customers may buy a new product or mastermind.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not request access to edit the shared tracker. Make your own copy from File > Make a Copy.
- Do not overcomplicate call tracking. Record the fields and outcomes the sheet uses; the instructor says to ignore other items.
- Do not skip logging calls that end without an offer, with a no-show, or after cancellation. Those outcomes remain useful activity data.
- Do not treat the weekly assignment as a sales-only quota. The stated goal is to pitch on a call and post about it, whether or not the prospect buys.
- Do not forget to customize the goal cash cell to your own business goal. The $100,000 default is an example baseline, not a mandatory target.
- Do not discard the tracker after the initial launch. The instructor treats it as a future lead list and a source of context for subsequent launches.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use this lesson when a member is about to begin taking sales calls or wants a lightweight way to track launch activity and improve the pitch. Ask what cash target they are working toward, which fields matter for their offer, how they define a qualified prospect, what payment terms they actually use, and where they will store follow-up context. Ask whether they have made their own copies of both the script and tracker. For coaching, check whether they have watched the supporting discovery, pitch, and objection-handling lessons and whether they have delivered at least one pitch on a call.
