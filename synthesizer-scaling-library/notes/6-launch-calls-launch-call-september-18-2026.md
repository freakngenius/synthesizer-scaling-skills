## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Launch sequence:** Complete and get approval for the Business Backbone; use Launch Prep, also called resonance posts, to test the offer and warm the audience; run Launch Attract to book calls; then focus on Launch Convert and Launch Deliver. Stay focused on the current stage rather than building every business system at once.
- **Call-booking focus:** The memorable shorthand is “cook balls” becomes “book calls.” During Launch Attract, focus on qualified sales calls taken. Set a whole-launch call target and organize daily work around booking. When sales calls begin, continue outreach and booking alongside them.
- **11-post persuasion sequence:** Posts position the creator as an expert and the premium offer as a new opportunity. They should use the approved offer, mechanism, proof, and relevant personal journey. The intended reader response includes wanting the outcome, believing the proof, and feeling motivated to apply. The transcript does not enumerate all 11 titles or a complete step-by-step for each post.
- **New opportunity:** Frame the offer as a new way to reach an outcome, not as criticism. Explain why a past attempt failed and introduce a new method with proof.
- **Goal math:** Work backward from revenue to customers, then from customers to calls. At $1,000 per customer, a $10,000 goal requires 10 customers. If call data is unavailable, use the stated assumptions of 30% close rate and 80% show-up rate, which implies about 42 booked calls.
- **Research before finalizing an unclear offer:** Use three resonance posts, polls, conversations, and optionally research calls to find which problem or outcome the audience wants. Offer choices plus “other.” Once demand and the offer are clearer, return for Backbone approval and tailored launch posts.
- **One-to-one before cohort:** First deliver to several clients and learn the repeatable process. Then document and teach that process to a cohort. The team's first cohort began without a finished classroom; it created training week by week after the delivery process was understood.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Confirm Backbone approval before publishing the launch. If approval is pending, review prior Backbone recordings, refine the offer, and talk to prospects. Wait to launch posts because the offer may shift.
2. Set the call goal from price and revenue. The call gives 10 calls as a beginner target, 30 intermediate, and 100 advanced. It associates these with $2,000-$20,000, $5,000-$50,000, and $20,000-$200,000 revenue ranges, without explaining the assumptions behind those ranges.
3. If new to sales calls, spend seven days booking, then begin taking calls the following week. Those confident in calls can book and take them immediately. Keep booking once calls begin.
4. Open next week's calendar for Monday through Wednesday first, then add Thursday and Friday as slots fill. Avoid scheduling too far ahead so prospects remember the offer.
5. Publish the 11 provided launch posts. The speaker jokingly says each could take three to four hours to write, 33 to 44 hours total, but directs members to use their personalized posts rather than inventing copy. If already partway through the sequence, continue from the matching point.
6. Publish at most one launch post a day. An 11-day sequence is possible; alternatively, post Monday, Wednesday, Friday for an approximately month-long launch. Choose a pace suited to audience activity and call target; weekends can be skipped if the audience responds poorly then.
7. Keep existing channels and posting habits that work, and align additional content with the offer. For email, use the post title as subject and the body as copy; adapt posts into videos, reels, or stories.
8. Use outreach to turn engagement and warm-network conversations into calls, following the supplied template.
9. For offer research, poll with three options plus “other,” start conversations, and optionally conduct research calls. Say the first call has no sale; assess fit, then book a longer call to present a custom roadmap.
10. For an untested one-to-one offer, give clients close support. A weekly call and WhatsApp are examples; a Skool group and Skool payment processing are not prerequisites.
11. State the main offer price and pause. Start with paid-in-full. If the prospect says they cannot pay, establish that finances are the remaining barrier before offering installments.
12. Skool one-time payment workflow described: before sending the link, go to Settings, Pricing, select “one time,” enter the amount, save, return to the About page, and send its link. After payment, restore the regular subscription price. For limited installments, Skool does not stop billing automatically, so track the end date and change pricing manually. If an extra charge occurs, refund it. To let existing members switch plans, use tiers and the About-page “/plans” page.
13. Take action for several days, note blockers, and bring questions to the Thursday implementation call for next steps.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not skip Backbone approval or publish posts for an offer likely to change.
- Do not split attention across unrelated audience growth, new platforms, and the launch. Build scalable systems and audience after the launch.
- Do not treat low early engagement as proof of failure; continue the sequence and outreach.
- Do not exceed one launch post daily as a default, and do not abandon existing content that works.
- Do not position the offer as criticism. Present a new opportunity and its mechanism.
- Do not create a free community, Skool classroom, or complex payment structure without a need.
- Do not introduce several prices, continuity, and payment options together. Present the main offer first; offer installments only after confirming a financial barrier.
- Do not assume Skool automatically stops a finite payment plan. Manually track installments and update pricing.
