# Attract Call Recording
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 2,839 transcript lines
## One-paragraph summary
This call addresses practical bottlenecks in attraction and early sales: making offers, finding and contacting prospects, following up, and staying focused on the current constraint. The coach describes a simple path from identifying a prospect through outreach, DM qualification, booking, and pitching. Leads should be prioritized by intent and warmth, and follow-up should use relevant content and proof rather than generic repeated nudges. The call also explains how to recognize a business moat, improve profile and video communication, and increase client value through price, continuity, upsells, and retention. Its broader operating advice is to celebrate action inputs, support peers, and use training when needed rather than consume everything “just in case.”
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Rising tide:** Members learn from each other's wins and setbacks. Share input wins as well as revenue wins, and ask for support when overwhelmed.
- **Ask for the sale:** Posting and networking do not create sales unless an offer is presented and payment is requested. Count weekly pitches; zero is a clear bottleneck.
- **Attraction-to-sale sequence:** Find a person, send outreach, build trust and qualify in DMs, send a booking link, take the call, present the offer, and ask for payment. Focus on the earliest missing step.
- **Intent-based versus non-intent leads:** Start with people who ask for help, describe the problem, or respond to a CTA. Then work toward less explicit signals, warm contacts, and cold prospects who fit the ideal customer profile.
- **Moat and three S's:** A moat is a durable difference competitors cannot easily copy. Identify a specific person, a specific problem, and a specific way of solving it. Delivery and client results often reveal the differentiator.
- **BamFam and follow-up:** “BamFam” means booking a follow-up meeting from the current meeting. When a prospect needs to consider or consult someone, set a specific next time, ideally same day and within three days.
- **Client value:** Continuity provides more of the same over a longer period, often at the same or lower price per time unit. An upsell adds a new, higher-level offer. Retention depends on good delivery.
- **Just-in-time learning:** Study training when the relevant task is current; listen during low-attention activities for familiarity and take notes when mastering a subject.

## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Track input wins such as first video, first call, first pitch, and increased DM volume. At week's end, count offers presented. If none, focus on calls and asking for payment.
2. Work backward from the missing step: pitch on calls; send booking links to active conversations; qualify and build trust in DMs; send outreach if conversations are absent; identify likely prospects if there is no one to contact.
3. When there are no sales, put more time into DMs instead of spreading effort across many platforms. Build trust and qualify. Find prospects where need is likely, such as newly certified personal trainers for an online-coaching offer.
4. Prioritize explicit intent first, then new group members and other warm signals, then ideal-customer-profile prospects. Do not contact people who do not fit simply because they are easy to find.
5. Use a timer and music to start a focused DM block. The speaker used a 90-minute timer. Prepare water or coffee, and do not watch training or multitask while writing messages.
6. If there are few leads, personalize each interaction and keep track of them with a CRM, Scoot tag, or another reliable system. A free Skool group can nurture with a weekly value video, call, engagement post, proof post, and DMs.
7. Raise price only after some sales validate the offer and delivery supports the value. Change one major variable at a time. The coach advises keeping price stable while onboarding a closer. Compare revenue, not just conversion rate: 10 leads at 50% and $1,000 yields $5,000; 10 leads at 30% and $2,000 yields $6,000.
8. For continuation, discuss progress, remaining obstacles, and the longer-term goal during a regular delivery call. Ask whether the client wants to explore working together longer; if yes, present the offer and investment. One continuity example was $3,000 for three months versus $5,000 for six months.
9. Offer continuity around a major milestone or two to three weeks before the program ends. Avoid a complex new upsell until there are enough clients and capacity. Use the call's distinction: continuity extends existing work; upsell adds a new, higher-level offer.
10. Follow-up example after a sales call: daily for three days, weekly for four weeks, then monthly for about six months. Use relevant proof or content. A scheduled BamFam takes priority over generic follow-up.
11. Send a case study that resembles the prospect's situation and ask what questions they have. Similarity in background, problem, and previous attempts makes the proof easier to identify with.
12. For videos, speak from bullet points rather than reading every word if a script sounds flat. Use live delivery, passion, preparation, and practice to build energy.
13. Build a moat by noticing what clients tried before, what produced results, and what is hard for competitors to reproduce. Describe the person, problem, and method specifically.

## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Coach, opening a qualification call:** “I'd love to ask a few questions, see where you're at, and if I think I can help, schedule a more in-depth call where I can break down a roadmap. On that call, you can decide if you want to work with me or do it on your own.” Use for a short first call before a full pitch.
- **Coach, qualifying:** “What's your goal? What's your problem? Do you have funds to potentially invest? Are you a decision maker, or does someone need to decide with you? When do you want to get started?”
- **Coach, booking a BamFam after spouse consultation:** “Is she around right now? ... What time will you talk to her? ... I have a spot open at [time]. Let's hop on then so I can answer any questions.” Use to book around the decision partner's availability.
- **Coach, old-lead follow-up:** “I know last time we talked, it was [problem]. I just had a few quick ideas about how we could help. Is [goal] still a priority for you, or did you already achieve it?”
- **Coach, case-study follow-up:** “I just interviewed [client]. She had your same problem. We talked about how she overcame it. Thought it might be helpful. Let me know what questions you have about this video.”
- **Coach, agreed buyer delaying payment:** “Why don't you go grab your wallet right now? I'm here. That way we can get you on board and show you the next steps.” Use only after the prospect has agreed to buy and needs to complete payment.
- **Coach, offer close:** “Here's how I can help you. It costs [price]. You ready to rock and roll?”

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not confuse activity with sales. Present an offer and ask for payment.
- Do not fix ten problems at once; work on the earliest missing step in the sequence.
- Do not wait for a perfect lead. Prioritize intent and fit.
- Do not raise prices automatically or change several variables together. Validate value, move in stages, and keep pricing steady while onboarding a closer.
- Do not build a complex upsell before you have enough customers and delivery capacity. Continuity is often simpler.
- Do not re-pitch the entire offer on every follow-up call. Resume from the prior conversation.
- Do not leave a needed BamFam unscheduled. Aim for same day or within three days.
- Do not read a script word for word if it makes video delivery flat. Prepare talking points and practice.
- Do not watch unrelated training just in case. Study the current bottleneck and protect focused DM time.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use this call when a creator is doing activity but has few sales, is unsure whom to contact, has a backlog of leads or “no/maybe” calls, needs a follow-up plan, or is preparing a continuation offer for clients. Ask how many offers they pitched this week, where prospects currently drop out of the sequence, which people have shown buying intent, who fits the ideal customer profile, what proof and client results they have, what the current offer and price are, whether a prospect needs another decision maker, and when the client is approaching a milestone or program end. Ask whether the user wants a one-call or two-call process before applying the qualification script.
