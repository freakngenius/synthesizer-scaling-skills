# Client Attraction Call (VSL Edition)
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 2014 lines, transcript with warm VSL template, example and sales-call analysis prompt
## One-paragraph summary
This call explains when to use a video sales letter (VSL) as a conversion asset in a Skool funnel. A short warm VSL aims to book better-informed calls and filter applicants; it does not generate traffic. Build one after at least 10 sales, when you have proof and call conversion is a bottleneck. The example sequence is audience/outcome hook, client proof, reason the method works, values, qualification, expectations, value stack and CTA. A second, deeper video can go to booked prospects before their call. Use sales-call transcripts to source audience language and objections. Related Q&A covers selling to “I can do it myself,” upsells, busy-client delivery and getting clients to an early win.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Warm VSL:** It helps warm prospects book calls and improves the quality and conversion of those calls. It filters for fit and educates the undecided middle. It amplifies existing traffic; it does not create traffic.
- **Readiness:** Make a VSL after 10 or more sales, with at least one piece of personal or client proof, when call conversion is the constraint. Sales experience gives you knowledge of prospect pains, testimonials, offer and pitch. If the issue is too few eyeballs or you have not made initial sales, work on that first.
- **Warm VSL sequence:** 1) Hook naming niche, goal and platform. 2) Specific proof from different customer situations. 3) Credibility statement explaining why results happen. 4) Unique method/pillars. 5) Values or philosophy. 6) Qualification and first CTA. 7) Price/selectivity expectations. 8) Hypothetical value stack for the call. 9) Final CTA and proof montage. The example gets to proof immediately after the hook.
- **Proof across avatars:** Show results relevant to distinct situations, such as a hobby niche, someone working a 9-to-5, and an established business owner who wants to scale. Proof can answer beliefs such as “high ticket only works in make-money niches.” Personal transformation can substitute for client proof when it relates directly to the offer.
- **VSL sandwich:** The pre-booking video is short and drives a booking. After the booking, a second, more detailed video can deepen trust, explain the offer and pre-handle objections during the wait before the call. Add this only after the front end works and call conversion becomes a constraint.
- **Three common constraints:** Lack of clarity, insufficient leads/eyeballs, or limited time and need for scalable systems. These were patterns the coach found in Synthesizer prospects, not a universal diagnosis.
- **“I can do it myself” discovery:** A buyer needs a reason support helps: faster results, a proven roadmap/mentorship, or knowledge they lack. Ask what has prevented them from doing it alone. If discovery has not established such a gap, do not pitch.
- **Busy-client retention:** Reduce operational complexity. Send one easy question or task at a time, reduce deliverables and call time, help with scheduling, remind clients of their desired outcome, and get an early win. Product usage and perceived progress were named as churn factors.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Review recorded calls before scripting. Extract repeated beliefs, goals, pains, objections and the language prospects use. The coach analyzed 10 to 20 transcripts with ChatGPT, comparing successful and failed answers. Adapt the provided prompt to focus on objections if needed.
2. Confirm the VSL fits the current bottleneck: 10+ sales, a proof point, existing traffic and calls that need better conversion. Otherwise pursue initial sales or traffic.
3. Write a direct hook, then move into proof. Use specific before/result/time/context and a few different prospect types. The example removed 35 seconds of introductory talk before proof.
4. Explain why the results happen, then name the method and actual values. Synthesizer named attracting clients, converting them and delivering results at scale; its values line was “beautiful money,” where customer value exceeds price.
5. Qualify who should apply, set expectations and sell the call rather than making the VSL a full sales presentation. Use a hypothetical value stack based on needs heard in calls, then give a clear booking CTA.
6. Film without overbuilding. The example script took about an hour; the presenter read it about three times on a phone, filmed with an iPhone and borrowed mic, made one pickup, and published the same day. No teleprompter was used.
7. Place the video where warm prospects already go: Skool About page, booking page, pinned post, classroom or email broadcast. Direct viewers clearly to apply/book because Skool may foreground “Join group.” Keep an existing website rather than making a new one without a reason. For a premium funnel, the coach suggested placing the VSL inside the free group, not the paid group's About page.
8. For cold traffic, use more trust-building and generally a longer video. The coach suggested backstory after proof, with the story's “epiphany bridge” leading to the unique method. Match wording, colors and editing cues across the VSL and booking page, and across both videos, so the steps feel connected.
9. To handle a recurring price objection, compare successful and failed call transcripts, keep what worked, remove what did not, and use proof/process to explain value or ROI. Put the answer in a pre- or post-booking video.
10. For an upsell, review the client's current situation, progress, new goals and obstacles, then present the next offer as a solution. Initiate around two weeks before the program ends or at a milestone. Keep the tone familiar but follow a sales process; pre-handle predictable objections before presenting the offer.
11. For a busy client, reduce the work required to progress: one request at a time, yes/no questions when possible, shorter calls, fewer deliverables, and scheduling help. Deliver a first small win quickly.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **VSL speaker, warm hook:** “If you're an educational creator who wants to make $10,000 to $100,000 a month on Skool.” Model for stating audience, outcome and platform; replace with supported claims for the user's offer.
- **VSL speaker, credibility after proof:** “The reason we get these results isn't because I'm some super genius ... but because we do things very differently from the rest of this space.”
- **VSL speaker, qualification:** “If you are an educational creator and have achieved a valuable outcome ... click the link below this video and apply.” Use to define who should book.
- **VSL speaker, hypothetical value stack:** “If all it did was give you that proven roadmap to follow so it could reduce some of your overwhelm...” Continue only with benefits grounded in customer conversations.
- **Sales coach, discovery:** “What's holding you back from doing this on your own?” Ask after establishing the prospect's goal and gap.
- **Sales coach, lower-barrier question:** “What do you like about your nine to five?” Then ask, “What do you not like about that? What do you want to change?” Start with what is working before exploring dissatisfaction.
- **Upsell seller, discovery:** “Where are you at now? ... What are your goals now that you've achieved this? ... What are you running into now?” Use before presenting the next offer.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not build a VSL before learning the audience and making sales; the coach calls it a distraction for someone without initial sales.
- Do not expect a VSL to bring new traffic or solve a traffic shortage.
- Do not add one when calls convert and there is no conversion bottleneck. Do not solve problems that do not exist yet.
- Do not assume the short warm format fits cold traffic. Cold audiences generally need more trust-building.
- Do not overproduce the video; the example used basic equipment and a fast filming process.
- Do not use unsupported proof or claims. Public pricing is not automatically right: Synthesizer showed its price because of Skool tier display behavior, while the coach said most users would need stronger trust, proof and traffic to publish price.
- Do not change a working funnel or create a website just to host a VSL. With busy clients, do not send multiple complex requests at once.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use with an educator, coach or consultant who has made 10+ sales, can show proof, has warm traffic and wants more qualified or better-converting calls. Ask how many sales they have made, what verifiable proof they can show, where leads come from, whether calls are being booked, what happens to conversion, what objections recur in recorded calls, whether traffic is warm or cold, and where viewers should book. If initial sales or traffic are the problem, focus there before adding a VSL.
