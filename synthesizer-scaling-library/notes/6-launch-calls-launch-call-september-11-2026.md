Type: call   Stage: Launch Attract   Length/size: 2,563 transcript lines
## One-paragraph summary
This call starts the Launch Attract phase for members who have completed their Business Backbone and Launch Prep. The first week's single priority is booking sales calls with the 11-post launch sequence, publishing across channels where the audience already gathers, then contacting people who engage. The launch promotes a new opportunity based on the member's premium offer, not a live event or a fully built course. Start with warm prospects and a specific call target, and open near-term calendar slots so interest does not go cold. If the offer or outcome is still unclear, continue research calls before launching. Sales, cohort timing and delivery come later, though the Q&A covers pre-selling and building delivery with customers.

## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)

- **Rapid Launch Protocol:** Complete the Business Backbone, finish Launch Prep (including the opportunity audit and prep posts), then move to Launch Attract and book calls. Launch Convert and Launch Deliver follow. If the offer is still unclear, continue research calls and return for a Backbone review. Work on the step at hand rather than solving later-stage questions.
- **“Cook balls” means “book calls”:** The chef story is a memory device for the launch's central action. In the first attract week, focus on booked calls rather than unrelated metrics or extra polish.
- **11-post launch sequence:** Use the provided posts to announce an offer or mechanism and build buyer belief in the outcome. When people react, start a conversation and invite suitable prospects to a call. Posts can be adapted to the member's channels and formats.
- **Warm-to-cold outreach:** Begin with past customers, former clients and people who have expressed the need, then contact colder prospects. Ask non-buyers for referrals after prioritizing people with buying intent. The coaches advise against starting paid ads.
- **Research-first offer validation:** If the offer is not defined well enough to explain its outcome, keep doing research calls. The coaches suggest 30 as a useful target for learning what people want and need. Research may be unnecessary for someone who has already sold the offer, knows sales, or has clear product-market resonance.
- **Two-call close:** Use a 15-minute research call to understand the goal and problem. If you can help, book a separate 45-minute sales call, preferably tomorrow or the next day, to present a tailored solution.
- **Sell the call, then the offer:** Outreach should lead to a call, where the offer is explained and sold. Personalized Loom videos can help with a small list; for thousands of prospects, individual videos may not scale. Balance personalization with volume.
- **Build as you go:** Sell the transformation, set a clear start date, and develop delivery with the cohort as you learn participants' needs. The coaches said this helps solve their problems in real time.
- **New opportunity framing:** Present the offer as a new path to the result. Explain why past attempts may not have worked without blaming the buyer. Sell the transformation and benefits of delivery, not a community or a list of features.

## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)

1. Finish the Backbone and required Launch Prep first. If research shows the outcome is still unclear, continue interviews and seek another Backbone review before writing launch posts.
2. Choose one specific call-booking target. The call's guideposts are 10 calls for beginners, 30 for intermediate launches, and 100 for advanced launches. Do not set a vague range; choose a number.
3. Spend the first seven days booking calls. New sellers can open sales-call slots the following week; experienced sellers can begin sooner. Keep booking while taking calls. Some members may sell longer before delivery, but that is not the default schedule.
4. Open near-term sales-call availability. Example: post from the 13th through the 17th, then offer the 18th, 19th and 20th; add the 21st and 22nd as earlier slots fill. A far-future booking may lose interest because the prospect forgets the offer or conversation.
5. Start with the Launch Prep list, described as roughly 100 to 300 possible prospects, and existing followers on LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram or other active channels. Contact people who engage promptly, continue the conversation, and invite them to a call.
6. Publish the 11 launch posts on platforms where the audience already is. Adapt them for Instagram Reels/Stories, YouTube, TikTok, email and Skool. Keep other content on-topic with the launch and mechanism. Post no more than once a day; daily posting completes the sequence in 11 days. Alternatively, post every other day or about three times weekly. Weekend posts are optional and should follow the creator's usual schedule.
7. Use the supplied posts and built-in calls to action. Small edits are fine; members have copy-pasted the sequence. If a previous launch is already generating calls and sales, use fewer posts to meet the remaining goal. The call's three-to-four-hours-per-post estimate is a joke about overcomplicating copy, not an instruction.
8. For a cohort, set a start date and number of places before selling. You can sell before the full program is built if you explain the date. A weekly preparation call can help. In Skool, Settings > Pricing offers one-time or subscription payments; send the payment link on the call, then restore prior pricing if needed.
9. If capacity is limited, focus on filling the next cohort before planning another. State actual capacity and remaining places.

## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)

- **Launch post opener, coaches' template:** “Working on an exciting project that will help [audience] [achieve outcome].” Fill in the audience and outcome from the member's Backbone.
- **Welcome DM, coach example:** “Hey, welcome to Synthesizer. Are you new to Skool or are you looking to grow?” Use as an automatic welcome; adapt the either/or question to the audience, such as “Do you already speak Spanish or do you want to get started?”
- **Referral ask, coach example:** “Hey, I'm thinking about helping [niche] with [outcome]. Do you happen to know anybody who would benefit from this?” Use with a contact who may know prospects but is not one.
- **Past-client first-look, coach guidance:** “Hey, I want you to hear from me about this first. I'm going to launch it to my [audience] a little later, but I wanted you to hear it from me first and I want you to get the first [spot] if you're interested.” Use with a past customer who already trusts you.
- **Research-call questions, coaches:** “What's your goal? Where are you trying to go? What's your biggest problem? Where are you struggling with right now?” Use on the 15-minute research call.
- **Research-to-sales transition, coach example:** “I think I can help you with this, but I would like to get you scheduled in for another longer call when I can actually break it down for you and what that would look like. Are you available tomorrow or the next day?” Use after confirming a problem you can solve.
- **Cohort expectation-setting, coach guidance:** Tell the prospect the start date and what happens before it, such as a weekly call to discuss pain points and prepare for the program.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules

- If the offer outcome is unclear, research and get feedback before launching. During the first attract week, focus on calls booked and offer near-term dates.
- Use the supplied posts rather than writing an elaborate sequence from scratch. Keep other content relevant to the launch, and do not pitch the full program in the first outreach message.
- Learn your own close rate, process and common objections before hiring a closer. Group selling can work when objections are familiar, but prospects may stay quiet about individual concerns.
- Sell the transformation and benefit of delivery, not community membership or calls as the result.
- State actual capacity and remaining places. Do not invent scarcity.
- Build delivery with clients while clearly stating start dates and what happens before the start. Members can delay if travel, health or family circumstances make launching impractical.

- **Posting cadence:** No more than one launch post daily; posting once a day completes the sequence in 11 days. Other stated cadences are every other day or about three per week.
- **Member-specific pricing examples:** Brandon and Colleen discussed $3,000 for three months and a $1,000-per-month downsell/payment plan. A separate real-estate offer discussion considered $3,000 for higher support and $1,500 or potentially $1,000 for lower support; that offer was still being clarified. These are not general price recommendations.
- **Capacity:** An in-person event had eight to ten places. The coaches suggested setting a challenging target, such as 30 when 20 feels easy, or 60 when 50 feels easy.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)

Use this guidance for a member who has a defined premium offer and is ready to attract prospects but needs a call-booking plan, posting cadence, outreach order or calendar strategy. First ask whether the offer and outcome are clear, whether it has sold before, how many relevant warm contacts and followers the member can reach, their call target and availability, and whether they have launched before. If the outcome is unclear, do research calls and Backbone review first. For a fixed cohort date or limited capacity, establish the actual start date and number of places before writing urgency into the launch.
