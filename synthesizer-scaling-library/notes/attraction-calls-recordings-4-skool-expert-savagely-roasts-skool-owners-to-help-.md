# Skool Expert Savagely Roasts Skool Owners To Help Them Scale
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 1,951 transcript lines, including attached continuity-offer document; runtime not stated
## One-paragraph summary
This attraction call covers Skool Discovery and Growth Boost changes, the priority of traffic over minor conversion optimization, and how to simplify a business when delivery exhausts its operator. The coach recommends treating platform changes as experiments, testing low-ticket pricing only when a free group attracts almost no members, and judging acquisition by relevant visits and members rather than a blended conversion figure. The call also gives a five-step simplification algorithm, guidance on free and high-ticket models, content positioning and sales conversations. An attached document lays out a continuity-offer process, pricing examples, bonuses and timing.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Traffic maxing:** Increase relevant visits and booked calls before fine-tuning conversion. A study of Skool groups reporting $100,000/month found that high revenue was not explained by unusually high about-page conversion; the higher-revenue groups had more visitors. Work on the current constraint.
- **Platform changes as experiments:** Do not react to a new Discovery or Growth Boost feature on a small sample. Keep what works. If a free group gets almost no members, test a low monthly price with a free trial and measure whether Skool sends more members. Revert or change the experiment if results do not improve.
- **Traffic-source diagnosis:** Separate volume, quality and source. Direct visits can include crawlers and scrapers, distorting a blended conversion rate. Check the sources the business values, such as affiliates, YouTube or Skool network.
- **Five-step simplification algorithm:** (1) Question every requirement. (2) Delete unnecessary steps. (3) Simplify what remains. (4) Accelerate cycle time. (5) Automate only after the earlier steps. The coach warns against automating a process that should have been removed.
- **Free reach and high-ticket depth:** Free content and a free community can help many people to a lesser degree; high-ticket services can help fewer people deeply. A small team attempting low-ticket, high-ticket and intensive content at once may overload itself. Treat a low-ticket group as a funnel to high-ticket only if it justifies its support cost and complexity.
- **Continuity versus upsell (attached document):** Continuity offers more or longer of the same thing at a lower price-to-time ratio, such as an annual upgrade. An upsell adds new, higher-priced benefits, often access, advanced training or events. The attachment says to defer upsells until 50+ clients and multiple success stories.
- **Sales gap:** Clarify both the prospect's current pain and the desired outcome. Ask how the problem affects relevant life areas, then explore what changes after they reach the desired result. Position a call around the prospect's goal and bottleneck.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. If a free group gets fewer than 10 new members/month, test $1, $2, $7 or $9/month with a free trial. The coach most often suggests $7. Measure whether member volume from Skool improves, not whether the conversion percentage stays constant. If a group already attracts members, keep it free unless there is a reason to change.
2. When a previously successful cover stops working, test a new cover first, then restore the old cover if signups do not return. In a case with 39 signups in the previous 30 days, the coach advised keeping the group free. Another operator with eight signups in 30 days was told a low-ticket test was reasonable.
3. Compare visits by source. Do not use direct visits as the only measure if automated crawlers may inflate them. The coach says to focus on the sources relevant to acquisition and compare those visitors with joins.
4. Identify the main Discovery keyword and include it in the group title when accurate. Search the keyword directly to check rank. The rank shown in group settings was said not to reflect the default Discovery order, which was sorted by trending.
5. Make cover images, group titles and video thumbnails clear to an unfamiliar viewer. Name both the audience and the outcome. Avoid broad labels such as “creator” if the intended niche is authors, AP Calculus students or another distinct group.
6. When delivery feels exhausting, list the activities causing it, rate what is most draining, and ask what is easiest to stop or delegate. Remove optional work first. In one case, the team planned to stop adding 1:1 support to a cohort, considered a sales rep for roughly 100 to 110 calls/month, and questioned whether its low-ticket group was necessary before hiring coaches.
7. Apply the simplification algorithm in order. If an offer or process cannot be removed, reduce the work it requires. Then consider cycle time, staffing and automation. Do not automate first.
8. For a returning-client offer, book a normal check-in or strategy call. Review the client's starting point and wins; ask where they are stuck; explore the next goal; clarify where they want to be after the current program; ask permission to explain an upgrade; then pitch solutions for the next goal. Explain price, deliverables and bonuses after establishing relevance.
9. Attached continuity examples use 2x price for 2x time as a standard anchor and 1.5x price for 2x time as a favorable offer. Add bonuses clients might otherwise buy separately, such as extra access, training, events or meetups. The attachment gives 24 hours as a possible availability window, but only use a deadline that is true.
10. For sales discovery, ask how the problem affects confidence, relationships, work, health or family, then ask how the desired outcome would affect those areas. For sensitive questions, explain there is no judgment and the person can decline.
11. Test replacing 1:1 cohort coaching with group calls and track progress; contact participants who stall.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not overhaul a working free group based on a small amount of data about Discovery or Growth Boost.
- Do not judge conversion using an unexamined blend of sources; direct traffic may include scrapers.
- Do not add paid community support if it creates substantial work without a clear role in high-ticket sales or profit.
- Do not automate or add staff to support something you could delete.
- Do not burden opening DMs with several questions or tasks. The call recommends a simple yes/no or this/that opener, then establishing goal and bottleneck in conversation.
- Do not pitch continuity upfront. The attachment places the pitch after a core milestone or 2 to 3 weeks before program end, and recommends pitching the next outcome rather than a list of deliverables.
- Do not assume delivery capacity is fixed. The coach recommends finding a scalable way to deliver, while acknowledging that poor delivery can damage reputation.
