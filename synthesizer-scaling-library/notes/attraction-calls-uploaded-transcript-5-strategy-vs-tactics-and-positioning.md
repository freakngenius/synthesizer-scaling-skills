# Positioning: become known for a specific outcome
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: 1h33m

## One-paragraph summary
This client-attraction call distinguishes strategy from tactics. Decide who you help, what outcome they want, and how you help before optimizing an About page, outreach message, or video. Narrow the work until you can credibly become known for a specific niche, outcome, and mechanism, using proof, market demand, and work you can sustain. The Q&A applies this to audience choice, buyer demand, and testing offers before building content.

## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Strategy before tactics:** Positioning determines who responds to content and outreach. Strong titles cannot create demand for a problem the audience does not have. Refine Backbone as the business learns.
- **"Become the best in the world at what you do, and keep redefining what you do until this is true":** The call attributes this to Naval Ravikant. It is not a demand to beat a famous expert in a broad field. Keep narrowing the work until it fits your specific knowledge, proof, capabilities, interests, and market. Template: "I will become known for being the best in the world at helping [niche] achieve [outcome] through [mechanism]."
- **Proof-led positioning:** List valuable outcomes you achieved and helped others achieve. Use evidence to find the area where you are strongest and can build recognition. The presenter says prospects use results as a proxy for expertise. Do not rely only on a claim that you are the best.
- **Readiness line:** Within an audience, advanced prospects may pay more but are fewer; beginners are more numerous. Choose the level below which you will not take clients. The presenters say they cannot help a Skool owner who has not yet achieved a viable outcome, because teaching that process may take years. Setting the line too high can make the addressable audience too small.
- **Founder-product-market fit and motivation:** Seek overlap between market demand, proof you can deliver, and work you can sustain. The presenter says Time Theory reached $100,000/month but helping people beat procrastination did not fit his interests; educational creators and Skool did. Positioning can emerge and change through experience.
- **Sell what people want, deliver what they need:** Lead with the transformation buyers seek. The mechanism may include material they would not buy by itself. The presenter compares this to wrapping garlic in ham so the customer accepts the needed ingredient.
- **Talent stack:** Combine skills or interests to occupy a narrower overlap instead of trying to dominate a broad field. The example is being strong at chess and Russian openings, or chess and content creation.
- **GPS framework:** Name the **Goal**, identify the **Problem** blocking it, and choose the **Solution**. This moves a vague discussion toward a concrete next step.
- **Offer-first market learning:** When several niches seem plausible, make customized offers to your network. Assess who buys, who you like helping, who gets results, and who wants more. Use this evidence to narrow the niche; the presenter recommends at least three offers daily.
- **Prioritize controllable inputs:** Address one bottleneck at a time. Consistent outreach and content do not guarantee consistent daily outputs; focus on actions you control.

## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Write who you help, what outcome they want, and how you help. Start with the audience and outcome before choosing the mechanism.
2. Inventory your proof: personal outcomes, customer results, clients helped, and relevant work. Find which audience/outcome combination your evidence best supports.
3. Narrow the position step by step. Move from a broad field to a specific audience, outcome, and mechanism. Stop at a claim you can support and want to build recognition around.
4. Compare audience levels. Decide how far along a prospect needs to be for your process to work, whether that segment can pay, and whether the segment is large enough. Do not target only beginners because they are numerous or only experts because they may pay more.
5. Check whether the promise matches buyer demand. If people want a concrete result but you prefer teaching mindset, sell the result and include mindset in the method when it helps deliver it.
6. If you have multiple plausible niches, look for an overlap between skills or interests. Top-10% ability in two areas may combine into a distinctive specialty.
7. If uncertain what to sell, list people in your network you can help and make customized offers. Track at least three per day by weekday. After customers respond, compare who bought, who got the most benefit, and who you want to keep serving.
8. Use your current network before broad content. The presenter says direct offers teach more early on; a custom Loom to relevant prospects may be more useful than a generic video to an unknown audience.
9. Once the audience and outcome are clear, follow the post-launch routine: weekly YouTube content, daily Skool networking, run a free group, and outreach. Use the Attract classroom lessons for channel and community instructions.
10. If content is already attracting the right people, keep doing more and improve it. Do not invent a new problem just because another topic or channel sounds interesting.
11. For a hard decision, state the goal, problem, and solution. If bills are due, take short-term action instead of waiting for perfect positioning.

## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Presenter, positioning worksheet:** "I will become known for being the best in the world at helping a niche achieve an outcome through a mechanism." Fill in a specific audience, outcome, and method; the presenter suggests making it a desktop or phone background.
- **Presenter, proof inventory:** "What are the valuable outcomes that you've achieved for yourself and other people?" Write them down and look for the audience/outcome pair your record supports.
- **Presenter, niche discovery:** "Who can I help and what can I help them do?" Ask this before selecting the mechanism.
- **Presenter, GPS decision:** "What's the goal? What's the thing holding me back? What do I need to do to solve the problem to reach the goal?" Use when discussion becomes theoretical.
- **Presenter, buyer-facing principle:** "Sell people what they want, then give them what they need." Use when a creator's preferred topic differs from the outcome buyers ask for.
- **Presenter, action target:** Track offers Monday through Sunday and aim for at least three offers each day when testing a direction.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not optimize tactics for weak positioning. Copy and production cannot create interest in an outcome the audience does not want.
- Do not describe yourself only by a broad skill. Narrow by audience, outcome, and mechanism until the claim fits your evidence.
- Do not choose a niche only because it is large or personally interesting. Check demand, ability to help, proof, and willingness to pay.
- Do not set the readiness line so high that it removes too much of the audience, or accept people so early that your process cannot help them.
- Do not let long-term positioning questions block near-term offers when you need income. Refine through experience.
- Do not add YouTube to avoid conversations. Do not switch away from content that is already working for the right audience.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use when positioning is broad, content draws the wrong audience, the audience asks for a topic the creator does not want to teach, or the member is stuck between niches. Ask what outcome they want, who they have helped, what results they can prove, what potential customers request or pay for, which audience level they can serve, and what work they can sustain. If they have a network but no clear niche, ask who they can make an offer to this week. Keep the financial Q&A separate from investment coaching.
