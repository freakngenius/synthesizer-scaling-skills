# CHRISTMAS EDITION Client Attraction Call
Type: call   Stage: Attract   Length/size: not stated in transcript
## One-paragraph summary
The call reviews Synthesizer's 2025 growth, sets a 2026 direction, and teaches a practical roadmap for choosing what to work on. The presenters connect growth to focused execution: keep one main project in progress, record other ideas for later, set a monthly financial goal, and use price, close rate, and show-up rate to calculate the activity required. In Q&A, they apply the same discipline to attracting leads, handling follow-up, simplifying delivery, improving a community, and deciding what work deserves attention. They advise measuring customer value directly, prioritizing warm prospects, and using the existing model before chasing new formats or channels.
## Core ideas and frameworks (named concepts, each with its steps or components, as taught)
- **Roadmap: Work in progress, Backlog, Probably never, Shipped, Goals.** Keep no more than three active projects, and usually only one. Record future ideas in Backlog instead of switching to them. Delete or park ideas in Probably never, and move completed work to Shipped. Use Goals to set a financial target and choose the next project. The roadmap is for projects, not routine tasks such as replying to comments.
- **One constraint at a time.** Identify the current problem, choose the most important action to solve it, and stay with that work until it is done. Then reassess the backlog. Solving the current constraint can make lower-priority projects unnecessary. The presenters call this “go at it.”
- **Goal-to-activity calculation.** Enter monthly income goal, offer price, close rate, and show-up rate. Calculate customers, calls taken, and calls booked per month and week. Compare the target with actual data to locate the bottleneck.
- **10x improvement.** If the gap is too few calls, prioritize actions that create qualified attention and calls instead of small conversion edits. Let the next constraint emerge after the current one improves.
- **Warm-to-cold outreach.** Contact current customers, past calls and non-buyers, engaged community members, new members, lurkers, then inactive or cold prospects. Prioritize explicit intent, such as asking for help with a problem, over generic likes.
- **Promote an existing asset.** Give away a training, template, recording, or call already made; ask people to comment; contact responders and invite suitable prospects to a call. Do not create another asset just to run a promotion.
- **Customer value survey.** Ask customers what they would keep and what they would remove. Use their answers to invest in valued parts and reduce unused parts. Poll your own customers instead of copying another company's results.
## Tactics and how-tos (concrete, ordered, include any numbers, thresholds, timings, tools, settings)
1. Copy the roadmap sheet. Set a monthly financial goal within the next year and enter the offer price. Add the close and show-up rates from recent calls; if unknown, estimate now and replace with actual figures later.
2. Check whether the required customer and call volume seems achievable. Test changes to price or close rate in the sheet rather than treating the first configuration as fixed.
3. At month end, enter actual income, price, customers, rates, and booked calls beside the target. If call volume is far below goal, prioritize Attract; if calls happen but few buy, examine sales and qualification.
4. Make roadmap items clear enough to understand as a project, such as “launch” or “drop a VSL.” Keep the smaller execution tasks on a separate to-do list.
5. To engage a free community, ask new members to introduce themselves, and have moderators or members welcome them. Consider an item unlocked at level two or three. Collect email and phone details when appropriate so inactive members can be reached elsewhere.
6. For a giveaway, use an existing resource and ask for a comment. A 24-hour Skool calendar event can point to the post. To send an additional email reminder, set another event just over 24 hours ahead and enable the one-day reminder; the example used about 24 hours plus 50 minutes. Avoid spamming members.
7. Contact past call leads before prospecting inactive members. Refer to their known problem and desired outcome, offer a useful idea, and ask if the goal is still a priority. For a no-show, message during the call, wait 15 minutes, then leave with a simple option to rebook. Confirm the day before, especially for calls booked a week out.
8. Once there are more than 10 customers, consider asking what they value and what they would remove. Treat the poll as input, not a complete decision rule.
9. Review content topics against calendar and booked calls. Reuse strong topics and hooks in a swipe file. The presenters say UTM tags are often more work than value at this stage; use lightweight correlation unless detailed attribution would change a decision.
10. When raising price, contact past leads and tell them about the upcoming change. Compare cash per call after the change because a higher price can reduce close rate.
11. Reduce calls, recordings, notifications, or lessons members do not use. Improve or remove low-use material before adding more content.
## Scripts, templates and wording (verbatim short examples of messages, posts, hooks, questions; mark each with who says it and when to use it)
- **Creator, giveaway post:** “Comment [keyword] and I'll unlock [existing resource] for you.” Use to offer a training, template, or recording and identify people to contact.
- **Creator, follow-up to a past lead:** “Hey, [name], just had a new idea for how I could help you [solve problem/reach goal]. Is [goal] still a priority for you?” Use after a no-show, past call, or interested DM; invite them to a call if they confirm.
- **Coach, prospect who is not a fit:** “I think what's best for you is probably to join the free community or go through the roadmap. Let's have this conversation again in a few months when it might be a better time.” Use to end the call without extended free coaching.
- **Coach, no-show follow-up:** “I've got to run. I don't have time today to check in. Happy to rebook you at a better time if things align.” Send after the 15-minute wait, without pleading.
- **Creator, price change:** “I know this program is amazing for you and a good fit, and I know you had hesitations about [X]. Is now a good time to get on a call before the price increases?” Use with prior call leads when the increase is real and upcoming.

## Mistakes, warnings and "do not" rules
- Do not split attention across many large projects or replace the current priority with every new idea. Park ideas in Backlog until the active work is done.
- Do not set a goal without comparing actual calls, attendance, and sales. The sheet estimates workload; it does not guarantee results.
- Do not polish small conversion details when the actual constraint is too few calls. Do not mistake viral reach for qualified demand.
- Do not cold-contact inactive members before following up with customers, past calls, and engaged members. Give explicit intent higher priority than generic engagement.
- Do not build a new giveaway asset when an existing one can create the same opportunity. Avoid funnels and product changes that add more process than the team can support.
- Do not assume customers value the same components as another program's customers. Avoid adding content by default when members already feel behind.
- Do not offer free work as the automatic response to “I need proof it will work.” Clarify the concern and re-present the offer if it fits. Do not pitch someone you cannot help.
- Do not beg a no-show to rebook or wait indefinitely. Confirm beforehand, message during the meeting, and leave after 15 minutes.
- Do not build detailed tracking unless the data will change a decision. The presenters prefer reviewing content and booking patterns for most members.

## Best used when (what kind of user or situation this helps, and what to ask the user before using it)
Use when a user has too many competing projects, cannot tell whether they need more calls or better conversion, is deciding whether to increase price or build another offer, or needs to make community delivery easier to use. Before applying the roadmap, ask for the monthly income goal and timeframe, price, customers per month, calls booked and taken, show-up and close rates, lead sources, and the one constraint causing the biggest gap. Before recommending a community promotion, ask which audience is warmest and what useful asset already exists. Before removing product elements, ask how many customers can answer the poll and what they actually use.
